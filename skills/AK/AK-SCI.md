## 5. ITEM TYPES

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Scenario-based or direct concept questions              |
|  May include diagrams, data tables, or illustrations     |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Part 1                                                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, AK.Sci.5.ESS.2 — Weather Patterns):**

Students in Fairbanks recorded the number of daylight hours each month for one year. They found that June had approximately 21 hours of daylight while December had approximately 4 hours. Which statement BEST explains this large difference?

- A. Fairbanks is closer to the sun in June than in December
- B. Earth's tilted axis causes Fairbanks to receive more direct sunlight in summer
- C. Clouds block more sunlight in December than in June
- D. The sun produces more light energy during the summer months

*Correct Answer: B*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, AK.Sci.8.ESS.2 — Earth's Surface):**

A geologist studying the Denali fault zone observes that rock layers on one side of the fault have shifted several meters relative to the other side. Which geoscience process BEST explains this observation?

- A. Erosion by glacial movement
- B. Deposition from volcanic ash
- C. Tectonic plate movement along the fault
- D. Chemical weathering of exposed rock surfaces

*Correct Answer: C*

---

**MC Example (Grade 10, DOK 2, AK.Sci.10.LS.2 — Ecosystem Dynamics):**

Scientists studying Bristol Bay salmon populations recorded a 15% decline in sockeye salmon returns over five years, while the population of predatory bears remained stable. Which factor would MOST LIKELY contribute to the continued decline in the salmon population?

- A. Increased water temperature reducing dissolved oxygen in spawning streams
- B. A decrease in the number of eagles in the region
- C. An increase in the growth of riparian vegetation along streambanks
- D. Stable annual rainfall patterns in the watershed

*Correct Answer: A*

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–3 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written explanation with science evidence               |
|  May require data analysis, explanation of phenomenon,   |
|  or design of an investigation                           |
|  Human scored using CR rubric                            |
|  Part 2                                                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, AK.Sci.5.LS.2):**

*"The diagram below shows a food web in an Alaska tundra ecosystem, including caribou, Arctic foxes, lemmings, tundra grasses, and wolves. Using the food web, explain what would most likely happen to the Arctic fox population if the lemming population suddenly decreased. Use evidence from the diagram to support your answer."*

---

**CR Example (Grade 8, DOK 3, AK.Sci.8.ESS.3):**

*"Scientists have observed that permafrost in northern Alaska has been melting at an increasing rate over the past 30 years. The data table below shows average annual ground temperature and permafrost depth at a research station near Prudhoe Bay from 1990 to 2020. Using the data, construct an explanation for the observed changes in permafrost depth. Then describe ONE method scientists could use to monitor the ongoing impact of permafrost thaw on local ecosystems."*

---

**CR Example (Grade 10, DOK 3, AK.Sci.10.ESS.2):**

*"The map below shows the locations of active volcanoes in the Aleutian Islands chain along the Pacific Ring of Fire. Using your knowledge of plate tectonics, explain why volcanic activity is concentrated in this region. Include in your explanation the type of plate boundary involved and the geoscience processes that lead to magma formation."*

## 7. THREE-DIMENSIONAL DESIGN

The Alaska Science Standards require that items reflect scientific practices, content knowledge, and crosscutting concepts. Below is a design guide for constructing well-aligned items.

### Three Dimensions Alignment Guide

| Dimension | Example Application in Items |
|---|---|
| **Content Knowledge** (What students know) | Alaska salmon life cycles; tundra food webs; plate tectonics; permafrost dynamics |
| **Scientific Practices** (What students do) | Analyze data; construct explanations; plan investigations; use models; argue from evidence |
| **Crosscutting Concepts** (Lens of thinking) | Cause and effect; patterns; energy and matter; stability and change; systems and system models |

## 8. SCORING RUBRICS

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–3 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 3 | Response accurately addresses the science concept; demonstrates clear understanding of the phenomenon or investigation; includes specific, relevant scientific evidence or data; explanation is complete and logically connected |
| 2 | Response partially addresses the science concept; demonstrates adequate understanding; includes some scientific evidence but it may be general or incompletely connected to the explanation; response is mostly correct |
| 1 | Response minimally addresses the science concept; demonstrates limited understanding; includes little or no scientific evidence; explanation may be vague, partially correct, or contain scientific inaccuracies |
| 0 | Response does not address the science concept; is blank, off-topic, or demonstrates no scientific understanding; restates the question without any explanation |

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 5 Phenomena:**
- "Every fall, millions of salmon return to the same Alaska streams where they hatched — how do they find their way back, and what happens to the ecosystem when they arrive?"
- "Students in Barrow noticed that the sun did not set for over two months in summer — what causes this extreme daylight pattern?"
- "After a large earthquake near Anchorage, students felt the ground shake and saw waves in a nearby lake — what caused the ground to move?"
- "A tundra food web near Nome shows caribou, Arctic foxes, lemmings, snowy owls, and grasses — what would happen if the lemming population crashed?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "Scientists measured that the Mendenhall Glacier near Juneau has retreated over 1.5 miles since 1929 — what factors are driving this change, and what evidence supports it?"
- "Permafrost near Prudhoe Bay has been thawing at increasing rates, causing buildings and roads to sink — what is happening beneath the surface?"
- "The 1964 Great Alaska Earthquake (magnitude 9.2) caused tsunamis that reached Hawaii — how did energy travel that far through the ocean?"
- "Researchers found that rising ocean temperatures in the Bering Sea are causing shifts in the distribution of fish species — what explains this pattern?"

**Grade 10 Phenomena:**
- "The Aleutian Islands form a volcanic arc stretching 1,200 miles — why are volcanoes concentrated in this specific chain, and what tectonic processes create them?"
- "Aurora borealis displays are far more frequent and vivid in Fairbanks than in Anchorage — what physical processes explain the variation in aurora visibility with latitude?"
- "Bristol Bay sockeye salmon populations have fluctuated dramatically over the past 50 years — what biological and environmental factors drive these population dynamics?"
- "Scientists predict that by 2100, most of Alaska's glaciers will have retreated significantly — what evidence supports this claim, and what are the cascading effects on Earth systems?"

## 11. QUALITY CHECKLIST AND COMMON PITFALLS

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 5, 8, or 10 ONLY (not other grades)
- [ ] Standard code uses AK.Sci.[Grade].[Domain].[#] format
- [ ] Item connects content knowledge, scientific practice, and crosscutting concept
- [ ] Phenomenon is authentic Alaska-based and grade-appropriate
- [ ] CR prompt includes evidence directive ("Use evidence to support your answer")
- [ ] Stimulus (data table/graph/diagram) is legible and clearly labeled
- [ ] All CR score anchors written before item is finalized
- [ ] Item avoids vocabulary beyond grade-band expectations
- [ ] Science content is accurate and current (especially climate/ecology data)
- [ ] Alaska-specific context is factually accurate (verify distances, elevations, species)
- [ ] Grade 10 items reflect high school rigor expectations
- [ ] Cultural sensitivity maintained when referencing Alaska Native connections to land and ecosystems

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing items for Grades 3, 4, 6, 7 | AK STAR Science tests only Gr 5, 8, and 10 | Restrict to Grades 5, 8, and 10 |
| Items testing only factual recall | Alaska standards emphasize scientific practices and reasoning | Embed scientific practices into item design |
| Generic science phenomena, not Alaska-anchored | AK STAR items benefit from Alaska-specific contexts | Use Denali, glaciers, salmon, tundra, volcanoes, aurora |
| Inaccurate Alaska science facts | Students and scorers may know Alaska specifics | Verify facts: Denali = 20,310 ft; 1964 earthquake = 9.2 magnitude; etc. |
| CR prompts without evidence requirement | Students may respond without citing data | Always include "Use evidence to support your answer" |
| Oversimplifying Alaska ecosystems | Alaska has highly complex, interconnected systems | Represent ecological complexity accurately |
| Ignoring Grade 10 | AK STAR Science includes Grade 10 unlike many states | Include Grade 10 items with appropriate rigor |

## 12. PROMPT PATTERNS

The following five patterns reflect the most common AK STAR Science item archetypes.

### Pattern 1: Observation to Explanation (CR, DOK 2)
> "Scientists observed [Alaska phenomenon — e.g., glacier retreat, salmon decline, volcanic activity]. Using your knowledge of [science concept], explain what is causing [observation]. Use evidence to support your answer."

### Pattern 2: Data Analysis (MC or CR, DOK 2)
> "The table/graph below shows [Alaska science data — e.g., permafrost depth, salmon populations, temperature trends]. Based on the data, which conclusion is BEST supported? / What pattern do the data show? Explain your reasoning."

### Pattern 3: Cause and Effect (MC, DOK 2)
> "A student learned that [Alaska-based observable change — e.g., Arctic sea ice is decreasing, a volcano erupted in the Aleutians] occurred after [event/condition]. Which explanation BEST describes why this happened?"

### Pattern 4: Investigation Design (CR, DOK 3)
> "A scientist wants to test whether [variable] affects [outcome] in [Alaska ecosystem — e.g., a salmon spawning stream, tundra plant growth, glacial melt rate]. Describe how the scientist could design an investigation to answer this question. Include what the scientist would measure and how they would know if the variable made a difference."

### Pattern 5: Human Impact and Solutions (CR, DOK 3)
> "Human activities have [impacted Alaska environment in specific way — e.g., oil development near Arctic refuge, fishing pressure in Bristol Bay, coastal erosion from warming permafrost]. Describe TWO effects this has had on [ecosystem/organism]. Then explain ONE method scientists or engineers could use to monitor or reduce this impact."
