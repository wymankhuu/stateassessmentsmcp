> **KEY NOTE:** Alaska uses its own **Alaska Academic Standards** for ELA, which are state-specific. While they share structural similarities with national frameworks, they include Alaska-specific content expectations and cultural responsiveness requirements. Always reference Alaska ELA Standards, not CCSS, when coding items.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Student demonstrates thorough and sophisticated understanding of grade-level ELA standards; applies skills flexibly across complex texts |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Student demonstrates solid understanding of grade-level ELA standards; meets expectations for reading, writing, and language |
| 2 | **Below Proficient** | Student demonstrates partial understanding of grade-level ELA standards; inconsistent application of skills |
| 1 | **Far Below Proficient** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding of grade-level ELA standards; significant gaps in foundational reading and writing skills |

> **Item-writing target:** "Proficient" performance. Items should require genuine application of Alaska ELA standards at the expected DOK level — not mere recall.

## 2. STANDARDS FRAMEWORK

> AK STAR ELA is aligned to the **Alaska English Language Arts Standards**, which are state-specific standards organized around key literacy strands. These standards emphasize culturally responsive literacy instruction reflecting Alaska's unique populations and geography.

### Alaska ELA Standards — Strands

| Strand | Focus | Grade Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading Literature (RL)** | Literary text comprehension; theme; character; craft; structure | All tested grades |
| **Reading Informational Text (RI)** | Informational and expository comprehension; central idea; evidence; argument | All tested grades |
| **Writing (W)** | Argumentative, informative/explanatory, and narrative writing; evidence use | All tested grades; ER items |
| **Language (L)** | Vocabulary acquisition; grammar; conventions; figurative language | Embedded across items |

### Key Reading Literature Standards (RL)

| Standard | Description |
|---|---|
| AK.ELA.RL.1 | Cite textual evidence to support analysis of what the text says explicitly and inferentially |
| AK.ELA.RL.2 | Determine theme or central idea; analyze development over the course of a text; provide summary |
| AK.ELA.RL.3 | Analyze how characters, setting, and plot interact and contribute to meaning |
| AK.ELA.RL.4 | Determine meaning of words and phrases, including figurative and connotative language |
| AK.ELA.RL.5 | Analyze structure of text (chapters, scenes, stanzas) and how parts contribute to the whole |
| AK.ELA.RL.6 | Analyze point of view; how author develops and distinguishes perspectives |
| AK.ELA.RL.9 | Compare and contrast texts addressing similar themes or topics |

### Key Reading Informational Text Standards (RI)

| Standard | Description |
|---|---|
| AK.ELA.RI.1 | Cite textual evidence; distinguish what the text says from inferences drawn |
| AK.ELA.RI.2 | Determine central idea; analyze development; provide objective summary |
| AK.ELA.RI.3 | Analyze how individuals, events, and ideas are introduced, illustrated, and elaborated |
| AK.ELA.RI.4 | Determine meaning of academic and domain-specific vocabulary in context |
| AK.ELA.RI.6 | Determine author's point of view or purpose; analyze how rhetoric advances the position |
| AK.ELA.RI.8 | Evaluate the reasoning and evidence in an argument; assess sufficiency |
| AK.ELA.RI.9 | Analyze how two or more texts address similar topics; compare approaches |

### Key Writing Standards (W)

| Standard | Description |
|---|---|
| AK.ELA.W.1 | Write arguments supported by relevant evidence from literary or informational sources |
| AK.ELA.W.2 | Write informative/explanatory texts that examine and convey ideas clearly |
| AK.ELA.W.9 | Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis and reflection |

> **Alaska Standards Note:** When coding items, always use the **AK.ELA** prefix (e.g., AK.ELA.RL.5.2 for Grade 5 Reading Literature Standard 2). Do not use generic CCSS notation.

## 3. TEST STRUCTURE

The AK STAR ELA test is a computer-adaptive assessment administered in a single testing window during spring.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|              AK STAR ELA TEST STRUCTURE (Grades 3–8, 10)         |
+-------------------+--------------------+-------------------------+
|   SECTION 1       |   SECTION 2        |   SECTION 3             |
|   ~40–50 minutes  |   ~40–50 minutes   |   ~40–50 minutes        |
|                   |                    |                         |
| LITERARY READING  | INFORMATIONAL      | WRITING FROM SOURCES    |
| - Passage(s)      |   READING          | - Extended Response     |
| - MC items        | - Passage(s)       | - MC (language/craft)   |
| - MS items        | - MC items         | - CR items              |
| - CR items        | - MS items         |                         |
|                   | - CR items         |                         |
+-------------------+--------------------+-------------------------+
```

### Section Details by Grade Band

| Section | Grades 3–4 Items | Grades 5–6 Items | Grades 7–8 Items | Grade 10 Items |
|---------|------------------|------------------|------------------|----------------|
| Section 1 (Literary) | 6 MC + 1 MS + 1 CR | 7 MC + 1 MS + 1 CR | 8 MC + 2 MS + 1 CR | 8 MC + 2 MS + 1 CR |
| Section 2 (Informational) | 6 MC + 1 MS + 1 CR | 7 MC + 1 MS + 1 CR | 8 MC + 2 MS + 1 CR | 8 MC + 2 MS + 1 CR |

> **Grade 10 Note:** The Grade 10 AK STAR ELA assessment serves as Alaska's high school accountability measure. Text complexity and item rigor increase accordingly, with passages reflecting high school expectations.

## 5. ITEM TYPES

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Single correct answer                                   |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Typically DOK 1–2                                       |
|  Tests reading comprehension, vocabulary, craft          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 6, DOK 2, AK.ELA.RL.4 — Figurative Language):**

*Passage excerpt: "The northern lights draped across the sky like a quilt sewn from ribbons of green and violet, wrapping the frozen land in an eerie glow."*

Which phrase from the passage uses figurative language to describe the aurora borealis?

- A. "across the sky"
- B. "the frozen land"
- C. "like a quilt sewn from ribbons"
- D. "an eerie glow"

*Correct Answer: C*

---

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT — 1 point                                  |
|                                                          |
|  5–7 options; student selects ALL that apply             |
|  All correct selections required for full credit         |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Typically DOK 2                                         |
|  Tests evidence identification, multiple detail recall   |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, AK.ELA.RI.1):**

*"Select TWO details from the passage that support the central idea that the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race tests the endurance of both mushers and their dog teams."*

- A. "The race covers over 1,000 miles of frozen terrain."
- B. "The first Iditarod race was held in 1973."
- C. "Mushers must carry mandatory survival gear at all times."
- D. "Nome is a city in western Alaska."
- E. "Temperatures along the trail can drop to minus 50 degrees."

*Correct Answers: A, E*

---

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written response (2–5 sentences typical)                |
|  MUST cite evidence from the text                        |
|  Human scored using CR rubric                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
|  Tests reading comprehension + evidence use              |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 7, DOK 2, AK.ELA.RI.3):**

*"Explain how the construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline affected the lives of Alaska Native communities along its route. Use evidence from the passage to support your answer."*

---

### Extended Response (ER)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EXTENDED RESPONSE — 0–4 points                          |
|                                                          |
|  Multi-paragraph essay (typically 3–5 paragraphs)        |
|  Draws from one or more source texts                     |
|  Must include textual evidence with elaboration          |
|  Human scored using ER rubric                            |
|  DOK 3                                                   |
|  Tests synthesis + argumentation + writing craft         |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**ER Example (Grade 8, DOK 3, AK.ELA.W.1 + RI.9):**

*"Both the article about subsistence fishing rights in rural Alaska and the passage about commercial fishing regulations describe how different groups depend on Alaska's salmon. Using evidence from BOTH texts, write an essay arguing which approach to salmon management better serves the long-term needs of Alaska's communities."*

## 6. DEPTH OF KNOWLEDGE (DOK)

| DOK | Label | ELA Application Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recall & Reproduction | Identify a word's meaning from context; locate a detail in text; define a literary term |
| 2 | Skills & Concepts | Explain how a character's actions affect plot; analyze text structure; compare two details |
| 3 | Strategic Thinking | Compare two authors' perspectives on Alaska statehood; synthesize evidence across texts; evaluate argument strength |
| 4 | Extended Thinking | (Rare on AK STAR ELA) Long-term research or project-based tasks |

> The **ER item is always DOK 3**. Most MC are DOK 2. Approximately 15–20% of MC are DOK 1 (vocabulary or detail location). MS items typically target DOK 2.

## 7. LEXILE AND TEXT COMPLEXITY

### Lexile Ranges by Grade (AK STAR ELA Targets)

| Grade | Lexile Range | Example Text Types |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 520–820L | Folktales, simple informational passages, Alaska Native legends (adapted) |
| 4 | 740–940L | Chapter book excerpts, magazine articles, Alaska history narratives |
| 5 | 830–1010L | Historical narratives (gold rush, Iditarod), science articles (wildlife) |
| 6 | 925–1070L | Biographies (Alaska pioneers), persuasive essays, literary fiction |
| 7 | 970–1120L | Primary source documents, complex narratives set in Alaska |
| 8 | 985–1130L | Speeches, analytical essays, literary excerpts with Alaska themes |
| 10 | 1050–1335L | Complex literary works, extended informational texts, editorials, policy documents |

> Text complexity also considers **qualitative factors** (purpose, structure, knowledge demands) and **reader/task considerations**. Alaska-specific passages may include culturally specific vocabulary (e.g., umiak, potlatch, muktuk) which should be supported by context clues within the passage.

## 8. SCORING RUBRICS

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | The response accurately addresses the prompt; demonstrates clear understanding of the text; includes specific, relevant textual evidence; evidence is clearly connected to the response and supports the analysis |
| 1 | The response partially addresses the prompt; demonstrates partial understanding; includes some evidence, but it may be general, loosely connected, or partially inaccurate; reasoning is underdeveloped |
| 0 | The response does not address the prompt; demonstrates little or no understanding; includes no evidence or irrelevant content; blank or off-topic responses |

> **CR Key Reminder:** A score of 0 is given for responses that are entirely off-task, blank, or restate the question without engagement. Partial credit (1) requires at least an attempt at evidence.

---

### Extended Response (ER) Rubric — 0–4 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | The response clearly addresses the prompt with a well-developed, focused claim or controlling idea. Evidence from text(s) is specific, accurate, and effectively elaborated. Writing demonstrates strong organization, transitions, and command of language conventions. |
| 3 | The response addresses the prompt with an adequate claim or idea. Evidence from text(s) is mostly specific and mostly accurate, with adequate elaboration. Organization is generally clear; minor errors in conventions do not impede meaning. |
| 2 | The response partially addresses the prompt; claim may be unclear or underdeveloped. Evidence is present but may be general, partially inaccurate, or inadequately elaborated. Organization may be inconsistent; some errors in conventions. |
| 1 | The response minimally addresses the prompt; claim is very weak or missing. Little or no textual evidence; response may summarize rather than analyze. Poor organization and significant convention errors that impede meaning. |
| 0 | The response does not address the prompt; is blank, off-topic, copied directly from the passage, or is incomprehensible. |

## 10. CONTEXTUALIZATION — ALASKA ELA

When generating passages and item contexts for the AK STAR ELA test, prioritize the following authentic Alaska settings and themes. Alaska has the most distinctive context of any U.S. state — its geography, cultures, history, and environment are deeply woven into its educational standards and assessment philosophy.

### Alaska Native Cultures

| Culture / People | Grade Band | Suggested Use |
|---|---|---|
| Tlingit traditions and oral storytelling | 3–8, 10 | Literary passages; narrative structure analysis; theme of cultural preservation |
| Haida art and totem pole traditions | 4–8, 10 | Informational texts on art and symbolism; author's purpose |
| Inupiat whaling traditions and community life | 5–8, 10 | Informational passages; compare/contrast traditional and modern practices |
| Yupik language preservation efforts | 6–8, 10 | Argumentative/persuasive informational texts; central idea and evidence |
| Potlatch ceremonies and cultural significance | 4–8, 10 | Literary and informational; cultural context; author's perspective |
| Subsistence fishing and hunting practices | 5–8, 10 | Informational texts; argumentation; multiple perspectives on resource management |

### Geographic and Historical Contexts

| Setting / Theme | Grade Band | Suggested Use |
|---|---|---|
| Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race | 3–8 | Narrative nonfiction; sequence; endurance themes; data-embedded passages |
| Alaska Gold Rush (Klondike, Nome) | 4–8, 10 | Historical informational texts; primary sources; cause and effect |
| Trans-Alaska Pipeline | 6–8, 10 | Informational texts; engineering narratives; environmental argumentation |
| Jack London and Alaska literary connections | 7–8, 10 | Literary excerpts; author's craft; setting analysis; naturalism |
| Alaska statehood (1959) and its significance | 5–8, 10 | Historical informational; compare/contrast before/after statehood |
| Aurora borealis descriptions and cultural meaning | 3–8, 10 | Literary passages (descriptive writing); informational science texts |
| Subsistence way of life in rural Alaska | 4–8, 10 | Informational passages; perspective; argumentation about tradition vs. modernity |
| Denali and wilderness narratives | 3–8, 10 | Literary fiction; adventure narratives; descriptive and figurative language |

### Passage Topic Suggestions by Grade

**Grades 3–4:**
- A Tlingit legend about Raven bringing light to the world (RL)
- An informational passage about how sled dogs are trained for the Iditarod (RI)
- A story about a child experiencing the aurora borealis for the first time (RL)
- An article about Alaska's state symbols and what they represent (RI)

**Grades 5–6:**
- A narrative about a family's subsistence fishing camp on the Yukon River (RL)
- An informational text comparing the Alaska Gold Rush to the California Gold Rush (RI)
- A biography of a modern Iditarod champion (RI)
- A literary passage about surviving winter in a remote Alaska village (RL)

**Grades 7–8:**
- An excerpt from Jack London's work set in the Yukon/Alaska territory (RL)
- An argumentative article about the Trans-Alaska Pipeline's environmental impact (RI)
- Paired texts: a Haida oral tradition and a modern essay about cultural preservation (RL + RI)
- An informational passage about Alaska statehood debates of the 1950s (RI)

**Grade 10:**
- Paired texts on subsistence fishing rights: Alaska Native perspectives vs. state regulatory perspectives (RI + RI)
- A literary analysis passage featuring Alaska wilderness writing (London, Krakauer connections) (RL)
- An extended informational text on the economic and cultural significance of the Iditarod (RI)
- An argumentative essay on balancing resource extraction and environmental preservation in Alaska (RI)

> **Cultural Sensitivity Note:** When using Alaska Native cultural content, ensure passages are respectful, accurate, and reflect living cultures — not stereotyped or solely historical portrayals. Consult Alaska Standards for Culturally Responsive Schools when selecting or generating content.

## 11. QUALITY CHECKLIST AND COMMON PITFALLS

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Passage Lexile falls within grade-band range (see Section 7)
- [ ] All MC options are plausible; no "trick" options or clearly wrong choices
- [ ] CR prompt ends with "Use evidence from the text to support your answer"
- [ ] ER prompt references source text(s) explicitly
- [ ] DOK level matches intended cognitive demand
- [ ] Standard code uses AK.ELA format (not CCSS)
- [ ] Item avoids cultural bias; reviewed for Alaska-specific fairness and cultural sensitivity
- [ ] MS items have exactly the correct number of right answers stated in the stem
- [ ] ER rubric anchor responses are written before item is finalized
- [ ] Grade 10 items reflect high school text complexity expectations
- [ ] Alaska Native cultural content is respectful and accurate
- [ ] Calculator/tools noted as N/A (ELA)

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Using CCSS standard codes | Alaska uses Alaska Academic Standards, not CCSS | Use AK.ELA.RL/RI/W notation |
| CR prompt without evidence requirement | Students may respond without citing text | Always include "Use evidence from the text" |
| Generic "Lower 48" context | AK STAR benefits from Alaska-specific authenticity | Anchor passages in Alaska places, cultures, events |
| Stereotyping Alaska Native cultures | Reduces to igloos, polar bears, and frozen tundra | Present living, diverse cultures; consult cultural standards |
| Ignoring Grade 10 | AK STAR tests Grade 10 unlike many states that stop at 8 | Include Grade 10 items with appropriate rigor |
| Over-reliance on DOK 1 MC | AK STAR expects mostly DOK 2 MC | Shift MC to inference/analysis tasks |
| MS items with ambiguous correct count | Students need to know how many to select | Specify "Select TWO" or "Select THREE" in stem |

## 12. PROMPT PATTERNS

The following five prompt patterns cover the most frequently tested item archetypes on the AK STAR ELA assessment.

### Pattern 1: Central Idea + Evidence (CR/ER, RI.2)
> "What is the central idea of [passage title]? Use evidence from the text to support your answer."

### Pattern 2: Character Analysis (CR, RL.3)
> "How does [character name]'s [action/decision/reaction] in the passage reveal [character trait or theme]? Use evidence from the text to support your answer."

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose / Point of View (MC/CR, RI.6 or RL.6)
> "Why does the author include [detail/section/quote]? What does this reveal about the author's [purpose/perspective]?"

### Pattern 4: Compare Across Texts (ER, RI.9 or RL.9)
> "[Text 1 title] and [Text 2 title] both address [shared theme — e.g., life in remote Alaska, cultural preservation]. Using evidence from BOTH texts, write an essay explaining how each author [analyzes/presents/argues about] [topic]."

### Pattern 5: Vocabulary in Context (MC, RL.4 or RI.4)
> "As used in paragraph [#], what does the word '[word]' most likely mean?"
> - A. [synonym 1]
> - B. [synonym 2]
> - C. [CORRECT: contextually accurate meaning]
> - D. [common denotation that doesn't fit context]
