## AssessmentForge Skill Guide | Grades 4 and 8

## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Pennsylvania System of School Assessment — Science (PSSA Science) |
| **Administering Agency** | Pennsylvania Department of Education (PDE) |
| **Vendor** | Data Recognition Corporation (DRC) |
| **Grades Tested** | **Grade 4** and **Grade 8** |
| **Testing Window** | April – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based and paper-based options |
| **Standards** | Pennsylvania Academic Standards for Science and Technology & Engineering Education |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** PSSA Science is administered **only at Grades 4 and 8**. Do not generate PSSA Science items for any other grade levels. Grade 4 covers elementary science and technology standards. Grade 8 covers middle-level science and technology standards.

> **PA Standards — NOT NGSS:** Pennsylvania does **not** use the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS). The PSSA is aligned to the **Pennsylvania Academic Standards for Science and Technology & Engineering Education**, which are organized into four main categories: Biological Sciences (3.1), Physical Sciences (3.2), Earth and Space Sciences (3.3), and Technology & Engineering Education (3.4). Items are **not** required to be three-dimensional. Instead, PA emphasizes content knowledge alongside Science as Inquiry and Technology & Engineering Design.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Demonstrates a thorough understanding of science concepts and principles; applies scientific reasoning and inquiry skills with precision; integrates technology and engineering design concepts fluently |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Demonstrates an adequate understanding of science concepts and principles; applies scientific reasoning and inquiry appropriately; engages with technology and engineering design concepts competently |
| 2 | **Basic** | Demonstrates a partial understanding of science concepts; applies some scientific reasoning but with gaps; limited engagement with inquiry and technology/engineering design |
| 1 | **Below Basic** | Demonstrates minimal understanding of science concepts; unable to apply scientific reasoning or inquiry skills meaningfully; little to no engagement with technology/engineering design |

## 2. Standards Framework

> **Reference:** Pennsylvania Academic Standards for Science and Technology & Engineering Education. Standards are organized by category codes: 3.1 (Biological Sciences), 3.2 (Physical Sciences), 3.3 (Earth and Space Sciences), and 3.4 (Technology & Engineering Education). Grade-band indicators use letter suffixes (e.g., 3.1.4.A for Grade 4, 3.1.8.A for Grade 8).

|---|---|---|

| Biological Sciences | 3.1.4.C | Identify how organisms adapt to their environment and how ecosystems change over time |

## 3. Test Structure

PSSA Science uses a multi-session format. Items are scenario-based and incorporate data tables, diagrams, graphs, and models as stimuli. Both computer-based and paper-based testing are available.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|           PSSA SCIENCE STRUCTURE (Grades 4 and 8)                |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  TWO SESSIONS                                                     |
|  Computer-Based or Paper-Based                                    |
|  Approximately 50-70 minutes per session                          |
|                                                                   |
|  ~50-60 items per grade (across both sessions)                    |
|                                                                   |
|  Item types:                                                      |
|    * Multiple Choice (MC) — ~80% (~40-48 items; 1 pt each)       |
|    * Constructed Response (CR) — ~20% (~6-8 items; 0-3 pts each) |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Grade-Level Test Specifications

| Specification | Grade 4 | Grade 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Total Items (approx.) | ~50–58 | ~50–60 |
| Multiple Choice (MC) | ~40–48 | ~40–48 |
| Constructed Response (CR) | ~6–8 | ~6–8 |
| Sessions | 2 | 2 |
| Testing Time per Session | ~50–70 min | ~55–70 min |

> **Content and Inquiry Integration:** PSSA Science items emphasize both science content knowledge and the application of inquiry skills. Items should reflect how students use scientific processes (observation, measurement, investigation, analysis) to demonstrate understanding of PA science standards.

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Scenario, diagram, data table, or model stimulus        |
|  Must align to PA Science Standards                      |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, 3.2.8.B — Properties of Matter):**

A student heats a sample of limestone collected from a quarry near Lancaster, Pennsylvania. After heating, a gas is released and a white powder remains. Which observation provides the BEST evidence that a chemical change occurred?

A. The limestone sample changed temperature during heating
B. The white powder that remained has a smaller mass than the original limestone
C. A new substance with different properties was produced when the gas was released
D. The student used a Bunsen burner as the heat source

*Correct Answer: C*
*Alignment: 3.2.8.B | Process: Analyzing and Interpreting Data | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 4, DOK 2, 3.1.4.C — Ecosystems and Adaptations):**

A diagram shows: Sunlight -> Grasses -> Meadow Voles -> Red-tailed Hawks

A student studies a food chain from a Pennsylvania meadow. Which statement BEST explains why the grasses are important to this food chain?

A. Grasses receive energy from the meadow voles that live near them
B. Grasses capture energy from the sun and convert it to food for the ecosystem
C. Grasses are decomposers that break down dead organisms in the meadow
D. Grasses absorb energy from the soil through their root system

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: 3.1.4.C | Process: Applying Scientific Concepts | DOK 2*

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0-3 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written explanation using evidence from the stimulus    |
|  Stimulus always provided (data, diagram, scenario)      |
|  Must demonstrate content knowledge and reasoning        |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2-3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 8, DOK 3, 3.1.8.C — Ecosystems):**

*Stimulus: A data table showing the population changes of white-tailed deer, black bears, and understory plant cover in a Pennsylvania state forest over 15 years. When a controlled deer management program was introduced (Year 8), deer populations decreased, and understory plant cover increased significantly.*

*"Using data from the table, explain how the deer management program affected the forest ecosystem in Pennsylvania. Include evidence from the data and describe the cause-and-effect relationships between deer populations and plant cover."*

## 7. Science Inquiry & Engineering Design

Pennsylvania's science standards place strong emphasis on **Science as Inquiry** and **Technology & Engineering Education** (Standard Category 3.4). Unlike NGSS-based assessments, PA treats technology and engineering as a distinct and co-equal domain alongside the traditional science content areas.

### Science Inquiry Skills Alignment Guide

| Inquiry Skill | What Students Do | Example in PSSA Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Observing and Measuring** | Use senses and tools to gather data | Measure water quality parameters in a Susquehanna River sample; observe mineral properties in PA rocks |
| **Designing Investigations** | Plan controlled experiments | Design an experiment to test how slope angle affects erosion on PA farmland |
| **Analyzing Data** | Interpret tables, graphs, and patterns | Analyze population data for white-tailed deer in a PA state forest |
| **Drawing Conclusions** | Use evidence to support claims | Explain why acid mine drainage affects stream biodiversity using water chemistry data |
| **Communicating Results** | Present findings clearly | Write an explanation of how seasonal changes affect Lake Erie ecosystems |

### Technology & Engineering Design Process

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  PA ENGINEERING DESIGN PROCESS (Standard 3.4)                    |
|                                                                  |
|  PROBLEM: Students in Pittsburgh need to design a device that    |
|  keeps a greenhouse warm using only passive solar heating         |
|                                                                  |
|  1. DEFINE THE PROBLEM — Identify criteria and constraints       |
|  2. RESEARCH — Gather information about solar energy and heat    |
|  3. DESIGN — Propose a solution (sketch, materials list)         |
|  4. BUILD / TEST — Construct prototype; collect data             |
|  5. EVALUATE — Compare results to criteria; identify trade-offs  |
|  6. COMMUNICATE — Present the solution with evidence             |
|                                                                  |
|  PSSA items test Steps 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6 (no physical builds)   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Engineering Design by Grade Band

| Grade | Engineering Focus | Example Context |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 4 | Identify a problem; propose a simple solution; describe trade-offs | Design a birdhouse to attract Pennsylvania songbirds using given materials and constraints |
| Grade 8 | Apply the full design process; evaluate solutions against criteria; optimize designs | Design a water filtration system for a community affected by coal mine runoff in northeastern PA |

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0-3 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 3 | Response demonstrates a thorough understanding of the science concept; provides a complete and accurate explanation; includes specific, relevant evidence from the stimulus; reasoning is logically connected and scientifically sound; integrates inquiry or engineering design thinking where appropriate |
| 2 | Response demonstrates an adequate understanding of the science concept; explanation is mostly accurate but may be incomplete; evidence from the stimulus is present but may be partially developed or imprecisely cited; reasoning is generally logical |
| 1 | Response demonstrates a minimal understanding of the science concept; explanation is vague, overgeneralized, or contains minor scientific inaccuracies; little or no evidence from the stimulus is used; reasoning is incomplete or unclear |
| 0 | Response does not address the science concept; blank; off-topic; restates the stimulus without explanation; demonstrates no science understanding |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Evidence from the stimulus is required.** A response that relies solely on prior knowledge without referencing the provided data, diagram, or scenario earns a maximum of 1 point.
- **Scientific accuracy matters.** A significant scientific misconception drops the response by at least one score point.
- **Inquiry and engineering reasoning earns full credit.** A Score 3 response integrates content knowledge with scientific reasoning, evidence use, or engineering design thinking as appropriate to the prompt.
- **Grade-appropriate vocabulary.** Grade 4 responses should use simpler terminology than Grade 8 responses, but both must demonstrate accurate use of science vocabulary.

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 4 Phenomena:**
- "A student visiting Presque Isle State Park on Lake Erie noticed that the sand dunes near the shore have very different plants growing on them compared to the forests farther inland. Why do different plants grow in these two areas?"
- "Farmers in Lancaster County noticed that after a heavy rainstorm, the soil in their fields washed downhill into a nearby creek, turning the water brown. What caused the soil to move, and how could the farmer reduce this problem?"
- "Students at a school near the Susquehanna River observed that the river looks very different in spring compared to summer — it is higher, faster, and muddier in spring. What explains these seasonal changes?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "Scientists monitoring streams near abandoned coal mines in northeastern Pennsylvania found that the water is orange-colored and very few fish or insects live in these streams. What is causing the water to change, and why are organisms unable to survive there?"

- "After the Three Mile Island incident in 1979, scientists monitored radiation levels, water quality, and wildlife populations in the surrounding Susquehanna River area for decades. How can long-term data collection help scientists understand the environmental impact of such an event?"

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 4 or 8 ONLY (PSSA Science is not administered at other grades)
- [ ] Standard code is a valid PA Academic Standard (3.1.x.x, 3.2.x.x, 3.3.x.x, or 3.4.x.x)
- [ ] Grade band in standard code matches the target grade (e.g., 3.1.4.A for Grade 4, 3.1.8.A for Grade 8)
- [ ] Item aligns to PA standards, NOT NGSS (do not use NGSS PE codes or three-dimensional language)
- [ ] Anchoring phenomenon or scenario is authentic and Pennsylvania-relevant where possible
- [ ] Stimulus (data table, graph, diagram) has clear labels, units, and realistic data
- [ ] CR prompt directs students to use evidence from the stimulus
- [ ] All MC distractors represent real misconceptions or plausible errors (not obviously wrong)
- [ ] Score 3 anchor response written before finalizing CR item
- [ ] Science content is accurate and reflects current scientific understanding
- [ ] Vocabulary is grade-appropriate for the targeted grade level (Grade 4 vs. Grade 8)
- [ ] Technology & Engineering items reflect PA Standard 3.4 emphasis on design process and societal impact

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing PSSA Science items for grades other than 4 or 8 | PSSA Science is only at Grades 4 and 8 | Restrict to tested grades |
| Using NGSS PE codes or three-dimensional language | PA uses its own standards, not NGSS | Use PA standard codes (3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4) and PA terminology |
| Requiring "DCI + SEP + CCC" integration | This is an NGSS framework; PA does not use it | Focus on PA content standards + inquiry skills |
| Ignoring Technology & Engineering (3.4) domain | PA explicitly tests technology and engineering as ~15% of the assessment | Include 3.4 items in every item set |
| Generic science contexts not anchored to Pennsylvania | PSSA favors PA-specific contexts | Use Susquehanna River, Appalachian Mountains, PA wildlife, PA agriculture contexts |
| CR prompts without evidence directive | Students may respond without citing data | Include "Use evidence from the [data/graph/diagram]" in all CR prompts |
| Mismatching grade band in standard code | 3.1.4.A is Grade 4; 3.1.8.A is Grade 8 | Verify the grade digit matches the target grade |
| Overly complex vocabulary for Grade 4 | Grade 4 students are 9-10 years old | Use age-appropriate science vocabulary; define technical terms in context |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

The following patterns reflect the most common PSSA Science item archetypes.

### Pattern 1: Data-to-Conclusion (MC, DOK 2)
> "The [graph / table / diagram] below shows [Pennsylvania science scenario]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / explanation / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 2: Phenomenon Explanation (CR, DOK 3)
> "Scientists observed [Pennsylvania-anchored phenomenon]. Using evidence from the [data / model / diagram], explain [specific science focus]. Describe the [cause-and-effect / pattern] relationship and include specific evidence from the [stimulus]."

### Pattern 3: Investigation Design (CR, DOK 3)
> "A student wants to investigate whether [variable] affects [outcome] in [Pennsylvania context]. Describe a step-by-step procedure the student could follow. Include what the student would change, what would be measured, and what would be kept the same."

### Pattern 4: Engineering Design Evaluation (MC, DOK 2-3)
> "A group of students is designing a [device / solution] to [solve a Pennsylvania-relevant problem]. They have the following materials and constraints: [list]. Which [change / step / modification] would BEST improve their design?"

### Pattern 5: Model Interpretation (MC, DOK 2)
> "A student made a [diagram / model] showing [Pennsylvania science concept]. Which statement BEST describes what the [diagram / model] shows about [science focus]?"

### Pattern 6: Evidence-Based Argument (CR, DOK 3)
> "Two students disagree about [Pennsylvania science claim]. Student A says [misconception]. Student B says [scientifically accurate claim]. Using evidence from the [data / diagram], explain which student is correct and why. Include at least [two] pieces of evidence from the [stimulus] to support your answer."
