## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Missouri Assessment Program (MAP) — English Language Arts |
| **Administering Agency** | Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) |
| **Vendor** | MO State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 |
| **Testing Window** | April – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based |
| **Standards** | Missouri Learning Standards for English Language Arts |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **MAP ELA Overview:** The Missouri Assessment Program measures student mastery of the Missouri Learning Standards for English Language Arts. Items are passage-based and assess reading comprehension, writing, and language skills across literary and informational texts. MAP ELA includes Technology-Enhanced Items (TEI) that go beyond traditional multiple choice.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Student demonstrates thorough and sophisticated understanding of grade-level ELA standards; analyzes complex texts with precision and depth |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Student demonstrates adequate understanding; reads and comprehends grade-level texts; uses relevant evidence to support analysis |
| 2 | **Basic** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; inconsistent text analysis; limited use of evidence |
| 1 | **Below Basic** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding; significant gaps in reading comprehension and analysis skills |

## 2. Standards Framework

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RL.1** — Key details & inference | Identify explicit details; make simple inferences | Cite textual evidence; analyze explicit vs. inferential meaning | Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine where text is ambiguous |
| **RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine the lesson, moral, or central message; summarize | Analyze theme development across the text | Analyze multiple themes and their complex interplay within texts |
| **RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe character traits, motivations, and feelings | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development |
| **RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; impact of word choice | Analyze impact of specific word choices on meaning, tone, and aesthetic quality |
| **RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's perspective | Analyze how differences in point of view create effects | Analyze how point of view and perspective shape content and style |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RI.1** — Key details & inference | Ask and answer questions; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how key ideas are developed across sections | Analyze complex set of ideas and explain how they interact |
| **RI.6** — Author's purpose & POV | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view or purpose; analyze how it is conveyed | Determine author's point of view in text with multiple perspectives |
| **RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support an author's point | Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims | Evaluate the reasoning and rhetoric in seminal texts |
| **RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare texts on the same topic | Analyze how different authors present information | Analyze and synthesize foundational documents of significance |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, examples | 3 |
| **W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5 Informational, DOK 2, RI.5.3):**

*[After reading an article about the construction of the Gateway Arch in St. Louis]*

Which detail from the article BEST explains why the Gateway Arch was built in St. Louis?

A. The Gateway Arch is 630 feet tall and made of stainless steel
B. St. Louis was known as the "Gateway to the West" because it was the starting point for pioneers heading westward
C. The Arch was designed by Finnish-American architect Eero Saarinen
D. The construction of the Arch took over two years to complete

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RI.5.3 | Reading — Informational Text | DOK 2*

### Technology-Enhanced Item (TEI)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  TECHNOLOGY-ENHANCED ITEM — 1–2 points                   |
|                                                          |
|  Formats: multi-select, drag-and-drop, evidence-based    |
|  selected response, matching, inline choice              |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**TEI Example — Evidence-Based Selected Response (Grade 7, DOK 3, RI.7.6):**

*[After reading an excerpt from Mark Twain's account of life on the Mississippi River]*

Part A: What is Mark Twain's purpose in describing the Mississippi River in this passage?

A. To argue that the river should be preserved for future generations
B. To convey the beauty and danger of the river through vivid description
C. To persuade readers to travel on steamboats
D. To compare the Mississippi River to other American rivers

Part B: Which sentence from the passage BEST supports your answer to Part A?

A. "The river had a fine time of it, dancing and swirling over the sand bars."
B. "I had seen nothing like it in all my travels east."
C. "The face of the water, in time, became a wonderful book."
D. "We arrived at the dock precisely at noon."

*Correct Answers: Part A: B; Part B: C*
*Alignment: RI.7.6 | Reading — Informational Text | DOK 3*

### Constructed Response (CR) — 0–2 Points

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Short written response (3–5 sentences)                  |
|  Must use evidence from the passage                      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 6 Literary, DOK 3, RL.6.3):**

*[After reading a story about a student exploring a cave in the Missouri Ozarks]*

Explain how the cave setting affects the main character's actions and feelings during the story. Use TWO pieces of evidence from the passage to support your response.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Identifies two relevant pieces of evidence showing how the cave setting affected the character; explains the connection clearly*
- *1 pt: One piece of evidence with explanation, OR two pieces without adequate connection*
- *0 pts: No relevant evidence or off-topic*

---

### Constructed Response (CR) — 0–4 Points

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EXTENDED CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–4 points              |
|                                                          |
|  Multi-paragraph written response                        |
|  Must use evidence from the passage(s)                   |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 3                                                   |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 11, DOK 3, RI.11.9 + W.11.1):**

*[After reading two articles — one about the economic revitalization of Kansas City's jazz district and another about the cultural significance of preserving jazz heritage]*

Both articles discuss the future of Kansas City's jazz heritage. Using evidence from BOTH articles, write an essay analyzing how each author's perspective shapes their argument about preserving jazz culture. Which argument is better supported? Explain your reasoning.

*Scoring:*
- *4 pts: Well-developed analysis; strong evidence from both sources; clear, well-organized argument with precise language*
- *3 pts: Adequate analysis; evidence from both sources; organized argument with minor gaps*
- *2 pts: Partial analysis; evidence from one or both sources but underdeveloped; inconsistent organization*
- *1 pt: Minimal analysis; limited evidence; weak organization*
- *0 pts: Off-topic, blank, or no evidence*

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response demonstrates thorough understanding. Uses specific, relevant evidence from the passage. Explanation is complete, accurate, and clearly connected to the question. |
| 1 | Response demonstrates partial understanding. Evidence is present but may be vague or incomplete. Explanation is partially developed. |
| 0 | Response does not address the question; blank; off-topic; no evidence from the passage. |

### Extended Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–4 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Well-developed, focused response with a clear claim or controlling idea. Specific, accurate evidence from text(s) is effectively elaborated. Strong organization, transitions, and command of language conventions. |
| 3 | Adequate response with a clear claim. Evidence from text(s) is mostly specific and accurate with adequate elaboration. Organization is generally clear; minor convention errors. |
| 2 | Partially developed response; claim may be unclear. Evidence is present but general or partially inaccurate. Organization inconsistent; some convention errors. |
| 1 | Minimally developed response; claim is very weak or missing. Little textual evidence. Poor organization and significant convention errors. |
| 0 | Does not address the prompt; blank; off-topic; copied from passage; incomprehensible. |

## 10. Contextualization — Missouri ELA

When generating passages and prompts, prioritize authentic Missouri settings and culture:

### Missouri Context Guide

| Context | Passage Type | Grade Band |
|---|---|---|
| Gateway Arch and St. Louis as the "Gateway to the West" | Informational or Historical fiction | 3–11 |
| Missouri River history and ecology | Informational or Literary | 4–11 |
| Mark Twain and Missouri literary heritage | Literary or Informational | 5–11 |
| Ozark Mountains culture, ecology, and folklore | Literary fiction or Informational | 3–8 |
| Kansas City jazz history and culture | Informational or Literary | 5–11 |
| Missouri cave systems (Meramec Caverns, Mark Twain Cave) | Informational or Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Lewis and Clark Expedition from St. Louis | Informational or Historical fiction | 4–11 |
| Missouri Civil War history and border state status | Informational | 6–11 |
| Missouri agriculture and small-town life | Literary fiction or Informational | 3–8 |
| Kansas City BBQ traditions and food culture | Informational or Literary | 4–8 |
| St. Louis Cardinals / sports culture | Literary fiction or Informational | 3–7 |
| Harry Truman and Independence, Missouri | Informational / Argumentative | 6–11 |

### Suggested Passage Topics by Grade

**Grades 3–5:**
- A class field trip to the Gateway Arch and what it symbolizes about westward expansion
- An informational passage about Missouri's famous cave systems
- A story about a student exploring the Ozark Mountains with grandparents

**Grades 6–8:**
- The history of Kansas City jazz and its influence on American music
- Mark Twain's Missouri: how the Mississippi River shaped his writing
- A narrative about a student helping with flood recovery along the Missouri River

**Grade 11:**
- Paired texts comparing perspectives on the economic revitalization of St. Louis
- Analysis of Mark Twain's craft and how Missouri settings shaped his literary voice
- An editorial examining the legacy of Missouri's role as a border state during the Civil War

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Missouri Learning Standards code verified for grade level
- [ ] MC items have exactly 4 options with one correct answer
- [ ] TEI items have clear Part A and Part B structure where applicable
- [ ] CR items (0–2) require text evidence from the passage
- [ ] CR items (0–4) require sustained analysis with evidence
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] Missouri context incorporated where appropriate
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions
- [ ] DOK level matches actual cognitive demand
- [ ] Grade 11 items reflect appropriate complexity for high school

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| CR prompt not requiring evidence | Students may respond without citing text | Include "Use evidence from the passage" in all CR prompts |
| Confusing 0–2 and 0–4 CR rubrics | MAP uses both scoring scales | Match rubric to item type; extended responses use 0–4 |
| TEI items without clear Part A/Part B linkage | Part B must support Part A | Ensure Part B evidence directly supports Part A answer |
| Generic Midwest settings | Misses state contextualization | Use Missouri-specific locations, history, and culture |
| Grade 11 items at middle school complexity | Underestimates high school expectations | Use complex texts and analytical prompts for Grade 11 |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Key Idea / Main Idea (MC, DOK 2)
> "Which statement BEST expresses the [main idea / central message / theme] of the passage?"

### Pattern 2: Evidence-Based Selected Response (TEI, DOK 3)
> "Part A: [Analytical question]. Part B: Which [sentence / detail] from the passage BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose or Craft (MC, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice] in [paragraph/section] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding]?"

### Pattern 4: Evidence-Based Analysis (CR 0–2, DOK 3)
> "[Analytical question]. Use TWO pieces of evidence from the passage to support your response."

### Pattern 5: Extended Analysis (CR 0–4, DOK 3)
> "[Complex analytical question across one or two texts]. Use evidence from [the passage / BOTH passages] to develop a well-organized response."

### Pattern 6: Vocabulary in Context (MC, DOK 2)
> "As used in paragraph [X], the word [vocabulary word] MOST LIKELY means —"
