## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Oklahoma School Testing Program (OSTP) — Science |
| **Administering Agency** | Oklahoma State Department of Education (OSDE) |
| **Vendor** | OK State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | **Grade 5**, **Grade 8**, and **Grade 11** |
| **Testing Window** | April – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; single session |
| **Standards** | Oklahoma Academic Standards for Science |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** OSTP Science is administered **only at Grades 5, 8, and 11**. Do not generate science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7.

> **Oklahoma Science Standards:** Oklahoma adopted science standards that integrate disciplinary core ideas with scientific and engineering practices. Items should assess content knowledge alongside inquiry skills and crosscutting concepts.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Demonstrates thorough science understanding; expertly integrates scientific knowledge with practices; constructs sophisticated evidence-based explanations |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Demonstrates adequate science understanding; appropriately applies scientific practices and concepts |
| 2 | **Limited Knowledge** | Demonstrates partial understanding; applies content knowledge but struggles to integrate practices meaningfully |
| 1 | **Below Basic** | Demonstrates minimal science understanding; limited to recall of isolated facts |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Scenario, diagram, data table, or model stimulus        |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2 — Ecosystem Energy Flow):**

A diagram shows an Oklahoma prairie food chain: Sunlight -> Switchgrass -> Grasshoppers -> Meadowlarks -> Red-tailed Hawks

Which statement BEST explains the role of switchgrass in this Oklahoma prairie ecosystem?

A. Switchgrass receives energy from grasshoppers through the food chain
B. Switchgrass captures energy from the sun and converts it into food stored in its cells
C. Switchgrass is a decomposer that breaks down dead organisms in the prairie
D. Switchgrass gets most of its energy from nutrients in the red clay soil

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: Life Science — Ecosystems | Practice: Developing and Using Models | CCC: Energy and Matter | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Earth's Surface / Tornado Science):**

Meteorologists in Oklahoma observed that tornadoes form most frequently when warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico collides with cool, dry air from the north. Which statement BEST explains why this collision of air masses leads to tornado formation?

A. The cool air pushes the warm air underground, creating pressure
B. The warm, moist air rises rapidly over the cooler air, creating powerful rotating updrafts
C. The two air masses cancel each other out, causing the atmosphere to become calm
D. The cool air absorbs all the moisture from the warm air, creating dry conditions

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Weather and Atmosphere | Practice: Constructing Explanations | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–6 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  All must be selected for credit                         |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Natural Hazard Mitigation):**

Oklahoma communities experience frequent tornadoes during spring months. Select the TWO strategies that would BEST help reduce tornado-related injuries in Oklahoma communities.

A. Building homes with flat roofs to reduce wind resistance
B. Constructing community storm shelters reinforced to withstand high winds
C. Planting more trees along main streets to block tornado paths
D. Implementing early warning systems with sirens and mobile alerts
E. Moving all buildings to the southern part of the state

*Correct Answers: B, D*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Natural Hazards | Practice: Engineering Solutions | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written explanation using evidence from the stimulus    |
|  Stimulus always provided (data, diagram, scenario)      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 11, DOK 3 — Geology / Oil Formation):**

*Stimulus: A diagram showing the geologic cross-section of Oklahoma's Anadarko Basin, with layers of sedimentary rock, ancient marine deposits, and underground oil reservoirs. The diagram includes labels showing the age of each rock layer.*

Using evidence from the diagram, explain how petroleum formed in Oklahoma's Anadarko Basin. Describe TWO geologic processes that contributed to the formation and trapping of oil in this region.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Accurately explains oil formation from ancient marine organisms under heat and pressure; identifies two specific geologic processes (e.g., sedimentation, tectonic folding creating trap structures); references diagram evidence*
- *1 pt: Partially correct explanation with one process identified, OR two processes without adequate reference to diagram*
- *0 pts: Off-topic, blank, or major scientific misconception*

## 7. Three-Dimensional Design

Every OSTP Science item should integrate scientific practices, content knowledge, and crosscutting concepts.

### Three-Dimensions Alignment Guide

| Dimension | What It Is | Example in OSTP Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Content Knowledge** (What students know) | Disciplinary science content | Tornado meteorology; Oklahoma prairie ecosystems; Red River watershed; oil formation geology |
| **Scientific Practices** (What students do) | How scientists work | Analyze tornado data; construct explanations from evidence; use models to predict ecosystem changes |
| **Crosscutting Concepts** (Lens of thinking) | Cross-domain thinking tools | Cause and effect; patterns; energy and matter; stability and change; systems and system models |

### Sample Three-Dimensional Item Design (Grade 8)

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3D ITEM STRUCTURE                                               |
|                                                                  |
|  PHENOMENON: Tornado frequency in central Oklahoma has           |
|  varied over the past 50 years                                   |
|  (Authentic Oklahoma phenomenon)                                 |
|                                                                  |
|  CONTENT: Earth Science — Weather; Atmospheric dynamics          |
|  PRACTICE: Analyzing and Interpreting Data                       |
|  CROSSCUTTING: Patterns                                          |
|                                                                  |
|  STIMULUS: Data table showing annual tornado counts, average     |
|  wind speeds, and seasonal timing for central Oklahoma           |
|  over 50 years                                                   |
|                                                                  |
|  PROMPT: "Using data from the table, describe the pattern in     |
|  tornado frequency and timing. Explain what atmospheric          |
|  conditions contribute to this pattern. Include specific          |
|  evidence from the data."                                        |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response accurately addresses the science concept; demonstrates understanding of practices and crosscutting concepts; includes specific, relevant evidence from the stimulus; explanation is complete, accurate, and logically connected |
| 1 | Response addresses the science concept with partial accuracy; evidence present but may be incomplete; reasoning partially developed |
| 0 | Response does not address the science concept; blank; off-topic; restates stimulus without explanation; no science understanding demonstrated |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Evidence from the stimulus is required.** Responses relying solely on prior knowledge earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Scientific reasoning** integrating content with practices or crosscutting concepts is needed for full credit.
- **Scientific misconceptions** reduce the score by at least one point.

## 10. Contextualization — Oklahoma Science

When generating OSTP Science items, prioritize authentic Oklahoma natural settings:

### Oklahoma Science Context Guide

| Setting / Phenomenon | Grade | Science Domain | Standard Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tornado Alley meteorology and severe weather | 5, 8, 11 | Earth Science | Weather systems; atmospheric dynamics |
| Oklahoma prairie ecology and grassland habitats | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; biodiversity |
| Red River watershed and hydrology | 5, 8, 11 | Earth Science | Water cycle; erosion; human impact |
| Oklahoma geology and oil/gas formation | 8, 11 | Earth Science | Rock formation; fossil fuels |
| Oklahoma Dust Bowl soil erosion | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Soil science; human impact |
| Oklahoma wind energy development | 8, 11 | Physical Science | Energy transfer; engineering |
| Wichita Mountains wildlife refuge | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; biodiversity |
| Oklahoma earthquakes (induced seismicity) | 8, 11 | Earth Science | Geologic processes; human impact |
| Oklahoma tallgrass prairie prescribed burns | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; fire ecology |
| Cross Timbers forest ecology | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; habitat diversity |
| Oklahoma Lake Texoma and reservoir ecology | 5, 8 | Life/Earth Science | Water systems; aquatic ecosystems |

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 5 Phenomena:**
- "Oklahoma ranchers burn their prairie grassland every spring. Instead of destroying the ecosystem, the grass grows back thicker and healthier. Why does fire help the prairie?"
- "After a severe windstorm in western Oklahoma, a farmer noticed that the field with no-till crops lost much less topsoil than the plowed field next to it. What explains this?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "Central Oklahoma experiences more tornadoes than almost anywhere else on Earth. What atmospheric conditions make this region uniquely prone to tornado formation?"
- "Oklahoma has experienced a dramatic increase in earthquake frequency since 2009, even though it is not near a tectonic plate boundary. What might explain this increase?"

**Grade 11 Phenomena:**
- "Oklahoma's Anadarko Basin contains some of the deepest oil and gas wells in North America. What geologic processes over hundreds of millions of years created these petroleum deposits?"
- "Scientists studying the Red River watershed found that sediment levels downstream increased after upstream farmland was converted from grassland to row crops. What explains this pattern, and what solutions could reduce the impact?"

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 5, 8, or 11 ONLY
- [ ] Oklahoma science standard is accurate for grade level
- [ ] Item integrates content knowledge with a scientific practice
- [ ] Anchoring phenomenon is authentic and Oklahoma-relevant where possible
- [ ] Stimulus has clear labels, units, and realistic data
- [ ] CR prompt directs students to use evidence from the stimulus
- [ ] All distractors represent real misconceptions
- [ ] MS items specify how many answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] Science content is accurate and reflects current scientific understanding
- [ ] Vocabulary is grade-appropriate

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7 | OSTP Science is only at Grades 5, 8, 11 | Restrict to tested grades |
| Content-only items with no scientific practice | OSTP values practice integration | Embed a scientific practice into item design |
| CR prompts without evidence directive | Students may respond without citing data | Include "Use evidence from the [data/graph/diagram]" |
| Generic Southern Plains phenomena | OSTP favors Oklahoma-specific contexts | Use Tornado Alley, Oklahoma prairie, Red River, oil geology contexts |
| MS items with only 2 options in 4 total | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Data-to-Conclusion (MC, DOK 2)
> "The [graph / table / diagram] below shows [Oklahoma science scenario]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / explanation / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 2: Phenomenon Explanation (CR, DOK 3)
> "Scientists observed [Oklahoma-anchored phenomenon]. Using evidence from the [data / model / diagram], construct an explanation for [specific science focus]. Explain the [cause-and-effect / energy flow / pattern] relationship."

### Pattern 3: Model Evaluation (MC or MS, DOK 2–3)
> "A student made a model showing [Oklahoma science concept]. Which statement BEST describes what the model shows about [content focus]?"

### Pattern 4: Investigation Design (CR, DOK 3)
> "A scientist in Oklahoma wants to investigate whether [variable] affects [outcome] in [Oklahoma ecosystem/setting]. Describe a procedure including what would be measured and how results would be interpreted."

### Pattern 5: Evidence-Based Argument (CR, DOK 3)
> "Two students disagree about [Oklahoma science claim]. Student A says [misconception]. Student B says [correct claim]. Using evidence from the [data / diagram], explain which student is correct and why."

### Pattern 6: Human Impact and Solutions (MS or CR, DOK 2–3)
> "Human activities have [impacted Oklahoma environment]. Select the TWO strategies that BEST [monitor / reduce / address] this impact."
