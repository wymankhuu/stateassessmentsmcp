## 1. OVERVIEW

| Field                | Details                                                                          |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Assessment Name      | SC PASS Science (South Carolina Palmetto Assessment of State Standards)          |
| Administering Agency | South Carolina Department of Education (SC DOE)                                  |
| Grades Covered       | **Grade 4, Grade 6, and Grade 8 ONLY**                                           |
| Testing Window       | April–May (annual summative)                                                     |
| Format               | Computer-based testing (CBT); single session                                     |
| Duration             | Approximately 60–80 minutes                                                      |
| Standards Framework  | South Carolina Science Standards (SC Science Standards)                           |
| Performance Levels   | 4 levels: Does Not Meet, Approaches, Meets, Exceeds                              |
| Vendor               | SC DOE                                                                            |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** SC PASS Science is administered at **Grades 4, 6, and 8 only**. Do not generate SC PASS Science items for Grades 3, 5, or 7. SC PASS is a separate assessment from SC READY (which covers ELA and Math).

> **STANDARDS NOTE:** South Carolina uses its own **SC Science Standards**, which are state-specific. SC did not adopt NGSS. Items should reference SC Science Standards, not NGSS performance expectations.

> **ITEM FORMAT NOTE:** SC PASS Science uses **multiple choice (MC) items only**. There are NO constructed response items, NO multi-select items, and NO technology-enhanced items on SC PASS Science.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label              | Description                                                          |
|-------|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Does Not Meet      | Does not demonstrate expected science knowledge and skills           |
| 2     | Approaches         | Partially demonstrates science knowledge and skills for this grade   |
| 3     | Meets              | Demonstrates the science knowledge and skills expected at grade level|
| 4     | Exceeds            | Demonstrates advanced science knowledge and skills beyond grade level|

## 5. ITEM TYPES

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Single correct answer                                   |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Scenario-based items include data tables, diagrams,     |
|  graphs, and SC-specific phenomena                       |
|  Grades 4, 6, and 8                                      |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 4, DOK 2, 4.L.1 — Organisms and Environments):**

The brown pelican is South Carolina's state bird. Brown pelicans have a large pouch under their bill that they use to scoop fish from the water. How does this physical feature BEST help the pelican survive?

- A. It helps the pelican fly faster over long distances
- B. It allows the pelican to catch and hold food efficiently
- C. It protects the pelican from predators
- D. It helps the pelican stay warm during winter months

*Correct Answer: B*

---

**MC Example (Grade 6, DOK 2, 6.L.4 — Ecosystem Interactions):**

In the Congaree Swamp ecosystem, bald cypress trees provide shade over the water, reducing the water temperature. This cooler water supports certain species of fish and amphibians that cannot survive in warmer conditions. What does this BEST demonstrate?

- A. Organisms can survive in any environment
- B. Non-living factors do not affect living organisms
- C. Changes to one part of an ecosystem can affect other organisms
- D. All organisms in an ecosystem compete for the same resources

*Correct Answer: C*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, 8.P.2 — Forces and Motion):**

A student pushes a cart with a force of 15 Newtons across a flat surface. The cart accelerates at 3 m/s squared. According to Newton's Second Law, what is the mass of the cart?

- A. 5 kg
- B. 12 kg
- C. 18 kg
- D. 45 kg

*Correct Answer: A (F = ma; 15 = m x 3; m = 5 kg)*

**Scenario-Based Example (Grade 4, DOK 2, 4.E.2 — Weather):**

*A student in Charleston recorded the following weather data over 4 days:*

| Day | Temperature (deg F) | Cloud Cover   | Wind Direction |
|-----|---------------------|---------------|----------------|
| 1   | 82                  | Clear         | SW             |
| 2   | 78                  | Partly Cloudy | SE             |
| 3   | 70                  | Overcast      | NE             |
| 4   | 65                  | Overcast      | NE             |

*Based on the data, what weather change is MOST LIKELY occurring?*

- A. A warm front is moving into the area
- B. A cold front is moving through the area
- C. The weather will become warmer the next day
- D. A hurricane is approaching the coast

*Correct Answer: B*

## 7. SC SCIENCE STANDARDS vs. NGSS COMPARISON

This comparison is essential for avoiding the common error of treating SC Science Standards as if they were NGSS.

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature                     | SC Science Standards (USE THIS)              | NGSS (NOT used in SC)                      |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Adopted by SC               | YES — SC-specific standards                  | NO — SC did not adopt NGSS                 |
| Standard Code Example        | 4.L.1, 6.P.3, 8.E.4                        | 4-LS1-1, MS-PS3-1                         |
| Structure                    | Domain + Grade-level indicators              | Performance Expectation with 3 dimensions  |
| Science Practices            | Embedded in inquiry process skills           | Explicit SEPs (8 practices)                |
| Crosscutting Concepts        | Not explicitly labeled as separate dimension | Explicit CCCs (7 concepts)                 |
| Assessment implications      | Content knowledge + inquiry skills           | Three-dimensional assessment               |
| Tested Grades                | 4, 6, 8                                     | Varies by adopting state                   |

> **KEY RULE:** When writing SC PASS Science items, use SC standard codes (e.g., **4.L.1**, **8.P.2**) — never use NGSS PE codes like "4-LS1-1" or "MS-PS3-1."

## 8. SCORING

### Machine-Scored Only

Because SC PASS Science uses only MC items, **there are no rubrics** — all items are machine scored.

| Item Type    | Scoring Method  | Points |
|--------------|-----------------|--------|
| MC           | Machine scored  | 1 pt   |
| CR           | N/A — none used | —      |

> **All-MC Format:** Unlike SC READY (ELA/Math), SC PASS Science does not include constructed response items. All items are four-option multiple choice scored 0 or 1.

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 4 Phenomena:**
- "A student visiting a SC barrier island noticed that the beach on one side was wider than the beach on the other side — what process caused this difference?"
- "After a heavy rainstorm in Charleston, water from the harbor rose and flooded nearby streets — what causes tidal flooding?"
- "Brown pelicans dive from the air into the water to catch fish — what adaptations help them do this successfully?"

**Grade 6 Phenomena:**
- "Scientists studying the Congaree Swamp found that when bald cypress trees were removed from one area, the fish population decreased — how are these organisms connected?"
- "The Lowcountry salt marsh changes dramatically between high and low tide — how do organisms adapt to these changing conditions?"
- "A student noticed that an iron chain left near Charleston harbor rusted faster than one stored indoors — what conditions caused this difference?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "South Carolina's coast has experienced more frequent flooding events in the past two decades — what atmospheric and oceanic factors contribute to this trend?"
- "SC barrier islands shift and change shape over time due to hurricanes and ocean currents — what processes drive these changes?"
- "Lightning strikes are very common in South Carolina during summer — what causes the buildup of electrical charge that produces lightning?"

## 11. QUALITY CHECKLIST AND COMMON PITFALLS

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 4, 6, or 8 ONLY (not other SC grades for PASS Science)
- [ ] Standard code uses SC Science format ([Grade].[Domain].[Standard])
- [ ] No NGSS codes used anywhere in item
- [ ] No Constructed Response items (SC PASS Science is all MC, machine-scored)
- [ ] No Multi-Select items (SC PASS Science is MC only)
- [ ] No Technology-Enhanced items (SC PASS Science is MC only)
- [ ] No Lab Practical component
- [ ] SC-specific phenomenon used in scenario items
- [ ] All MC distractors reflect identifiable student misconceptions
- [ ] Data tables and graphs are clearly labeled with units
- [ ] Science content is accurate and grade-appropriate
- [ ] DOK level matches cognitive demand of item
- [ ] Item avoids vocabulary beyond grade-level expectations
- [ ] Cultural and environmental contexts are authentic to SC

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall                                   | Problem                                           | Fix                                                      |
|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Using NGSS PE codes                       | SC uses SC Science Standards, NOT NGSS            | Use [Grade].[Domain].[Standard] format only              |
| Writing CR items for SC PASS Science      | SC PASS Science is 100% MC, machine-scored        | Convert all items to MC format                            |
| Writing MS or TEI items                   | SC PASS Science uses only MC items                | Use standard 4-option MC format only                     |
| Items for Grades 3, 5, or 7              | SC PASS Science tests only Gr 4, 6, and 8         | Restrict items to Grades 4, 6, and 8                     |
| Generic phenomena (not SC-specific)       | SC items should anchor in real SC contexts         | Use barrier islands, Lowcountry, Congaree, Charleston    |
| Confusing SC PASS with SC READY           | SC PASS = Science; SC READY = ELA and Math         | Use correct assessment name for each subject              |
| Three-dimensional item structure          | SC standards are not structured like NGSS          | Focus on content knowledge + inquiry, not DCI/SEP/CCC    |
| Vocabulary too advanced for grade level   | SC PASS targets grade-appropriate terminology      | Review SC standards for expected vocabulary at each grade |
| Data items without clear labels/units     | Students need labeled data to interpret correctly  | Always include units and clear column/axis labels         |

## 12. PROMPT PATTERNS

The following patterns reflect the most common SC PASS Science item structures.

### Pattern 1: Adaptation and Survival (MC, DOK 2, Life Science)
> "[SC organism] has [specific physical or behavioral trait]. How does this [adaptation / feature] BEST help the organism survive in its environment?"

### Pattern 2: Data Table Interpretation (MC, DOK 2)
> "The table below shows [SC science data collected over time / in an experiment]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / statement / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 3: Cause and Effect — Ecosystem (MC, DOK 2, Life Science)
> "In the [Lowcountry / Congaree / SC coast] ecosystem, [change in one variable] occurred. What would MOST LIKELY be the effect on [another organism or part of the system]?"

### Pattern 4: Earth Process Identification (MC, DOK 1–2, Earth Science)
> "A student observed [erosion / weathering / deposition] at [SC location]. Which process BEST explains the changes the student observed?"

### Pattern 5: Experimental Design and Variables (MC, DOK 2–3)
> "A student designed an experiment to test whether [variable] affects [outcome] in a [SC context]. What is the [independent variable / controlled variable / best improvement to the design]?"

### Pattern 6: Physical/Chemical Change Application (MC, DOK 2, Physical Science)
> "A student observed [physical or chemical change] in a [SC context]. Which statement BEST explains whether a [physical / chemical] change occurred, and WHY?"

### Pattern 7: Weather and Climate Analysis (MC, DOK 2, Earth Science)
> "The [graph / data table] shows weather data recorded in [SC city] over [time period]. Based on the data, what weather pattern is MOST LIKELY occurring?"
