| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **KAP ELA Overview:** The Kansas Assessment Program measures student mastery of the Kansas Academic Standards for ELA. Kansas uses state-specific ELA standards that align closely with college and career readiness expectations. Items are passage-based and assess reading comprehension, writing, and language skills across literary and informational texts.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Level 4** | Student demonstrates thorough understanding of grade-level ELA standards; analyzes complex texts with precision; uses sophisticated evidence-based reasoning |
| 3 | **Level 3** | Student demonstrates adequate understanding; reads and comprehends grade-level texts; uses relevant evidence to support analysis |
| 2 | **Level 2** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; inconsistent text analysis; limited use of evidence |
| 1 | **Level 1** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding; significant gaps in reading comprehension and analysis skills |

## 2. Standards Framework

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RL.1** — Key details & inference | Identify explicit details; make simple inferences | Cite textual evidence; analyze explicit vs. inferential meaning | Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine where text is ambiguous |
| **RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine the lesson or moral; summarize | Analyze theme development across the text | Analyze multiple themes and their interaction within complex texts |
| **RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe character traits, motivations, and feelings | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development |
| **RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; impact of word choice | Analyze impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone |
| **RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's perspective | Analyze how differences in point of view create effects | Analyze how point of view and perspective shape content and style |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RI.1** — Key details & inference | Ask and answer questions; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how key ideas are developed across sections | Analyze complex set of ideas and explain how they interact |
| **RI.6** — Author's purpose & POV | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view or purpose; analyze how it is conveyed | Determine author's point of view in text with multiple perspectives |
| **RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support an author's point | Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims | Evaluate the reasoning and rhetoric of seminal U.S. texts |
| **RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare texts on the same topic | Analyze how different authors present information about the same topic | Analyze and synthesize foundational documents of historical significance |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, examples | 3 |
| **W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5 Informational, DOK 2, RI.5.3):**

*[After reading an article about the history of wheat farming in the Flint Hills region of Kansas]*

Which detail from the article BEST explains why the Flint Hills region became important to Kansas wheat farmers?

A. The Flint Hills receive less rainfall than other areas of Kansas
B. The rocky limestone soil prevented large-scale farming, so ranchers used the grasslands for cattle instead
C. The flat terrain and deep topsoil of nearby regions made wheat farming more practical there
D. The Flint Hills were named after the flint rock found throughout the region

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RI.5.3 | Reading — Informational Text | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–6 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  Full credit only; partial credit not awarded            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 7 Literary, DOK 2, RL.7.3):**

*[After reading a short story about a student who witnesses a tornado while visiting relatives on a Kansas wheat farm]*

Select the TWO details from the story that BEST show how the main character's feelings change from the beginning to the end of the story.

A. "The wheat stretched out forever, golden and boring, just like Grandpa's stories."
B. "She counted the fence posts along the highway, wishing she were back in her city apartment."
C. "Standing in the cellar doorway after the storm passed, she realized the prairie had a kind of strength she had never noticed before."
D. "Uncle Ray turned on the weather radio and frowned at the static."
E. "The sky had been clear when they arrived at the farm that morning."

*Correct Answers: A, C*
*Alignment: RL.7.3 | Reading — Literary Text | DOK 2*

---

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Short written response (3–5 sentences)                  |
|  Must use evidence from the passage                      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 8 Informational, DOK 3, RI.8.8):**

*[After reading an editorial about whether Dodge City should invest in preserving its Old West heritage sites]*

The author argues that preserving Old West heritage sites is essential for Dodge City's future. Explain how the author supports this argument using TWO pieces of evidence from the passage.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Identifies two relevant pieces of evidence from the passage and explains how each supports the author's argument*
- *1 pt: Identifies one piece of evidence with explanation, OR identifies two pieces without adequate explanation*
- *0 pts: No relevant evidence cited or response is off-topic*

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response demonstrates thorough understanding. Uses specific, relevant evidence from the passage. Explanation is complete, accurate, and clearly connected to the question. Analysis shows grade-level reasoning. |
| 1 | Response demonstrates partial understanding. Evidence is present but may be vague, incomplete, or only partially relevant. Explanation is partially developed or contains minor inaccuracies. |
| 0 | Response does not address the question; blank; off-topic; restates the question without analysis; no evidence from the passage. |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Text evidence is required.** Responses that rely on prior knowledge without referencing the passage earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Direct quotes or specific paraphrases** from the passage strengthen a response.
- **Two-evidence items** require both pieces of evidence for full credit (2 points).

## 10. Contextualization — Kansas ELA

When generating passages and prompts, prioritize authentic Kansas settings and culture:

### Kansas Context Guide

| Context | Passage Type | Grade Band |
|---|---|---|
| Kansas wheat farming / harvest season | Informational or Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Dodge City / Old West heritage and history | Informational or Historical fiction | 5–11 |
| Kansas tornadoes / severe weather on the plains | Literary fiction or Informational | 3–8 |
| Flint Hills tallgrass prairie / ecology and preservation | Informational | 4–11 |
| Kansas aviation industry (Wichita — "Air Capital") | Informational | 5–11 |
| Brown v. Board of Education (Topeka) | Informational / Argumentative | 6–11 |
| Kansas frontier settlement / pioneer life | Literary fiction or Informational | 4–8 |
| Kansas wildlife (bison, prairie chickens, sunflowers) | Informational | 3–6 |
| Small-town Kansas community life | Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Kansas state fair / agricultural traditions | Informational or Literary | 3–7 |
| Kansas Route 66 / transportation history | Informational | 5–8 |
| Kansas river systems (Kansas River, Arkansas River) | Informational | 4–8 |

### Suggested Passage Topics by Grade

**Grades 3–5:**
- A student helps with the wheat harvest on a family farm near Salina
- A class field trip to the Flint Hills tallgrass prairie
- The story of how sunflowers became the Kansas state flower

**Grades 6–8:**
- The history and legacy of Brown v. Board of Education in Topeka
- How Wichita became the "Air Capital of the World"
- Surviving a tornado on the Kansas plains — narrative fiction

**Grade 11:**
- An editorial on preserving Dodge City's Old West heritage versus modernization
- Analysis of the economic impact of the aviation industry on Kansas
- The role of Kansas in the national debate over slavery (Bleeding Kansas)

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Kansas Academic Standards code verified for grade level
- [ ] MC items have exactly 4 options with one correct answer
- [ ] MS items specify exact number of answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] CR items require text evidence from the passage
- [ ] CR scored on 0–2 point scale
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] Kansas context incorporated where appropriate
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions
- [ ] DOK level matches actual cognitive demand
- [ ] Grade 11 items reflect appropriate complexity for high school

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| CR prompt not requiring evidence | Students may respond without citing text | Include "Use evidence from the passage" in all CR prompts |
| MS items with fewer than 5 options | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |
| Items that can be answered without reading the passage | Not passage-dependent | Ensure all items require specific passage content |
| Grade 11 items at middle school complexity | Underestimates high school expectations | Use complex texts and analytical prompts for Grade 11 |
| Generic Midwest settings without Kansas specificity | Misses state contextualization | Use Kansas-specific locations, history, and culture |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Key Idea / Main Idea (MC, DOK 2)
> "Which statement BEST expresses the [main idea / central message / theme] of the passage?"

### Pattern 2: Evidence Selection (MS, DOK 2)
> "Select the TWO [details / sentences] from the passage that BEST [support / show / explain] [specific focus]."

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose or Craft (MC, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice] in [paragraph/section] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding]?"

### Pattern 4: Evidence-Based Analysis (CR, DOK 3)
> "[Analytical question]. Use TWO pieces of evidence from the passage to support your response."

### Pattern 5: Vocabulary in Context (MC, DOK 2)
> "As used in paragraph [X], the word [vocabulary word] MOST LIKELY means —"

### Pattern 6: Compare and Contrast (MC or CR, DOK 3)
> "How does the [author's treatment / perspective / information] in [Source 1] differ from [Source 2]? Use evidence from BOTH passages to support your answer."
