## AssessmentForge Skill Guide | Grades 3–8, 11

## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | New Jersey Student Learning Assessments (NJSLA) — English Language Arts/Literacy |
| **Administering Agency** | New Jersey Department of Education (NJDOE) |
| **Vendor** | Cognia |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 |
| **Testing Window** | April – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; multi-session test with three task types |
| **Standards** | New Jersey Student Learning Standards for English Language Arts (NJSLS-ELA) |
| **Score Scale** | 650–850 scale score (varies by grade); 5 performance levels |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **NJSLA and PARCC:** The NJSLA ELA assessment is derived from the **Partnership for Assessment of Readiness for College and Careers (PARCC)** design. Item types, task structures, and rubrics follow the PARCC framework. Key differences from SBAC-based assessments: NJSLA uses **Literary Analysis Task (LAT)**, **Research Simulation Task (RST)**, and **Narrative Writing Task (NWT)** formats. The primary extended-response format is the **Prose Constructed Response (PCR)** scored 0–4 points, rather than the SBAC Full Write (0–12 points across 3 traits).

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Exceeded Expectations** | Student demonstrates distinguished command of the knowledge and skills; advanced analytical reasoning with sophisticated use of textual evidence |
| 4 | **Met Expectations** | Student demonstrates strong command of the knowledge and skills; thorough analysis with effective use of textual evidence |
| 3 | **Approached Expectations** | Student demonstrates moderate command of the knowledge and skills; partial analysis with some use of textual evidence |
| 2 | **Partially Met Expectations** | Student demonstrates partial command of the knowledge and skills; limited analysis with inconsistent evidence use |
| 1 | **Did Not Yet Meet Expectations** | Student demonstrates minimal command of the knowledge and skills; significant gaps in reading and writing |

## 2. Standards Framework

> **Reference:** See `_shared/ccss_ela_framework.md` for full CCR Anchor Standards and grade-by-grade progressions. New Jersey uses the **New Jersey Student Learning Standards for ELA (NJSLS-ELA)**, which are closely aligned to Common Core ELA standards. Standards are coded as "NJSLS.ELA-Literacy" in the NJ system.

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NJSLS.RL.1** — Key details & inference | Explicit details; simple inference from text | Cite textual evidence; analyze what text says explicitly vs. inferentially | Cite strong and thorough evidence; draw complex inferences |
| **NJSLS.RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine message or lesson; summarize | Analyze theme development; how it emerges through characters and plot | Analyze themes across complex texts; evaluate development |
| **NJSLS.RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe characters' traits and responses | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character and advance plot | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development |
| **NJSLS.RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; word choice and tone | Analyze how an author's choices of words create meaning and tone |
| **NJSLS.RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's point of view | Analyze how differences in POV create effects like suspense or humor | Analyze multiple points of view across a text |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NJSLS.RI.1** — Key details & inference | Quote accurately; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **NJSLS.RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how a text makes connections between ideas | Analyze a complex set of ideas or sequence of events |
| **NJSLS.RI.6** — Author's purpose & point of view | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view; analyze how purpose shapes content | Determine author's purpose in a text with multiple purposes |
| **NJSLS.RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support points | Trace and evaluate argument; assess whether reasoning is sound | Evaluate reasoning and evidence in seminal U.S. texts |
| **NJSLS.RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare multiple texts on same topic | Analyze how two texts treat similar topics differently | Analyze foundational U.S. documents of historical significance |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **NJSLS.W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with clear claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **NJSLS.W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, examples | 3 |
| **NJSLS.W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop narrator/characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |
| **NJSLS.W.7** — Research | Conduct short research projects; gather relevant information | 3 |

## 3. Test Structure

NJSLA ELA uses three distinct PARCC-derived task types delivered across multiple sessions.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|            NJSLA ELA STRUCTURE (Grades 3–8, 11)                  |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 1 — Literary Analysis Task (LAT)                           |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  1–2 literary passages (paired or extended)                       |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts)                     |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 2 — Research Simulation Task (RST)                         |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  2–3 informational source documents (articles, data, excerpts)   |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts)                     |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 3 — Narrative Writing Task (NWT)                           |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  1 literary or informational stimulus passage                     |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts) — narrative writing |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Machine-Scored Item Distribution (Per Unit)

| Item Type | Points | Machine Scored | DOK |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected Response (SR) | 1 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Multi-Select Response (MS) | 1 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR) | 2 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Technology Enhanced (TE) | 1–2 | Yes | 2 |

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR) Structure

| Component | Description | Points |
|---|---|---|
| LAT PCR | Analytical essay about literary text(s) | 0–4 pts |
| RST PCR | Analytical/argumentative essay synthesizing research sources | 0–4 pts |
| NWT PCR | Narrative writing extending or responding to stimulus | 0–4 pts |

### Grade-Level Test Specifications

| Grade | LAT Items + PCR | RST Items + PCR | NWT Items + PCR | Total Approx. Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~6–8 items + 1 PCR | ~38–46 |
| 6–8 | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~12–14 items + 1 PCR | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~44–52 |
| 11 | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~12–14 items + 1 PCR | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~44–52 |

## 4. Claims & Blueprint

### Reporting Category Weightings

| Category | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Reading Literary Text | ~30% | Students can read closely and analytically to comprehend literary texts of appropriate complexity |
| Reading Informational Text | ~30% | Students can read closely and analytically to comprehend informational texts of appropriate complexity |
| Writing — Written Expression | ~20% | Students can produce effective writing for a range of purposes and audiences; development, organization, evidence use |
| Writing — Knowledge of Language and Conventions | ~20% | Students demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar, usage, and mechanics |

> **Note:** Unlike SBAC's claim structure, NJSLA reports reading by text type (literary vs. informational) and writing by expression vs. conventions. Research skills are embedded within the RST rather than reported as a separate claim.

### DOK Distribution

| DOK Level | Description | Approximate % |
|---|---|---|
| DOK 1 | Recall; identify; locate | ~5–10% |
| DOK 2 | Infer; explain; apply; analyze surface features | ~50–55% |
| DOK 3 | Analyze craft; evaluate argument; synthesize across texts | ~35–40% |

### Passage Requirements

|---|---|---|---|
| Literary | 420–820L | 925–1185L | 1080–1385L |
| Informational | 420–820L | 925–1185L | 1080–1385L |
| RST Sources | Grade-appropriate band | Grade-appropriate band | Grade-appropriate band |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Selected Response (SR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  SELECTED RESPONSE — 1 point                             |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**SR Example (Grade 6 Informational, DOK 3, NJSLS.RI.6.6):**

*[After reading an article about the restoration of the Pine Barrens ecosystem in southern New Jersey]*

What is the author's MAIN purpose in writing this article?

A. To persuade readers to donate money to environmental programs in New Jersey
B. To entertain readers with stories about camping in the Pine Barrens
C. To inform readers about how conservation efforts have helped restore a unique and fragile New Jersey ecosystem
D. To warn readers about the dangers of wildfires in forested areas

*Correct Answer: C*
*Alignment: NJSLS.ELA-Literacy.RI.6.6 | Reading Informational | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select Response (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–8 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  Full credit only; partial credit not awarded            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 5 Literary, DOK 2, NJSLS.RL.5.3):**

*[After reading a short story about cousins exploring the NJ Shore during a summer storm]*

Select the TWO details from the story that BEST show how the relationship between the cousins changes over the course of the story.

A. "The waves crashed against the jetty with a sound like thunder."
B. "Sofia turned away from Marco when he suggested they leave the boardwalk."
C. "By the time the storm cleared, Sofia had taken Marco's hand, pulling him toward the lighthouse they had always been afraid to visit."
D. "Their grandmother had warned them not to go past the lifeguard station."
E. "The sand felt cold beneath their feet as the wind whipped off the Atlantic."

*Correct Answers: B, C*
*Alignment: NJSLS.ELA-Literacy.RL.5.3 | Reading Literary | DOK 2*

---

### Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EVIDENCE-BASED SELECTED RESPONSE — 2 points            |
|                                                          |
|  Part A: Inference or analysis (1 pt)                   |
|  Part B: Text evidence supporting Part A (1 pt)          |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**EBSR Example (Grade 8 Informational, DOK 3, NJSLS.RI.8.8):**

*[After reading an argumentative essay about whether New Jersey should invest in expanding its commuter rail network along the NJ Turnpike corridor]*

**Part A:** Which statement BEST describes the author's central argument in this essay?

A. Expanding commuter rail would only benefit residents of North Jersey and should be limited to the metropolitan area
B. New Jersey should expand commuter rail because it would reduce highway congestion, lower carbon emissions, and connect underserved communities along the Turnpike corridor
C. The cost of rail expansion makes it an impractical investment for New Jersey at this time
D. Commuter rail systems have failed in other states, and New Jersey should wait to observe those results

**Part B:** Which sentence from the essay BEST supports your answer to Part A?

A. "The construction phase alone would create an estimated 12,000 jobs statewide."
B. "Critics have raised concerns about the initial capital investment required for new track infrastructure."
C. "By connecting Trenton, New Brunswick, and Newark with expanded express service, New Jersey could cut commute times in half for 200,000 daily riders — while taking tens of thousands of cars off the Turnpike each day."
D. "Japan and several European nations offer models of successful commuter rail, though their geography differs from New Jersey's in important ways."

*Correct Answers: Part A: B | Part B: C*
*Alignment: NJSLS.ELA-Literacy.RI.8.8 | Reading Informational | DOK 3*

### Technology Enhanced (TE)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  TECHNOLOGY ENHANCED — 1–2 points                        |
|                                                          |
|  Selectable text, drag-and-drop, or matching             |
|  Interaction directly with passage or stimulus           |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2                                                   |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**TE Example (Grade 5 — Selectable Text, DOK 2):**

*Click on the sentence in paragraph 3 that BEST shows the narrator's feelings about moving away from her neighborhood near the Princeton campus.*

---

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  PROSE CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–4 points                 |
|                                                          |
|  Extended written response to passage(s)                 |
|  LAT: literary analysis essay                            |
|  RST: analytical/argumentative essay from sources        |
|  NWT: narrative writing from stimulus                    |
|  Human scored on a single 0–4 holistic rubric            |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**PCR Prompt Example — RST (Grade 7, Argumentative):**

*[Sources provided: Article 1 on NJ diner culture and its role in local economies; Article 2 on changing food trends affecting small restaurants; Data table on diner closures vs. new restaurant openings in NJ counties]*

You have read three sources about New Jersey diners and their importance to local communities. Write an essay that argues whether communities should take steps to preserve traditional New Jersey diners. Use evidence from at least two of the three sources to support your argument. Be sure to acknowledge a counterclaim.

**PCR Prompt Example — LAT (Grade 6, Literary Analysis):**

*[After reading two short stories about young people growing up near Ellis Island — one set in the early 1900s, one set in the present day]*

Both stories explore the theme of identity and belonging. Write an essay analyzing how each author develops this theme. Use specific details from both texts to support your analysis.

**PCR Prompt Example — NWT (Grade 4, Narrative):**

*[After reading a passage about Thomas Edison's early experiments at his Menlo Park laboratory]*

Write a story about a young inventor who discovers something unexpected while experimenting in a workshop. Use details from the passage about Edison to help you develop your narrative. Be sure to include a clear setting, characters, and a sequence of events.

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR) Rubric — Holistic 0–4 Points

**LAT and RST PCR (Analytical/Argumentative)**

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Demonstrates thorough understanding of the text(s). Skillfully develops a central idea with convincing and well-chosen evidence. Effectively organizes ideas with purposeful, clear coherence. Sophisticated use of language with precise vocabulary. Few or no errors in conventions. |
| 3 | Demonstrates adequate understanding of the text(s). Develops a central idea with sufficient and relevant evidence. Organizes ideas with adequate coherence and logical progression. Appropriate use of language with generally correct vocabulary. Some errors in conventions that do not interfere with meaning. |
| 2 | Demonstrates partial understanding of the text(s). Develops a central idea with limited or loosely connected evidence. Organization is inconsistent; some ideas lack coherence. Simplistic or imprecise language. Errors in conventions may occasionally interfere with meaning. |
| 1 | Demonstrates minimal understanding of the text(s). Central idea is vague or missing. Little or no evidence from the text. Lacks organization or coherence. Limited vocabulary. Frequent errors in conventions that interfere with meaning. |
| 0 | Blank, copied text, entirely off-task, or written in a language other than English. |

**NWT PCR (Narrative)**

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Effectively establishes and develops a real or imagined experience. Clear narrative arc with purposeful sequence of events. Well-developed characters and setting using dialogue, description, and pacing. Precise and vivid language. Few or no errors in conventions. |
| 3 | Adequately establishes and develops a real or imagined experience. Logical sequence of events with adequate character and setting development. Uses some narrative techniques effectively. Generally appropriate language. Some errors in conventions. |
| 2 | Partially establishes and develops an experience. Inconsistent sequence of events. Limited character or setting development. Basic or repetitive language. Errors in conventions may interfere with meaning. |
| 1 | Minimally establishes an experience. Little to no narrative structure. Undeveloped characters and setting. Limited vocabulary. Frequent errors in conventions. |
| 0 | Blank, copied text, entirely off-task, or written in a language other than English. |

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] NJSLS standard code verified (NJSLS.ELA-Literacy prefix)
- [ ] Task type identified: LAT, RST, or NWT
- [ ] EBSR Part B is a quote that directly supports Part A (not just same topic)
- [ ] MS items specify exact number of answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] PCR prompt is open-ended enough for student voice
- [ ] PCR scored on holistic 0–4 rubric (not 3-trait rubric)
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] RST sources include at least one non-print source (graph, data table, image)
- [ ] New Jersey context incorporated where appropriate (geography, ecology, culture, history)
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions (not trivially wrong)
- [ ] DOK level matches the actual cognitive demand of the question

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| EBSR Part B that supports a different answer | Part B must directly support Part A's correct answer | Cross-check: does Part B quote only make sense given Part A answer? |
| Using SBAC Full Write (0–12) instead of PCR (0–4) | NJSLA uses PARCC-derived holistic 0–4 PCR rubric | Score all PCR items 0–4 on holistic rubric |
| Mixing up task types | LAT = literary analysis; RST = research/argument; NWT = narrative | Match prompt type to correct task structure |
| PCR prompts with only one correct interpretation | Essay needs room for student perspective | Keep prompts debatable or open-ended |
| RST sources all the same format (three articles) | NJSLA RST includes varied source types | Include data tables, graphs, or images as at least one RST source |
| Missing Grade 11 | NJSLA tests Grade 11, unlike some other states | Include Grade 11 in all planning |
| Using CCSS codes without NJSLS prefix | NJ uses NJSLS prefix | Use NJSLS.ELA-Literacy for all standard codes |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Central Idea / Theme (SR or EBSR, DOK 2–3)
> "What [central idea / theme] does the [passage / author] develop? Which detail from the text BEST supports this [idea / theme]?"

### Pattern 2: Author's Craft (SR, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice / point of view] in [this section / paragraph #] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding of ____]?"

### Pattern 3: Evidence Selection (EBSR Part B)
> "Which [sentence / detail] from the [passage] BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 4: Multi-Source Argument — RST (PCR, DOK 4)
> "Using evidence from at least two of the three sources, write an [argumentative / analytical] essay about [NJ-relevant topic]. Be sure to [acknowledge a counterclaim / support your analysis with specific evidence]."

### Pattern 5: Literary Analysis — LAT (PCR, DOK 4)
> "Both texts explore [shared theme or idea]. Write an essay analyzing how each author develops [theme / central idea]. Use specific details from both texts."

### Pattern 6: Narrative Writing — NWT (PCR, DOK 3–4)
> "Using what you have learned from reading [stimulus passage], write a [story / narrative] about [scenario related to stimulus]. Develop your narrative with a clear setting, characters, and sequence of events."

### Pattern 7: Multi-Select Evidence (MS, DOK 2)
> "Select the TWO [details / sentences / examples] from the text that BEST [support / show / explain] [specific focus]."

### Pattern 8: Compare Texts (SR or EBSR, DOK 3)
> "Both sources discuss [common topic]. How does [Source 1] treat this topic differently from [Source 2]?"
