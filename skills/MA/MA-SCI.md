## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | MCAS — Science and Technology/Engineering (STE) |
| **Administering Agency** | Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) |
| **Vendor** | Cognia |
| **Grades Tested** | Grade 5, Grade 8, High School (Biology, Chemistry, Introductory Physics, or Technology/Engineering) |
| **Testing Window** | February (HS); April–May (Grades 5, 8) |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based (CBT); paper accommodations available |
| **Standards** | 2016 Massachusetts Science and Technology/Engineering Curriculum Framework (STE Framework) |
| **Score Scale** | Scale score; 4 performance levels |
| **High Stakes** | One HS STE MCAS is a **graduation requirement** (Competency Determination) |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** Massachusetts uses its own **STE Curriculum Framework (2016)**, which is influenced by NGSS but is NOT identical to NGSS. The Massachusetts framework retains technology/engineering as a distinct discipline and includes Massachusetts-specific standards. Standard codes use the format [Grade Band]-[Domain]-[#] (e.g., 5-LS1-1). HS STE MCAS is a graduation requirement — students must pass one HS STE exam.

> **STE vs. Science:** Massachusetts calls this subject area "Science and Technology/Engineering" (STE), not just "Science." The assessment includes technology/engineering design standards alongside traditional science content.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not Meeting Expectations** | Student does not demonstrate understanding of grade-level STE standards |
| 2 | **Partially Meeting Expectations** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; approaching expectations |
| 3 | **Meeting Expectations** | Student demonstrates grade-level proficiency in STE standards |
| 4 | **Exceeding Expectations** | Student demonstrates advanced, sophisticated understanding beyond expectations |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Phenomenon or stimulus anchored                         |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 1–2                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, 5-ESS2-1):**

Scientists studying Cape Cod noticed that during a severe winter storm, large amounts of sand were removed from the outer beach and deposited in a sandbar offshore. Which TWO Earth systems are MOST directly interacting in this process?

A. Geosphere and hydrosphere
B. Biosphere and atmosphere
C. Atmosphere and geosphere
D. Hydrosphere and biosphere

*Correct Answer: A*
*Alignment: 5-ESS2-1 | SEP: Developing Models | CCC: Systems and System Models | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, MS-LS2-4):**

After a pollution event in Boston Harbor, scientists observed that the population of blue mussels declined sharply. Which of the following would MOST LIKELY be a secondary effect of this decline on the harbor ecosystem?

A. The water temperature would increase significantly
B. Populations of organisms that feed on mussels would also decline
C. New species of mussels would immediately replace the blue mussels
D. The amount of dissolved oxygen in the water would increase

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: MS-LS2-4 | SEP: Constructing Explanations | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

---

### Open Response (OR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  OPEN RESPONSE (OR) — 0–4 points                        |
|                                                          |
|  Multi-part written response (5–10 sentences)            |
|  Must reference data, evidence, or scientific concepts   |
|  Typically Part A / Part B structure                     |
|  Human scored using OR rubric                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**OR Example (Grade 5, DOK 3, 5-ESS3-1):**

*A town near Cape Cod relies on underground wells for drinking water. Scientists discovered that the groundwater level has been dropping over the past 10 years. Data shows that the town's population grew by 25% during the same period, and average annual rainfall decreased slightly.*

Part A: Using evidence from the scenario, explain TWO possible causes for the decline in groundwater levels. Use scientific reasoning in your response.

Part B: Propose ONE action the community could take to reduce its impact on the groundwater supply. Explain how this action would help, using scientific concepts.

---

**OR Example (Grade 8, DOK 3, MS-ESS2-2):**

*Students on a field trip to the Massachusetts coast observed that the cliffs at Gay Head (Aquinnah) on Martha's Vineyard display distinct layers of red, orange, and white clay and sand. A geologist explained that these layers represent millions of years of geological history.*

Part A: Using your knowledge of geoscience processes, explain how these distinct layers of sediment were formed. Describe at least TWO processes involved.

Part B: The cliffs have been getting noticeably smaller over the past century. Explain what process is causing this change and predict what the cliffs might look like in 200 years if this process continues.

## 7. Three-Dimensional Design

Massachusetts STE standards integrate three dimensions, similar to NGSS. All items should reflect at least two dimensions.

### Three Dimensions

| Dimension | What It Is | Example in MCAS STE |
|---|---|---|
| **Disciplinary Core Ideas (DCIs)** | Content knowledge | Properties of matter; ecosystem dynamics; Cape Cod geology; heredity |
| **Science and Engineering Practices (SEPs)** | How students do science | Analyze Boston Harbor data; design an investigation; construct an explanation for coastal erosion |
| **Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs)** | Themes bridging disciplines | Cause and effect; systems; energy and matter; patterns; stability and change |

### Crosscutting Concepts Emphasized

| CCC | Grade 5 | Grade 8 | HS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patterns | Seasonal weather; organism traits | Population trends; periodic properties | Mathematical patterns in data |
| Cause and Effect | Erosion; food web disruptions | Predator-prey; chemical reactions | Human impact on ecosystems |
| Energy and Matter | Food web energy flow; water cycle | Energy transformations; matter cycling | Ecosystem energy balance |
| Systems and System Models | Earth systems; watersheds | Ecosystems; water cycle | Complex ecosystems |
| Stability and Change | Erosion/deposition | Population dynamics; geological change | Ecosystem resilience |

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Multiple Choice (MC) Scoring

| Item Type | Scoring Method | Points |
|---|---|---|
| MC | Machine scored | 1 pt |

### Open Response (OR) Rubric — 0–4 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Fully addresses all parts of the prompt. Demonstrates thorough understanding of the scientific concepts. Uses specific, relevant evidence from the stimulus or scientific principles. Explanation is accurate, complete, and well-organized. |
| 3 | Addresses all parts with mostly accurate content. Evidence is present but may be slightly incomplete. Solid reasoning with minor gaps in explanation. |
| 2 | Addresses some parts. Partial understanding of the scientific concepts. Evidence is vague or loosely connected to the explanation. |
| 1 | Minimal response. Limited understanding. Little or no evidence from the stimulus. Contains significant scientific inaccuracies. |
| 0 | Blank, off-topic, or restates the stimulus without explanation. |

## 10. Contextualization — Massachusetts STE

Massachusetts offers rich, authentic phenomena for STE assessment items rooted in the state's unique geology, ecology, and environmental history.

### MA STE Context Guide

| Context / Phenomenon | Grade | Domain | Standard Alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cape Cod geology (glacial origins, barrier beaches, erosion) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | 5-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2 |
| Massachusetts coastal ecosystems (salt marshes, tidal pools, marine life) | 5, 8, HS | Life Science | 5-LS2-1, MS-LS2-4, HS-LS2-6 |
| Boston Harbor cleanup (environmental remediation, water quality data) | 8, HS | Life/Earth/Tech | MS-ESS3-3, HS-ESS3-4, MS-LS2-4 |
| Cape Cod National Seashore (barrier beach dynamics, dune ecology) | 5, 8 | Earth/Life | 5-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2 |
| Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket geology (glacial deposits, clay cliffs) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | 5-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2 |
| Massachusetts freshwater ecosystems (ponds, reservoirs, Quabbin) | 5, 8 | Life/Earth | 5-LS2-1, MS-LS2-1 |
| North Atlantic right whales off Massachusetts coast | 5, 8, HS | Life Science | 5-LS2-1, MS-LS2-4, HS-LS4-5 |
| Massachusetts winter weather and Nor'easters | 5, 8 | Earth Science | 5-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2 |
| Plum Island and salt marsh ecology | 5, 8, HS | Life Science | 5-LS2-1, MS-LS2-4 |
| Road salt impact on Massachusetts freshwater ecosystems | 8, HS | Physical/Life | MS-PS1-2, MS-LS2-4 |

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 5 Phenomena:**
- "During a visit to Cape Cod, students noticed that the beach on the ocean side was steep and rocky, but the beach on the bay side was flat and sandy. What processes caused these differences?"
- "Scientists studying a pond near Walden discovered that after a nearby road was salted in winter, the water chemistry changed and some aquatic plants disappeared. How are these events connected?"
- "A student observed that cranberry bogs in Plymouth flood in autumn but drain in spring. How does water interact with the land to support cranberry farming?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "The Gay Head Cliffs on Martha's Vineyard show layers of red, orange, and white sediment. What geological processes created these layers, and why are the cliffs shrinking?"
- "After decades of pollution, Boston Harbor underwent a massive cleanup. Water quality data shows dramatic improvement since 1990, and marine species have returned. What caused these changes?"
- "Scientists found that rising ocean temperatures off Cape Cod are causing lobster populations to shift northward. How do environmental changes affect organism populations?"

**HS Phenomena:**
- "Harmful algal blooms have increased in frequency in Massachusetts coastal waters. Using water quality data, evaluate the factors contributing to these blooms and propose an evidence-based mitigation strategy."
- "North Atlantic right whale populations have declined despite protection efforts. Evaluate the evidence for multiple contributing factors and assess the effectiveness of current conservation strategies."

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as **5, 8, or HS only** (not other grades)
- [ ] Standard code uses Massachusetts STE Framework codes (not raw NGSS PEs)
- [ ] Item is three-dimensional: integrates DCI with at least one SEP and one CCC
- [ ] Massachusetts phenomenon used in stimulus where possible
- [ ] MC has exactly 4 options with 1 correct answer
- [ ] OR prompt uses Part A / Part B structure
- [ ] OR prompt directs students to use evidence from the stimulus
- [ ] Technology/Engineering standards included (~17% of items)
- [ ] Science content is accurate and grade-appropriate
- [ ] DOK level matches cognitive demand
- [ ] OR items scored on 0–4 rubric (not other scales)
- [ ] STE (not just "Science") terminology used throughout

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Using raw NGSS codes | Massachusetts uses its own STE Framework codes | Use MA STE codes |
| Omitting Technology/Engineering | TE is ~17% of the test | Include engineering design items |
| Items for non-tested grades | MCAS STE tests only Gr 5, 8, and HS | Restrict to tested grades |
| Calling it "Science" only | Massachusetts calls it STE (Science and Technology/Engineering) | Use STE terminology |
| One-dimensional items | Massachusetts requires three-dimensional learning | Embed SEPs and CCCs alongside DCIs |
| OR without evidence directive | Students must cite stimulus data | Include "Using evidence from..." in all OR prompts |
| Generic coastal phenomena | Use Massachusetts-specific locations | Use Cape Cod, Boston Harbor, Martha's Vineyard, etc. |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Phenomenon Explanation (MC, DOK 2)
> "Scientists observed [Massachusetts phenomenon]. Which explanation BEST describes why this occurred?"

### Pattern 2: Data Interpretation (MC, DOK 2)
> "The table/graph shows [Massachusetts STE data]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 3: Cause and Effect — Ecosystem (MC or OR, DOK 2–3)
> "In the [Cape Cod / Boston Harbor / Massachusetts coastal] ecosystem, [change in one variable] occurred. What would MOST LIKELY be the effect on [another part of the system]?"

### Pattern 4: Scientific Explanation — OR (DOK 3)
> "Part A: Using evidence from the [stimulus], explain [science concept] and the cause-and-effect relationship. Part B: Predict what would happen if [variable changed]. Use scientific reasoning."

### Pattern 5: Engineering Design (MC or OR, DOK 2–3)
> "A community in Massachusetts needs to [solve environmental problem]. [Design constraints given]. Which design solution BEST meets the criteria? / Propose and evaluate a design solution."

### Pattern 6: Investigation Design (OR, DOK 3)
> "A student wants to investigate whether [variable] affects [outcome] in a [Massachusetts context]. Part A: Describe a procedure. Part B: Predict results and explain reasoning."
