## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | MCAS (Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System) — Mathematics |
| **Administering Agency** | Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) |
| **Vendor** | Cognia (formerly Measured Progress / Questar) |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 |
| **Testing Window** | March (Grade 10); April–May (Grades 3–8) |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based (CBT); paper accommodations available |
| **Standards** | 2017 Massachusetts Curriculum Frameworks for Mathematics |
| **Score Scale** | Scale score; 4 performance levels |
| **High Stakes** | Grade 10 MCAS Math is a **graduation requirement** (Competency Determination) |
| **Calculator Policy** | No calculator Grades 3–4; scientific calculator Grades 5–8; graphing calculator Grade 10 |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **MCAS Mathematics** is one of the most rigorous state math assessments in the nation. Massachusetts adopted its own Curriculum Frameworks in 2017, which incorporate CCSS-Math with Massachusetts-specific enhancements including additional modeling standards, financial literacy applications, and higher expectations for Grade 10. Items emphasize conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, and real-world application.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not Meeting Expectations** | Student does not demonstrate understanding of grade-level mathematics standards |
| 2 | **Partially Meeting Expectations** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; approaching grade-level expectations |
| 3 | **Meeting Expectations** | Student demonstrates grade-level proficiency in the mathematics standards |
| 4 | **Exceeding Expectations** | Student demonstrates advanced, sophisticated understanding beyond grade-level expectations |

> **Item-writing target:** "Meeting Expectations" performance. Massachusetts demands rigorous, multi-step reasoning at all grade levels.

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Single correct answer                                   |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Both sessions                                           |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 6, DOK 2, MA.Math.6.RP.3 — Ratios):**

During the Boston Marathon, a runner completed the first 13.1 miles in 1.5 hours. At this constant rate, what is the runner's speed in miles per hour, rounded to the nearest tenth?

A. 8.7 mph
B. 9.5 mph
C. 10.3 mph
D. 11.0 mph

*Correct Answer: A (13.1 / 1.5 = 8.73... ≈ 8.7)*
*Alignment: MA.Math.6.RP.3 | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, MA.Math.8.F.4 — Functions):**

The table below shows the number of visitors to Cape Cod National Seashore over several summer weeks:

| Week (x) | Visitors (y) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2,400 |
| 2 | 2,850 |
| 3 | 3,300 |
| 4 | 3,750 |

Which equation BEST represents the relationship between week number and visitors?

A. y = 450x + 2,400
B. y = 450x + 1,950
C. y = 2,400x + 450
D. y = 1,950x + 450

*Correct Answer: B (slope = 450; y-intercept: when x=1, y=2400 so 450(1)+b=2400, b=1950)*
*Alignment: MA.Math.8.F.4 | DOK 2*

**SA Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, MA.Math.5.NF.1):**

A fishing boat off the coast of Cape Cod caught 3/4 of a ton of cod in the morning and 2/3 of a ton in the afternoon. What is the total weight of cod caught? Show your answer as a fraction or mixed number in simplest form.

*Correct Answer: 17/12 or 1 5/12 tons*

### Open Response (OR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  OPEN RESPONSE (OR) — 0–4 points                        |
|                                                          |
|  Multi-part problem or rich application                  |
|  Requires show work AND written explanation              |
|  Higher DOK — strategic thinking, justify reasoning      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  Session 2 only                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**OR Example (Grade 7, DOK 3, MA.Math.7.RP.2 + MA.Math.7.SP.5):**

The Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) tracks daily ridership on two bus routes:

| Month | Route A Riders | Route B Riders |
|---|---|---|
| January | 4,200 | 3,800 |
| February | 4,350 | 3,950 |
| March | 4,500 | 4,100 |
| April | 4,650 | 4,250 |

*Part A:* Which route has a greater monthly increase in ridership? Show your work.

*Part B:* If both routes continue at their current rate of increase, during which month will Route B first have more riders than Route A? Show your work and explain your reasoning.

*Part C:* A transit planner says "At these rates, Route B will eventually have double the riders of Route A." Is this claim reasonable? Use mathematics to justify your answer.

## 7. Mathematical Practices

The Massachusetts Curriculum Frameworks integrate the **Standards for Mathematical Practice (SMP)**, which should be reflected across all item types.

| SMP | Practice | Assessment Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make sense of problems; persevere in solving | Multi-step OR items; complex word problems |
| 2 | Reason abstractly and quantitatively | Contextual problems requiring decontextualization (e.g., Marathon data to equations) |
| 3 | Construct viable arguments; critique reasoning | OR items requiring explanation or error analysis |
| 4 | Model with mathematics | Real-world Massachusetts contexts (Marathon, MBTA, fishing industry) |
| 5 | Use appropriate tools strategically | Calculator decisions; reference sheet usage |
| 6 | Attend to precision | Labeling units; exact vs. approximate answers; rounding correctly |
| 7 | Look for and make use of structure | Pattern recognition; algebraic reasoning |
| 8 | Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning | Function tables; generalization items |

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Short Answer (SA) Scoring

| SA Type | Points | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Numeric entry (0–1) | 1 | Correct numeric answer; exact match or acceptable equivalent |
| | 0 | Incorrect or blank |
| Brief work (0–2) | 2 | Correct answer with appropriate work shown |
| | 1 | Correct approach but computational error; OR correct answer without work |
| | 0 | Incorrect approach; blank |

### Open Response (OR) Rubric — 0–4 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | All parts correctly and completely addressed. Mathematical work is thorough, clearly shown, and leads to correct answers. Written explanations are mathematically precise and demonstrate strong conceptual understanding. |
| 3 | Most parts correctly addressed. Work is mostly correct with minor computational errors. Explanations demonstrate solid understanding. One part may be slightly incomplete. |
| 2 | Some parts correctly addressed. Work shows a generally correct approach but contains errors. Demonstrates partial understanding. At least one part is correct. |
| 1 | Minimal correct work shown. Limited understanding demonstrated. Multiple significant errors or only a small portion addressed. |
| 0 | No correct mathematical work; blank or completely off-task. |

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Standard code uses MA.Math format (not raw CCSS codes)
- [ ] Calculator policy matches grade band (none for 3–4; scientific for 5–8; graphing for 10)
- [ ] OR items include "Show your work" and "Explain your reasoning" directives
- [ ] OR items have at least two parts (Part A and Part B minimum)
- [ ] Real-world context is authentic and Massachusetts-specific where appropriate
- [ ] All numerical values produce clean, non-ambiguous computations
- [ ] Distractors for MC reflect specific, identifiable student error types
- [ ] DOK level is verified against item cognitive demand
- [ ] Units are clearly specified and labels required where appropriate
- [ ] Grade 10 items reflect graduation-requirement rigor
- [ ] Item type matches correct point value (MC=1, SA=0–1 or 0–2, OR=0–4)

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Using CCSS codes without MA prefix | Massachusetts uses MA.Math codes | Always use MA.Math prefix |
| OR with only one part | MCAS OR requires multi-part structure | Ensure Part A + Part B minimum |
| Underestimating rigor | MCAS is one of the most rigorous state math assessments | Set high expectations for multi-step reasoning |
| Generic context | MCAS values authentic Massachusetts settings | Use Boston Marathon, MBTA, Cape Cod, fishing data |
| Ignoring Grade 10 | Grade 10 is a graduation requirement | Include Grade 10 with HS-level content |
| OR explanation prompt missing | DOK 3 requires justification component | Add "Explain your reasoning" to OR prompts |
| SA items with ambiguous answers | SA answers must be exact and unambiguous | Design problems with clean answers |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Contextual Computation (MC, DOK 2)
> "[Massachusetts context]. [Student/person] needs to find [quantity]. What is [quantity]?"
> (Options: correct answer + three error-type distractors)

### Pattern 2: Show Work — Application (SA, DOK 2)
> "A [Massachusetts setting] has [given information]. [Question requiring computation]. Show your work or enter your answer."

### Pattern 3: Multi-Part OR with Explanation (OR, DOK 3)
> "[Massachusetts data scenario with table or graph].
> Part A: [Computational or procedural task]. Show your work.
> Part B: [Reasoning or justification task]. Use mathematical evidence to support your answer."

### Pattern 4: Error Analysis (OR or MC, DOK 3)
> "[Student name] solved the following problem and got [incorrect answer]. [Student's work shown]. Explain the error [student] made and find the correct answer."

### Pattern 5: Comparison and Justification (OR, DOK 3)
> "Two [Massachusetts contexts] are described. [Data for each]. Which [option] is [better/greater/more efficient]? Use mathematical evidence to justify your answer."

### Pattern 6: Data Interpretation (MC or SA, DOK 2)
> "[Data table or graph from Massachusetts context]. Based on the data, which [statement / conclusion / value] is correct?"
