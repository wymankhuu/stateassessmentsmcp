## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Iowa Statewide Assessment of Student Progress (ISASP) — English Language Arts |
| **Administering Agency** | Iowa Department of Education |
| **Vendor** | IA State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 |
| **Testing Window** | March – April |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based |
| **Standards** | Iowa Core Standards for English Language Arts |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **ISASP ELA Overview:** The Iowa Statewide Assessment of Student Progress measures student mastery of the Iowa Core Standards for English Language Arts. Items are passage-based and assess reading comprehension, writing, and language skills across literary and informational texts. Iowa's standards emphasize evidence-based analysis, critical thinking, and engagement with diverse texts.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Student demonstrates thorough and sophisticated understanding of grade-level ELA standards; analyzes complex texts with precision and depth |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Student demonstrates adequate understanding; reads and comprehends grade-level texts; uses relevant evidence to support analysis |
| 2 | **Progressing** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; inconsistent text analysis; limited use of evidence |
| 1 | **Beginning** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding; significant gaps in reading comprehension and analysis skills |

## 2. Standards Framework

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RL.1** — Key details & inference | Identify explicit details; make simple inferences | Cite textual evidence; analyze explicit vs. inferential meaning | Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine where text is ambiguous |
| **RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine the lesson, moral, or central message; summarize | Analyze theme development across the text | Analyze multiple themes and their complex interplay within texts |
| **RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe character traits, motivations, and feelings | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development |
| **RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; impact of word choice | Analyze impact of specific word choices on meaning, tone, and aesthetic quality |
| **RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's perspective | Analyze how differences in point of view create effects | Analyze how point of view and perspective shape content and style |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RI.1** — Key details & inference | Ask and answer questions; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how key ideas are developed across sections | Analyze complex set of ideas and explain how they interact |
| **RI.6** — Author's purpose & POV | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view or purpose; analyze how it is conveyed | Determine author's point of view in text with multiple perspectives |
| **RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support an author's point | Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims | Evaluate the reasoning and rhetoric in seminal texts |
| **RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare texts on the same topic | Analyze how different authors present information | Analyze and synthesize foundational documents |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, examples | 3 |
| **W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5 Informational, DOK 2, RI.5.3):**

*[After reading an article about Iowa corn farming and its role in the U.S. food supply]*

Which detail from the article BEST explains why Iowa is one of the top corn-producing states in the nation?

A. Iowa has a state fair that celebrates agriculture every summer
B. Iowa's rich prairie topsoil and temperate growing season provide ideal conditions for corn
C. Corn was first grown by Native Americans thousands of years ago
D. Iowa is located in the central part of the United States

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RI.5.3 | Reading — Informational Text | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–6 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  Full credit only; partial credit not awarded            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 7 Literary, DOK 2, RL.7.3):**

*[After reading a short story about a student who moves from a city to a small Iowa farming community]*

Select the TWO details from the story that BEST show how the narrator's feelings about Iowa change from the beginning to the end of the story.

A. "The fields stretched out forever in every direction, flat and empty, and I missed the skyline of buildings back home."
B. "The school had only two hundred students in the entire building."
C. "When the first spring thaw came and the land turned green, I understood why my parents called this place beautiful."
D. "Our neighbor brought over a casserole the day we moved in."
E. "The nearest movie theater was thirty miles away."

*Correct Answers: A, C*
*Alignment: RL.7.3 | Reading — Literary Text | DOK 2*

---

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Short written response (3–5 sentences)                  |
|  Must use evidence from the passage                      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 8 Informational, DOK 3, RI.8.8):**

*[After reading an article about the Iowa caucuses and their role in American presidential elections]*

The author argues that the Iowa caucuses play a unique and important role in the presidential election process. Explain how the author supports this argument using TWO pieces of evidence from the passage.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Identifies two relevant pieces of evidence and explains how each supports the author's argument*
- *1 pt: One piece of evidence with explanation, OR two pieces without adequate explanation*
- *0 pts: No relevant evidence cited or response is off-topic*

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response demonstrates thorough understanding. Uses specific, relevant evidence from the passage. Explanation is complete, accurate, and clearly connected to the question. Analysis shows grade-level reasoning. |
| 1 | Response demonstrates partial understanding. Evidence is present but may be vague, incomplete, or only partially relevant. Explanation is partially developed or contains minor inaccuracies. |
| 0 | Response does not address the question; blank; off-topic; restates the question without analysis; no evidence from the passage. |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Text evidence is required.** Responses that rely on prior knowledge without referencing the passage earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Direct quotes or specific paraphrases** from the passage strengthen a response.
- **Two-evidence items** require both pieces of evidence for full credit (2 points).

## 10. Contextualization — Iowa ELA

When generating passages and prompts, prioritize authentic Iowa settings and culture:

### Iowa Context Guide

| Context | Passage Type | Grade Band |
|---|---|---|
| Iowa corn and soybean farming / harvest season | Informational or Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Iowa caucuses and their role in American democracy | Informational / Argumentative | 6–11 |
| Des Moines River and Iowa waterways | Informational or Literary | 4–8 |
| Loess Hills of western Iowa | Informational or Literary fiction | 4–11 |
| Iowa prairie restoration and conservation | Informational | 5–11 |
| Iowa state fair and agricultural traditions | Informational or Literary | 3–7 |
| Iowa small-town community life | Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Iowa wind energy and renewable technology | Informational | 5–11 |
| Iowa immigration history (European, Latino, refugee communities) | Informational or Literary | 5–11 |
| Iowa literary heritage (Hamlin Garland, Grant Wood's legacy) | Literary or Informational | 6–11 |
| Herbert Hoover birthplace / Iowa historical figures | Informational | 4–8 |
| Effigy Mounds National Monument | Informational or Literary | 4–8 |

### Suggested Passage Topics by Grade

**Grades 3–5:**
- A student helps with corn harvest on a family farm near Des Moines
- An informational passage about the Iowa state fair and what it means to Iowa communities
- The story of how the Loess Hills formed from ancient wind-blown soil

**Grades 6–8:**
- The history and significance of the Iowa caucuses in American politics
- How Iowa's prairie lands were transformed by farming and why restoration matters
- A narrative about a student exploring Effigy Mounds and learning about Native American history

**Grade 11:**
- An editorial debating the future of Iowa agriculture: corn ethanol vs. prairie restoration
- Analysis of Grant Wood's artistic vision and how Iowa's landscape shaped American art
- Paired texts comparing perspectives on Iowa's role in renewable energy and food production

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Iowa Core Standards code verified for grade level
- [ ] MC items have exactly 4 options with one correct answer
- [ ] MS items specify exact number of answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] CR items require text evidence from the passage
- [ ] CR scored on 0–2 point scale
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] Iowa context incorporated where appropriate
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions
- [ ] DOK level matches actual cognitive demand
- [ ] Grade 11 items reflect appropriate complexity for high school

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| CR prompt not requiring evidence | Students may respond without citing text | Include "Use evidence from the passage" in all CR prompts |
| MS items with fewer than 5 options | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |
| Items that can be answered without reading the passage | Not passage-dependent | Ensure all items require specific passage content |
| Grade 11 items at middle school complexity | Underestimates high school expectations | Use complex texts and analytical prompts for Grade 11 |
| Generic Midwest settings without Iowa specificity | Misses state contextualization | Use Iowa-specific locations, history, and culture |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Key Idea / Main Idea (MC, DOK 2)
> "Which statement BEST expresses the [main idea / central message / theme] of the passage?"

### Pattern 2: Evidence Selection (MS, DOK 2)
> "Select the TWO [details / sentences] from the passage that BEST [support / show / explain] [specific focus]."

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose or Craft (MC, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice] in [paragraph/section] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding]?"

### Pattern 4: Evidence-Based Analysis (CR, DOK 3)
> "[Analytical question]. Use TWO pieces of evidence from the passage to support your response."

### Pattern 5: Vocabulary in Context (MC, DOK 2)
> "As used in paragraph [X], the word [vocabulary word] MOST LIKELY means —"

### Pattern 6: Compare and Contrast (MC or CR, DOK 3)
> "How does the [author's treatment / perspective / information] in [Source 1] differ from [Source 2]? Use evidence from BOTH passages to support your answer."
