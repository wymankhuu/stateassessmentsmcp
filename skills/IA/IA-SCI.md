## AssessmentForge Skill Guide | Grades 5, 8, and 11

## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Iowa Statewide Assessment of Student Progress (ISASP) — Science |
| **Administering Agency** | Iowa Department of Education |
| **Vendor** | IA State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | **Grade 5**, **Grade 8**, and **Grade 11** |
| **Testing Window** | March – April |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; single session |
| **Standards** | Iowa Core Standards for Science |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** ISASP Science is administered **only at Grades 5, 8, and 11**. Do not generate science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7.

> **Iowa Science Standards:** Iowa science standards integrate disciplinary core ideas with scientific and engineering practices and crosscutting concepts. Items should assess content knowledge alongside inquiry skills and reasoning.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Advanced** | Demonstrates thorough science understanding; expertly integrates scientific knowledge with practices; constructs sophisticated evidence-based explanations |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Demonstrates adequate science understanding; appropriately applies scientific practices and concepts |
| 2 | **Progressing** | Demonstrates partial understanding; applies content knowledge but struggles to integrate practices meaningfully |
| 1 | **Beginning** | Demonstrates minimal science understanding; limited to recall of isolated facts |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Scenario, diagram, data table, or model stimulus        |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2 — Ecosystem Energy Flow):**

A diagram shows an Iowa tallgrass prairie food chain: Sunlight -> Big Bluestem Grass -> Prairie Voles -> Red-tailed Hawks -> Decomposers

Which statement BEST explains the role of big bluestem grass in this Iowa prairie ecosystem?

A. Big bluestem grass receives energy from prairie voles through the food chain
B. Big bluestem grass captures energy from the sun and converts it into food stored in its cells
C. Big bluestem grass is a decomposer that breaks down dead organisms in the prairie
D. Big bluestem grass gets most of its energy from nutrients in the rich Iowa topsoil

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: Life Science — Ecosystems | Practice: Developing and Using Models | CCC: Energy and Matter | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Earth's Surface / Loess Hills Geology):**

The Loess Hills of western Iowa are made of fine, wind-deposited silt that accumulated over thousands of years during the last Ice Age. Which statement BEST explains how the Loess Hills formed?

A. Volcanic eruptions deposited ash that hardened into the hills
B. The Mississippi River deposited sediment that was then pushed into hills by earthquakes
C. Glaciers ground rock into fine silt, and prevailing winds carried and deposited the silt along the Missouri River valley
D. Ancient forests died and their remains compressed into the landform

*Correct Answer: C*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Earth's Surface | Practice: Constructing Explanations | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |

```

**MS Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Human Impact on Environment):**

Iowa farmers have observed increased soil erosion and nutrient runoff from corn and soybean fields, which affects water quality in the Des Moines River. Select the TWO strategies that would BEST help reduce agricultural runoff in Iowa.

A. Remove all vegetation from stream banks to increase water flow
B. Plant buffer strips of native grasses along waterways to filter runoff
C. Apply more fertilizer to increase crop yields and offset losses
D. Implement no-till farming to reduce soil disturbance and erosion
E. Divert all river water directly to crop fields for irrigation

*Correct Answers: B, D*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Human Impact | Practice: Engineering Solutions | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written explanation using evidence from the stimulus    |
|  Stimulus always provided (data, diagram, scenario)      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 11, DOK 3 — Biodiversity / Prairie Restoration):**

*Stimulus: A data table showing the number of plant species, insect species, and bird species in three Iowa land-use types: a monoculture corn field, a Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) grassland, and a restored native tallgrass prairie. The restored prairie has significantly higher biodiversity across all categories.*

Using evidence from the data table, explain why the restored native prairie supports greater biodiversity than the monoculture corn field. Describe TWO ecological factors from the data that support your explanation.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Accurately explains the relationship between habitat diversity and species richness; cites two specific data points; integrates cause-and-effect reasoning about habitat structure and food web complexity*
- *1 pt: Partially correct explanation with one piece of evidence, OR two pieces of evidence without adequate reasoning*
- *0 pts: Off-topic, blank, or major scientific misconception*

## 7. Three-Dimensional Design

Every ISASP Science item should integrate scientific practices, content knowledge, and crosscutting concepts.

### Three-Dimensions Alignment Guide

| Dimension | What It Is | Example in ISASP Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Content Knowledge** (What students know) | Disciplinary science content | Iowa prairie ecology; Loess Hills geology; wind energy physics; Maquoketa Caves; Des Moines watershed |
| **Scientific Practices** (What students do) | How scientists work | Analyze soil data; construct explanations from evidence; use models to predict ecosystem changes |
| **Crosscutting Concepts** (Lens of thinking) | Cross-domain thinking tools | Cause and effect; patterns; energy and matter; stability and change; systems and system models |

### Sample Three-Dimensional Item Design (Grade 8)

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3D ITEM STRUCTURE                                               |
|                                                                  |
|  PHENOMENON: Iowa's Loess Hills are one of only two             |
|  extensive loess formations in the world (the other is           |
|  in China), yet they are slowly eroding                          |
|  (Authentic Iowa phenomenon)                                     |
|                                                                  |
|  CONTENT: Earth Science — Erosion; Geologic processes            |
|  PRACTICE: Analyzing and Interpreting Data                       |
|  CROSSCUTTING: Stability and Change                              |
|                                                                  |
|  STIMULUS: Data table showing Loess Hills erosion rates,         |
|  vegetation cover, and precipitation over 30 years               |
|                                                                  |
|  PROMPT: "Using data from the table, describe the pattern        |
|  of erosion in the Loess Hills. Explain what factors are         |
|  contributing to the erosion and how vegetation cover             |
|  affects the rate of soil loss."                                 |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response accurately addresses the science concept; demonstrates understanding of practices and crosscutting concepts; includes specific, relevant evidence from the stimulus; explanation is complete, accurate, and logically connected |
| 1 | Response addresses the science concept with partial accuracy; evidence present but may be incomplete; reasoning partially developed |
| 0 | Response does not address the science concept; blank; off-topic; restates stimulus without explanation; no science understanding demonstrated |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Evidence from the stimulus is required.** Responses relying solely on prior knowledge earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Scientific reasoning** integrating content with practices or crosscutting concepts is needed for full credit.
- **Scientific misconceptions** reduce the score by at least one point.

## 10. Contextualization — Iowa Science

When generating ISASP Science items, prioritize authentic Iowa natural and agricultural settings:

### Iowa Science Context Guide

| Setting / Phenomenon | Grade | Science Domain | Standard Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iowa wind energy (turbine technology, energy conversion) | 8, 11 | Physical Science | Energy transfer; engineering |
| Loess Hills geology (wind-deposited silt, erosion) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Geologic processes; erosion |
| Iowa prairie restoration and biodiversity | 5, 8, 11 | Life Science | Ecosystems; biodiversity |
| Maquoketa Caves (karst geology, cave formation) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Rock formation; water chemistry |
| Iowa agricultural runoff and water quality | 8, 11 | Earth/Life Science | Human impact; water systems |
| Iowa soil science (topsoil depth, erosion prevention) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Soil formation; conservation |
| Des Moines River watershed hydrology | 5, 8, 11 | Earth Science | Water cycle; erosion; systems |
| Iowa wetland restoration and waterfowl habitats | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; biodiversity |
| Iowa severe weather and tornado formation | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Weather systems; energy |
| Iowa corn-ethanol production chemistry | 11 | Physical Science | Chemical reactions; energy |
| Iowa tallgrass prairie remnants and fire ecology | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; fire ecology |

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 5 Phenomena:**
- "Iowa farmers noticed that fields with cover crops lose much less topsoil during spring rains than bare fields. Why does plant cover reduce erosion?"
- "A class visited Maquoketa Caves State Park and noticed that the cave ceiling was covered in stalactites made of rock. How did rock form on a cave ceiling?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "Iowa's Loess Hills are made of fine silt deposited by wind during the last Ice Age. Without conservation, these hills erode much faster than surrounding landscapes. What factors make loess especially vulnerable to erosion?"
- "Water quality tests in the Des Moines River show higher nitrate levels during spring and early summer, corresponding with corn planting season. What explains this seasonal pattern?"

**Grade 11 Phenomena:**
- "Scientists found that restored native prairies in Iowa filter significantly more nitrogen from groundwater than corn fields, even though both are planted on similar soil types. What ecological and chemical processes explain this difference?"
- "Iowa now generates over 60% of its electricity from wind energy. What physical principles explain how wind turbines convert kinetic energy of moving air into electrical energy?"

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 5, 8, or 11 ONLY
- [ ] Iowa science standard is accurate for grade level
- [ ] Item integrates content knowledge with a scientific practice
- [ ] Anchoring phenomenon is authentic and Iowa-relevant where possible
- [ ] Stimulus has clear labels, units, and realistic data
- [ ] CR prompt directs students to use evidence from the stimulus
- [ ] All distractors represent real misconceptions
- [ ] MS items specify how many answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] Science content is accurate and reflects current scientific understanding
- [ ] Vocabulary is grade-appropriate

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7 | ISASP Science is only at Grades 5, 8, 11 | Restrict to tested grades |
| Content-only items with no scientific practice | ISASP values practice integration | Embed a scientific practice into item design |
| CR prompts without evidence directive | Students may respond without citing data | Include "Use evidence from the [data/graph/diagram]" |
| Generic Midwest phenomena | ISASP favors Iowa-specific contexts | Use Loess Hills, Iowa prairie, wind energy, Maquoketa Caves contexts |
| MS items with only 2 options in 4 total | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Data-to-Conclusion (MC, DOK 2)
> "The [graph / table / diagram] below shows [Iowa science scenario]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / explanation / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 2: Phenomenon Explanation (CR, DOK 3)
> "Scientists observed [Iowa-anchored phenomenon]. Using evidence from the [data / model / diagram], construct an explanation for [specific science focus]. Explain the [cause-and-effect / energy flow / pattern] relationship."

### Pattern 3: Model Evaluation (MC or MS, DOK 2–3)
> "A student made a model showing [Iowa science concept]. Which statement BEST describes what the model shows about [content focus]?"

### Pattern 4: Investigation Design (CR, DOK 3)
> "A scientist in Iowa wants to investigate whether [variable] affects [outcome] in [Iowa ecosystem/setting]. Describe a procedure including what would be measured and how results would be interpreted."

### Pattern 5: Evidence-Based Argument (CR, DOK 3)
> "Two students disagree about [Iowa science claim]. Student A says [misconception]. Student B says [correct claim]. Using evidence from the [data / diagram], explain which student is correct and why."

### Pattern 6: Human Impact and Solutions (MS or CR, DOK 2–3)
> "Human activities have [impacted Iowa environment]. Select the TWO strategies that BEST [monitor / reduce / address] this impact."
