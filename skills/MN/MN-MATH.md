## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Minnesota Comprehensive Assessments (MCA) — Mathematics |
| **Administering Agency** | Minnesota Department of Education (MDE) |
| **Vendor** | Pearson |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10 (Grade 11 optional) |
| **Testing Window** | March – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; paper accommodations available |
| **Standards** | Minnesota Academic Standards — Mathematics |
| **Score Scale** | Scaled score; 4 performance levels |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **MCA Math Structure:** The MCA Mathematics assessment uses multiple choice (MC) and short response (SR, scored 0–2) items. Items emphasize real-world application, conceptual understanding, and procedural fluency aligned to Minnesota Academic Standards.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Exceeds the Standards** | Demonstrates advanced understanding; applies Minnesota math standards in complex, non-routine situations |
| 3 | **Meets the Standards** | Demonstrates solid understanding of grade-level Minnesota math standards; meets proficiency expectations |
| 2 | **Partially Meets the Standards** | Demonstrates partial understanding; developing but not yet meeting grade-level expectations |
| 1 | **Does Not Meet the Standards** | Demonstrates minimal understanding; significant gaps in grade-level mathematics |

> **Item-writing target:** "Meets the Standards" performance. Items should require genuine application of Minnesota Academic Standards — not mere recall of procedures.

## 2. Standards Framework

> MCA Mathematics is aligned to the **Minnesota Academic Standards for Mathematics**, organized by strands that build progressively from number sense through algebra, geometry, and data analysis.

### Minnesota Mathematics Strands

| Strand | Focus | Grade Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Number and Operation** | Place value, fractions, decimals, integers, rational numbers, operations | Primary focus Grades 3–7 |
| **Algebra** | Patterns, expressions, equations, inequalities, functions | Grows Grades 4–10 |
| **Geometry and Measurement** | Shapes, area, volume, transformations, coordinate geometry, units | All grades |
| **Data Analysis and Probability** | Data displays, central tendency, probability, statistical reasoning | All grades; increases Grades 6–10 |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Real-world or mathematical context                      |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 1–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 6, DOK 2, MN.Math6.1.2.1):**

Minnesota has approximately 11,842 lakes that are 10 acres or larger. A student wants to find how many lakes per county there are on average across Minnesota's 87 counties. Which expression represents this calculation?

A. 87 / 11,842
B. 11,842 / 87
C. 11,842 x 87
D. 11,842 - 87

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: MN.Math6.1.2.1 | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2, MN.Math8.4.1.1):**

A student collected data on the surface area (in square miles) and maximum depth (in feet) of six Minnesota lakes. The scatterplot shows a weak positive association.

Which statement is BEST supported by the scatterplot?

A. As surface area increases, maximum depth always increases proportionally
B. There is a general trend that larger lakes tend to be deeper, but there are exceptions
C. Surface area and maximum depth have no relationship
D. The deepest lake always has the largest surface area

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: MN.Math8.4.1.1 | DOK 2*

### Short Response (SR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  SHORT RESPONSE — 0–2 points                             |
|                                                          |
|  Show work and/or explain reasoning                      |
|  May require numeric answer + justification              |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**SR Example (Grade 7, DOK 3, MN.Math7.1.2.1 — 0–2 pts):**

The Iron Range in northern Minnesota produced 40 million tons of iron ore in 2020 and 36 million tons in 2021. If production continues to decrease at the same rate, how many tons will the Iron Range produce in 2023? Show your work and explain your reasoning.

---

**SR Example (Grade 10, DOK 2, MN.Math10.2.1.1 — 0–2 pts):**

Lake Superior has a surface area of approximately 31,700 square miles and a volume of approximately 2,900 cubic miles. Lake Michigan has a surface area of approximately 22,400 square miles and a volume of approximately 1,180 cubic miles. Which lake has a greater average depth? Show your work.

## 7. Mathematical Representations

MCA Mathematics items frequently require students to move between multiple representations.

### Representation Types

| Representation | Description | Common Item Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tabular** | Input/output table; data table | Identify patterns; find missing values; analyze trends |
| **Graphical** | Coordinate plane; bar/line graph; scatterplot | Identify slope; read data; compare quantities |
| **Algebraic** | Equations; inequalities; expressions | Solve; write; simplify; model real-world contexts |
| **Verbal** | Word problem; real-world scenario | Apply to Minnesota context |
| **Visual/Geometric** | Diagrams; figures with measurements; maps | Compute area/volume; apply transformations |

### Representation Connections by Grade Band

| Grade Band | Frequent Connections |
|---|---|
| 3–5 | Number line ↔ fraction; table ↔ pattern rule; model ↔ computation |
| 6–7 | Table ↔ equation ↔ graph; ratio ↔ percent ↔ proportion |
| 8–10 | Equation ↔ graph ↔ table; scatterplot ↔ linear model; function ↔ multiple representations |

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Multiple Choice (MC) — 1 Point

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | Student selects the correct answer |
| 0 | Student selects an incorrect answer or leaves blank |

### Short Response (SR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Correct answer with complete, accurate mathematical work shown; reasoning is clear and valid |
| 1 | Partially correct; correct approach with computational error, OR correct answer without sufficient work shown |
| 0 | Incorrect, irrelevant, or blank |

## 10. Contextualization — Minnesota Mathematics

When generating math problem contexts, use authentic Minnesota settings:

### Minnesota Math Context Guide

| Context | Domain | Grade Band |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Superior dimensions (surface area, depth, volume, temperature) | Measurement, Data Analysis, Ratio | 5–10 |
| Minnesota's 10,000 lakes data (counts, areas, depths, comparisons) | Data Analysis, Operations, Ratio | 3–10 |
| Iron Range production data (tonnage, trends, economic impact) | Data Analysis, Proportionality, Functions | 5–10 |
| Minnesota agriculture (corn, soybeans, sugar beets, dairy) | Operations, Ratio, Data Analysis | 3–7 |
| Minneapolis / St. Paul population and geography | Data Analysis, Operations | 5–8 |
| Minnesota winter weather data (temperature, snowfall, wind chill) | Data Analysis, Integers, Statistics | 4–8 |
| Boundary Waters canoe trip distances | Rate, Measurement, Proportionality | 5–8 |
| Minnesota state parks acreage and visitor data | Data Analysis, Operations | 4–8 |
| Mall of America dimensions and statistics | Geometry, Measurement | 4–7 |
| Mississippi River headwaters data | Measurement, Rate, Data Analysis | 5–10 |

### Suggested Problem Contexts by Grade Band

**Grades 3–4:**
- A student counts fish caught at a Minnesota lake over five days and finds the total
- A class measures snowfall in Minneapolis each week and creates a bar graph
- A table shows the number of visitors to a Minnesota state park each month

**Grades 5–6:**
- Lake Superior temperature data used to calculate mean and range across months
- A proportional relationship between canoe trip distance and time in the Boundary Waters
- Minnesota corn and soybean yield comparisons across counties

**Grades 7–10:**
- Scatterplot of Minnesota lake depth vs. surface area with line of best fit
- Iron Range iron ore production over decades analyzed for linear and nonlinear trends
- Lake Superior water volume calculations and rate-of-change problems

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Minnesota Academic Standard code is accurate and grade-appropriate
- [ ] Calculator policy followed for the target grade (no calculator Gr 3–5)
- [ ] Real-world context is relevant to Minnesota students
- [ ] MC distractors represent actual student errors (not trivially wrong)
- [ ] SR items require showing work or explaining reasoning
- [ ] DOK level matches the cognitive demand of the problem
- [ ] All computations verified before item is finalized
- [ ] Answer choices are in logical order (ascending/descending for numeric)
- [ ] Units and labels are consistent throughout the item
- [ ] SR scored on 0–2 scale with clear rubric criteria

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Calculator use at Grade 3–5 | MCA Gr 3–5 has no calculator | Design problems solvable by hand |
| SR without "show work" requirement | MCA SR items require demonstrated reasoning | Include "show your work" or "explain your reasoning" |
| MC distractors obviously wrong | Distractors must represent real student errors | Use common computation or conceptual errors |
| DOK 1 items that only require recall | MCA aims for mostly DOK 2 | Embed problems in real-world context |
| Over-complex word problems at Grades 3–4 | Reading demand should not exceed grade-level ELA | Keep problem setup brief at lower grades |
| Using CCSS codes | Minnesota uses its own Academic Standards | Reference Minnesota standard codes exclusively |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Real-World Multi-Step (MC or SR, DOK 2–3)
> "[Minnesota context — Lake Superior, Iron Range, lakes data]. [Specific mathematical question]. What is [quantity]? Show your work."

### Pattern 2: Representation Connection (MC, DOK 2)
> "A student created a [table / graph / equation] to represent [relationship]. Which [representation / statement] represents the SAME relationship?"

### Pattern 3: Data Interpretation (MC or SR, DOK 2–3)
> "The [table / graph / scatterplot] shows [Minnesota data]. Based on the data, [what is the value / which conclusion / what prediction] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 4: Geometry Application (MC or SR, DOK 2)
> "[Minnesota context — lake, building, park]. What is the [area / volume / perimeter] of [figure]?"

### Pattern 5: Proportional Reasoning (MC, DOK 2)
> "[Minnesota context — production, distance, recipe]. If [proportional setup], then what is [unknown quantity]?"

### Pattern 6: Explain and Justify (SR, DOK 3)
> "[Minnesota scenario]. [Mathematical question]. Show your work and explain your reasoning."
