| Heritage             | PARCC-influenced task types (Literary Analysis, Research Simulation, Narrative)  |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** LEAP 2025 ELA uses **Louisiana Student Standards for ELA**, which are aligned to Common Core State Standards (CCSS). Standard codes follow the CCSS format (e.g., RL.5.2, RI.8.6) but are administered under Louisiana's framework. LEAP 2025 retains PARCC-influenced task types including Literary Analysis Tasks (LAT), Research Simulation Tasks (RST), and Narrative Writing Tasks (NWT).

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label              | Description                                                                   |
|-------|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Unsatisfactory     | Student has not yet met grade-level expectations; major gaps in understanding |
| 2     | Approaching Basic  | Student is approaching basic mastery of grade-level content                   |
| 3     | Basic              | Student demonstrates basic mastery of knowledge and skills                   |
| 4     | Mastery            | Student has met grade-level expectations (proficient)                        |
| 5     | Advanced           | Student has exceeded grade-level expectations; advanced analysis and writing  |

> **Proficiency Target:** Level 4 (Mastery) is the proficiency target in Louisiana. Level 3 (Basic) indicates the student is on track but has not yet achieved full proficiency.

## 2. STANDARDS FRAMEWORK

> **Reference:** See `_shared/ccss_ela_framework.md` for full CCR Anchor Standards and grade-by-grade progressions. Louisiana Student Standards for ELA incorporate CCSS ELA standards with Louisiana-specific additions.

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard   | Gr 3–5 Focus                                                    | Gr 6–8 Focus                                                        | Gr 11 Focus                                                     |
|------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **RL.1**   | Ask and answer questions; quote from text                       | Cite textual evidence to support analysis                            | Cite strong and thorough evidence; draw inferences               |
| **RL.2**   | Determine theme or central message; summarize                   | Determine theme; analyze development over course of text             | Analyze theme development; provide objective summary             |
| **RL.3**   | Describe characters, settings, events in detail                 | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character and advance plot  | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story elements      |
| **RL.4**   | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language       | Analyze figurative, connotative meanings; word choice and tone       | Analyze cumulative impact of specific word choices on meaning    |
| **RL.5**   | Explain how chapters, scenes, stanzas fit together              | Analyze how structure contributes to meaning and style               | Analyze how structure creates effects such as tension or surprise |
| **RL.6**   | Identify narrator's point of view                               | Analyze how differences in POV create effects                        | Analyze a case in which understanding POV requires distinguishing what is directly stated from what is meant |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard   | Gr 3–5 Focus                                                    | Gr 6–8 Focus                                                        | Gr 11 Focus                                                     |
|------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **RI.1**   | Quote accurately; draw inferences                               | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis                            | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis            |
| **RI.2**   | Determine main idea; summarize                                  | Determine central idea; analyze development                          | Determine two or more central ideas; analyze development         |
| **RI.3**   | Explain relationships between events, ideas, concepts           | Analyze how key individuals, events, or ideas are introduced and elaborated | Analyze a complex set of ideas and explain how they interact    |
| **RI.6**   | Identify author's purpose                                       | Determine author's point of view; analyze how purpose shapes content | Determine author's purpose; analyze how style and content contribute |
| **RI.8**   | Explain how reasons support points                              | Trace and evaluate argument; assess whether reasoning is sound       | Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal U.S. texts          |

## 3. TEST STRUCTURE

LEAP 2025 ELA is administered across **multiple sessions** over two or more days. The test includes PARCC-influenced task types.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|             LEAP 2025 ELA STRUCTURE (Grades 3–8, 11)             |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SESSION 1 — Literary Analysis Task (LAT)                        |
|  ~90 minutes                                                      |
|                                                                   |
|  1–2 literary passages                                            |
|  MC items (1 pt), EBSR items (2 pt)                              |
|  Short Constructed Response (0–2 pt)                              |
|  Extended Constructed Response (0–4 pt) — essay-style prompt     |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SESSION 2 — Research Simulation Task (RST)                      |
|  ~90 minutes                                                      |
|                                                                   |
|  2–3 informational sources (articles, data, excerpts)            |
|  MC items (1 pt), EBSR items (2 pt)                              |
|  Short CR (0–2 pt)                                                |
|  Extended CR (0–4 pt) — analytical/argumentative essay           |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SESSION 3 — Narrative Writing Task (NWT)                        |
|  ~90 minutes                                                      |
|                                                                   |
|  1 literary passage as stimulus                                   |
|  MC items (1 pt), EBSR items (2 pt)                              |
|  Extended CR (0–4 pt) — narrative writing prompt                 |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Session Item Counts by Grade Band

| Session                        | Grades 3–5                            | Grades 6–8                            | Grade 11                              |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Session 1 (LAT)              | 8–12 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR | 10–14 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR| 10–14 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR|
| Session 2 (RST)              | 8–12 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR | 10–14 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR| 10–14 MC/EBSR + 1 Short CR + 1 Ext CR|
| Session 3 (NWT)              | 6–8 MC/EBSR + 1 Ext CR               | 6–10 MC/EBSR + 1 Ext CR              | 8–10 MC/EBSR + 1 Ext CR              |

> **PARCC Heritage:** LEAP 2025 retains the three PARCC task types (LAT, RST, NWT). Each task type has a distinct purpose: LAT assesses literary analysis, RST assesses research and informational text synthesis, and NWT assesses narrative craft.

## 5. ITEM TYPES

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Single correct answer                                   |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  All sessions                                            |
|  DOK 1–2                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, RL.5.2 — Theme):**

*Passage excerpt: A young boy in the Louisiana bayou helps his grandfather repair fishing nets as a hurricane approaches, learning that their family's livelihood depends on respecting the water rather than fearing it.*

Which theme is BEST supported by the events in the passage?

- A. People should avoid dangerous situations at all costs
- B. Understanding and respecting nature is more valuable than fearing it
- C. Older people always know more than younger people
- D. Fishing is the best way to earn a living in Louisiana

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RL.5.2 | DOK 2*

---

### Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EVIDENCE-BASED SELECTED RESPONSE — 2 points            |
|                                                          |
|  Part A: Inference or analysis (1 pt)                   |
|  Part B: Text evidence supporting Part A (1 pt)          |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  All sessions                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**EBSR Example (Grade 7, DOK 3, RI.7.6 — Author's Purpose):**

*[After reading an article about the history of New Orleans jazz and its influence on American music]*

**Part A:** What is the author's MAIN purpose in including the section about early jazz musicians in the French Quarter?

- A. To entertain the reader with amusing stories about street performers
- B. To show how the cultural diversity of New Orleans directly shaped the development of jazz
- C. To argue that New Orleans is the only city where jazz should be performed
- D. To explain why modern music is not as good as traditional jazz

**Part B:** Which sentence from the article BEST supports your answer to Part A?

- A. "Tourists today can still hear live music on almost every corner of the French Quarter."
- B. "The blending of African rhythms, Caribbean melodies, and European harmonies in New Orleans' diverse neighborhoods gave birth to a uniquely American art form."
- C. "Many jazz musicians eventually left New Orleans and moved to cities like Chicago and New York."
- D. "The first jazz recordings were made in 1917 by a group of white musicians."

*Correct Answers: Part A: B | Part B: B*
*Alignment: RI.7.6 | DOK 3*

### Short Constructed Response (Short CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  SHORT CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                 |
|                                                          |
|  Brief written response (1–2 paragraphs)                |
|  Must cite textual evidence                              |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
|  LAT and RST sessions                                    |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Short CR Example (Grade 4, DOK 2, RL.4.3 — Character):**

*"How does the main character's attitude toward Mardi Gras change from the beginning to the end of the story? Use one detail from the passage to support your answer."*

---

### Extended Constructed Response (Extended CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EXTENDED CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–4 points              |
|                                                          |
|  Multi-paragraph written response                        |
|  Must cite textual evidence with elaboration             |
|  Human scored using trait-based rubric                   |
|  DOK 3–4                                                 |
|  1 per session (3 total per test)                        |
|  LAT: literary analysis essay                            |
|  RST: analytical/argumentative essay                     |
|  NWT: narrative writing                                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Extended CR Example — LAT (Grade 8, DOK 3, RL.8.2 + RL.8.3):**

*"In the passage, the author develops the theme that cultural heritage shapes identity. Analyze how the main character's experiences in the Cajun community of southern Louisiana contribute to the development of this theme. Use evidence from the text to support your analysis."*

**Extended CR Example — RST (Grade 6, DOK 3, RI.6.8 + W.6.1):**

*"Based on the three sources you have read about the Mississippi River Delta, write an essay that argues whether human activity or natural processes are more responsible for coastal land loss in Louisiana. Use evidence from at least two sources to support your argument."*

**Extended CR Example — NWT (Grade 5, DOK 3, W.5.3):**

*"Using what you have learned from the passage about life on the Louisiana bayou, write a story about a young person who discovers something unexpected during a fishing trip. Your story should include descriptive details about the setting and develop the characters through dialogue and action."*

## 8. SCORING RUBRICS

### Extended Constructed Response (Extended CR) Rubric — 0–4 Points

The LEAP 2025 Extended CR rubric evaluates **Reading Comprehension** and **Written Expression** holistically.

| Score | Reading Comprehension                                                       | Written Expression                                                         |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 4     | Demonstrates thorough understanding of the text(s); analysis is insightful and well-developed; all claims are fully supported with relevant, specific evidence | Writing is clear, coherent, and well-organized; effective use of transitions; precise and varied vocabulary; strong command of conventions with few errors |
| 3     | Demonstrates adequate understanding of the text(s); analysis is clear and developed; claims are supported with relevant evidence | Writing is generally clear and organized; adequate transitions; appropriate vocabulary; adequate command of conventions with some errors |
| 2     | Demonstrates partial understanding of the text(s); analysis is limited or uneven; evidence is present but may be vague or incomplete | Writing has some organization; limited transitions; general vocabulary; partial command of conventions; errors may interfere with meaning |
| 1     | Demonstrates minimal understanding of the text(s); little or no analysis; evidence is absent or inaccurate | Writing lacks organization; minimal transitions; imprecise vocabulary; limited command of conventions; frequent errors interfere with meaning |
| 0     | Does not address the prompt; blank; off-topic; copied from passage; or incomprehensible | No evidence of writing skill |

### Short Constructed Response (Short CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria                                                                                                          |
|-------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 2     | Accurately and completely addresses the prompt; cites specific, relevant textual evidence; clear explanation       |
| 1     | Partially addresses the prompt; evidence may be vague, incomplete, or only partially relevant; limited explanation |
| 0     | Does not address the prompt; no evidence cited; blank; off-topic; or incomprehensible                             |

## 11. QUALITY CHECKLIST AND COMMON PITFALLS

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Standard code uses CCSS format: RL/RI/W.[Grade].[Standard]
- [ ] Task type clearly identified (LAT, RST, or NWT)
- [ ] EBSR Part B directly supports Part A correct answer
- [ ] Extended CR prompt includes clear analytical focus (not "summarize")
- [ ] Extended CR prompt ends with evidence directive
- [ ] Short CR requires specific textual evidence
- [ ] Passage Lexile within grade-band range (see Section 7)
- [ ] MC options are plausible; no "trick" or obviously wrong choices
- [ ] Louisiana-specific context used where appropriate
- [ ] Performance levels use Louisiana terminology (Unsatisfactory through Advanced)
- [ ] NWT prompt provides stimulus passage and clear narrative task
- [ ] DOK level verified against item cognitive demand
- [ ] RST includes 2–3 sources for multi-source analysis

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall                                        | Problem                                              | Fix                                                          |
|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Omitting PARCC task type alignment             | LEAP 2025 retains LAT/RST/NWT structure             | Always specify which task type the item belongs to           |
| Writing RST with only one source               | RST requires multi-source synthesis                  | Include 2–3 sources for RST items                            |
| NWT prompt without stimulus passage            | NWT items are passage-dependent                      | Always provide a literary stimulus before the narrative prompt|
| Extended CR prompt that asks for summary        | DOK 3–4 requires analysis, not retelling            | Frame prompt around analysis, argument, or craft             |
| Ignoring Louisiana cultural diversity          | Test fairness requires representation                | Rotate across LA communities (Cajun, Creole, Native, Vietnamese, etc.) |
| Using generic "national" contexts              | Louisiana test values LA-specific authenticity        | Anchor passages in real LA places and communities            |
| EBSR Part B supports a different Part A answer | Part B must directly support Part A's correct answer | Cross-check: does Part B quote only make sense given Part A? |
| Confusing Basic (L3) with proficient           | In LA, Mastery (L4) is the proficiency target        | Ensure item difficulty targets Mastery-level performance     |

## 12. PROMPT PATTERNS

The following patterns reflect the most commonly tested item structures on LEAP 2025 ELA.

### Pattern 1: Literary Analysis — Theme Development (Extended CR, DOK 3, LAT)
> "In the passage '[title],' the author develops the theme that [theme]. Analyze how [character actions / literary devices / events] contribute to the development of this theme. Use evidence from the text to support your analysis."

### Pattern 2: Research Simulation — Argument from Sources (Extended CR, DOK 3–4, RST)
> "Based on the sources you have read about [Louisiana topic], write an essay that argues [position]. Use evidence from at least two sources to support your argument. Be sure to acknowledge and address a counterclaim."

### Pattern 3: Narrative Writing (Extended CR, DOK 3, NWT)
> "Using what you have learned from the passage about [setting/situation], write a story about [related scenario]. Include descriptive details about the setting, develop characters through dialogue and action, and use a clear sequence of events."

### Pattern 4: Author's Purpose and Evidence (EBSR, DOK 2–3, RST)
> "Part A: What is the author's MAIN purpose in [section/paragraph]? Part B: Which sentence from the text BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 5: Figurative Language and Meaning (MC, DOK 2, LAT)
> "Read this sentence from the passage: '[sentence with figurative language].' What does this [metaphor/simile/idiom] MOST LIKELY mean in the context of the passage?"

### Pattern 6: Central Idea with Evidence (EBSR, DOK 2–3, RST)
> "Part A: What is the central idea of [source/passage]? Part B: Which detail from the text BEST supports the central idea identified in Part A?"
