## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Nebraska Student-Centered Assessment System (NSCAS) — English Language Arts |
| **Administering Agency** | Nebraska Department of Education (NDE) |
| **Vendor** | NE State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 |
| **Testing Window** | March – April |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based |
| **Standards** | Nebraska College and Career Ready Standards for English Language Arts |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **NSCAS ELA Overview:** The Nebraska Student-Centered Assessment System measures student mastery of the Nebraska College and Career Ready Standards for English Language Arts. Items are passage-based, assessing reading comprehension, writing, and language skills across literary and informational texts. Nebraska standards emphasize critical thinking, evidence-based reasoning, and culturally relevant literacy.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Level 4 — Exceeds** | Student demonstrates thorough and sophisticated understanding of grade-level ELA standards; analyzes complex texts with precision and depth |
| 3 | **Level 3 — Meets** | Student demonstrates adequate understanding; reads and comprehends grade-level texts; uses relevant evidence to support analysis |
| 2 | **Level 2 — Approaching** | Student demonstrates partial understanding; inconsistent text analysis; limited use of evidence |
| 1 | **Level 1 — Beginning** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding; significant gaps in reading comprehension and analysis skills |

## 2. Standards Framework

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RL.1** — Key details & inference | Identify explicit details; make simple inferences from text | Cite textual evidence; analyze explicit vs. inferential meaning | Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine where text is ambiguous |
| **RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine the lesson, moral, or central message; summarize | Analyze theme development and interaction across the text | Analyze multiple themes and their complex interplay within texts |
| **RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe character traits, motivations, and feelings | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development and structure |
| **RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; impact of word choice | Analyze impact of specific word choices on meaning, tone, and aesthetic quality |
| **RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's perspective | Analyze how differences in point of view create effects such as suspense | Analyze how point of view and perspective shape content and style |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 11 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RI.1** — Key details & inference | Ask and answer questions; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis of what text says |
| **RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how key ideas are developed and refined across sections | Analyze complex set of ideas and explain how specific individuals, events, or ideas interact |
| **RI.6** — Author's purpose & POV | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view or purpose; analyze how it is conveyed | Determine author's point of view in text with multiple or conflicting perspectives |
| **RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support an author's point | Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text | Evaluate the reasoning and rhetoric in seminal and foundational texts |
| **RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare texts on the same topic | Analyze how different authors present information on the same topic | Analyze and synthesize foundational documents of historical and literary significance |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, and examples | 3 |
| **W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5 Informational, DOK 2, RI.5.3):**

*[After reading an article about the Nebraska Sandhills and their importance to the ranching industry]*

Which detail from the article BEST explains why the Nebraska Sandhills are important to ranchers?

A. The Sandhills cover approximately 19,600 square miles of north-central Nebraska
B. The grass-stabilized sand dunes provide natural grazing land for cattle because the root systems hold the sandy soil in place
C. The Sandhills were formed by wind deposits thousands of years ago
D. Several rivers flow through the Sandhills region of Nebraska

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RI.5.3 | Reading — Informational Text | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–6 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  Full credit only; partial credit not awarded            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 7 Literary, DOK 2, RL.7.3):**

*[After reading a short story about a family traveling the Oregon Trail who stops at Chimney Rock]*

Select the TWO details from the story that BEST show how the narrator's feelings about the journey change after seeing Chimney Rock.

A. "The wagon had creaked the same dull rhythm for weeks, and I was certain nothing interesting would ever appear on this flat earth."
B. "Mother counted the remaining flour sacks and frowned."
C. "When the rock tower appeared above the prairie like a finger pointing at the sky, I felt something rise in my chest — maybe it was hope."
D. "Father said we still had four hundred miles to go before Oregon."
E. "The oxen moved slower in the afternoon heat."

*Correct Answers: A, C*
*Alignment: RL.7.3 | Reading — Literary Text | DOK 2*

---

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Short written response (3–5 sentences)                  |
|  Must use evidence from the passage                      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 8 Informational, DOK 3, RI.8.8):**

*[After reading an informational article about the sandhill crane migration along the Platte River]*

The author argues that the Platte River crane migration is one of the most important wildlife events in North America. Explain how the author supports this argument using TWO pieces of evidence from the passage.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Identifies two relevant pieces of evidence from the passage and explains how each supports the author's argument*
- *1 pt: Identifies one piece of evidence with explanation, OR identifies two pieces without adequate explanation*
- *0 pts: No relevant evidence cited or response is off-topic*

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response demonstrates thorough understanding. Uses specific, relevant evidence from the passage. Explanation is complete, accurate, and clearly connected to the question. Analysis shows grade-level reasoning. |
| 1 | Response demonstrates partial understanding. Evidence is present but may be vague, incomplete, or only partially relevant. Explanation is partially developed or contains minor inaccuracies. |
| 0 | Response does not address the question; blank; off-topic; restates the question without analysis; no evidence from the passage. |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Text evidence is required.** Responses that rely on prior knowledge without referencing the passage earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Direct quotes or specific paraphrases** from the passage strengthen a response.
- **Two-evidence items** require both pieces of evidence for full credit (2 points).

## 10. Contextualization — Nebraska ELA

When generating passages and prompts, prioritize authentic Nebraska settings and culture:

### Nebraska Context Guide

| Context | Passage Type | Grade Band |
|---|---|---|
| Nebraska Sandhills ranching and ecology | Informational or Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Oregon Trail / Chimney Rock / pioneer migration | Informational or Historical fiction | 4–11 |
| Platte River sandhill crane migration | Informational or Literary | 3–8 |
| Nebraska corn and soybean farming | Informational or Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Nebraska beef industry and feedlots | Informational | 5–11 |
| Homestead Act / Nebraska settlement history | Informational or Historical fiction | 5–11 |
| Willa Cather and Nebraska literary heritage | Literary or Informational | 6–11 |
| Nebraska Unicameral Legislature | Informational / Argumentative | 7–11 |
| Ogallala Aquifer and water resources | Informational | 5–11 |
| Nebraska small-town community life | Literary fiction | 3–8 |
| Platte River ecology and riparian habitats | Informational | 4–8 |
| Nebraska state fair and agricultural traditions | Informational or Literary | 3–7 |

### Suggested Passage Topics by Grade

**Grades 3–5:**
- A student watches the sandhill crane migration along the Platte River near Kearney
- A class field trip to Chimney Rock to learn about the Oregon Trail
- The story of a farm family during corn harvest season in central Nebraska

**Grades 6–8:**
- The history and significance of the Homestead Act and pioneer settlement in Nebraska
- How the Nebraska Sandhills became one of the largest grass-stabilized dune systems in the world
- A narrative about a student helping with the annual sandhill crane count along the Platte River

**Grade 11:**
- An editorial debating water rights and the future of the Ogallala Aquifer in western Nebraska
- Analysis of Willa Cather's portrayal of Nebraska prairie life and its literary significance
- The economic and environmental impact of Nebraska's position as a top beef-producing state

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Nebraska College and Career Ready Standards code verified for grade level
- [ ] MC items have exactly 4 options with one correct answer
- [ ] MS items specify exact number of answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] CR items require text evidence from the passage
- [ ] CR scored on 0–2 point scale
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] Nebraska context incorporated where appropriate
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions
- [ ] DOK level matches actual cognitive demand
- [ ] Grade 11 items reflect appropriate complexity for high school

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| CR prompt not requiring evidence | Students may respond without citing text | Include "Use evidence from the passage" in all CR prompts |
| MS items with fewer than 5 options | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |
| Items that can be answered without reading the passage | Not passage-dependent | Ensure all items require specific passage content |
| Grade 11 items at middle school complexity | Underestimates high school expectations | Use complex texts and analytical prompts for Grade 11 |
| Generic Midwest settings without Nebraska specificity | Misses state contextualization | Use Nebraska-specific locations, history, and culture |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Key Idea / Main Idea (MC, DOK 2)
> "Which statement BEST expresses the [main idea / central message / theme] of the passage?"

### Pattern 2: Evidence Selection (MS, DOK 2)
> "Select the TWO [details / sentences] from the passage that BEST [support / show / explain] [specific focus]."

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose or Craft (MC, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice] in [paragraph/section] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding]?"

### Pattern 4: Evidence-Based Analysis (CR, DOK 3)
> "[Analytical question]. Use TWO pieces of evidence from the passage to support your response."

### Pattern 5: Vocabulary in Context (MC, DOK 2)
> "As used in paragraph [X], the word [vocabulary word] MOST LIKELY means —"

### Pattern 6: Compare and Contrast (MC or CR, DOK 3)
> "How does the [author's treatment / perspective / information] in [Source 1] differ from [Source 2]? Use evidence from BOTH passages to support your answer."
