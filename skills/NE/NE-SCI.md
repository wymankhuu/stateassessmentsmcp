## AssessmentForge Skill Guide | Grades 5, 8, and 11

---

## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Nebraska Student-Centered Assessment System (NSCAS) — Science |
| **Administering Agency** | Nebraska Department of Education (NDE) |
| **Vendor** | NE State DOE |
| **Grades Tested** | **Grade 5**, **Grade 8**, and **Grade 11** |
| **Testing Window** | March – April |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; single session |
| **Standards** | Nebraska College and Career Ready Standards for Science |
| **Score Scale** | 4 performance levels; scaled score varies by grade |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** NSCAS Science is administered **only at Grades 5, 8, and 11**. Do not generate science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7.

> **NSCAS Science Standards:** Nebraska science standards emphasize scientific inquiry and content knowledge. Items should integrate disciplinary core ideas with scientific practices and crosscutting concepts where applicable.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Level 4 — Exceeds** | Demonstrates thorough science understanding; expertly integrates scientific knowledge with practices and crosscutting concepts |
| 3 | **Level 3 — Meets** | Demonstrates adequate science understanding; appropriately applies scientific practices and concepts |
| 2 | **Level 2 — Approaching** | Demonstrates partial understanding; applies content knowledge but struggles to integrate practices meaningfully |
| 1 | **Level 1 — Beginning** | Demonstrates minimal science understanding; limited to recall of isolated facts |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Scenario, diagram, data table, or model stimulus        |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2 — Ecosystem Energy Flow):**

A diagram shows a Nebraska Sandhills grassland food chain: Sunlight -> Prairie Grasses -> Prairie Voles -> Coyotes -> Decomposers

Which statement BEST explains the role of prairie grasses in this Nebraska Sandhills ecosystem?

A. Prairie grasses receive energy from prairie voles through the food chain
B. Prairie grasses capture energy from the sun and convert it into food stored in their cells
C. Prairie grasses are decomposers that break down dead organisms in the grassland
D. Prairie grasses get most of their energy from nutrients in the sandy soil

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: Life Science — Ecosystems | SEP: Developing and Using Models | CCC: Energy and Matter | DOK 2*

---

**MC Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Earth's Surface):**

Geologists studying the Ogallala Aquifer beneath western Nebraska discovered that water levels have dropped significantly over the past 60 years while irrigation farming has increased. Which statement BEST explains this relationship?

A. The aquifer water evaporated because of rising temperatures
B. Groundwater is being pumped out for irrigation faster than it is being recharged by precipitation
C. Tectonic activity shifted the underground water to a different location
D. The aquifer was always shrinking and farming has no effect on water levels

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Human Impact | SEP: Constructing Explanations | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–6 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  All must be selected for credit                         |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 8, DOK 2 — Human Impact on Environment):**

Nebraska farmers have observed increased soil erosion on cropland during spring windstorms. Select the TWO strategies that would BEST help reduce soil erosion on Nebraska farmland.

A. Remove all vegetation from field edges to maximize planting area
B. Plant cover crops during the winter months to protect soil from wind
C. Increase the amount of chemical fertilizer applied each season
D. Use no-till farming methods to keep crop residue and root systems intact
E. Pump additional water from the Ogallala Aquifer to keep soil moist year-round

*Correct Answers: B, D*
*Alignment: Earth Science — Human Impact | SEP: Constructing Explanations | CCC: Cause and Effect | DOK 2*

### Constructed Response (CR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–2 points                       |
|                                                          |
|  Written explanation using evidence from the stimulus    |
|  Stimulus always provided (data, diagram, scenario)      |
|  Human scored                                            |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**CR Example (Grade 11, DOK 3 — Biodiversity):**

*Stimulus: A data table showing the number of plant species, insect species, and bird species in three Nebraska ecosystems: a monoculture corn field, a restored native prairie, and a Sandhills mixed-grass rangeland. The Sandhills rangeland and restored prairie have significantly higher biodiversity than the corn field.*

Using evidence from the data table, explain why the native grassland ecosystems support greater biodiversity than the monoculture corn field. Describe TWO factors from the data that support your explanation.

*Scoring:*
- *2 pts: Accurately explains the relationship between habitat diversity and biodiversity; cites two specific data points from the table; integrates cause-and-effect reasoning*
- *1 pt: Partially correct explanation with one piece of evidence, OR two pieces of evidence without adequate cause-and-effect reasoning*
- *0 pts: Off-topic, blank, or major scientific misconception*

## 7. Three-Dimensional Design

Every NSCAS Science item should reflect scientific practices, content knowledge, and crosscutting concepts.

### Three-Dimensions Alignment Guide

| Dimension | What It Is | Example in NSCAS Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Content Knowledge** (What students know) | Disciplinary science content | Sandhills ecology; Ogallala Aquifer; Platte River ecosystems; crane migration; grassland fire ecology |
| **Scientific Practices** (What students do) | How scientists work | Analyze population data; construct explanations from evidence; use a model to predict ecosystem changes |
| **Crosscutting Concepts** (Lens of thinking) | Cross-domain thinking tools | Cause and effect; patterns; energy and matter; stability and change; systems and system models |

### Sample Three-Dimensional Item Design (Grade 8)

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3D ITEM STRUCTURE                                               |
|                                                                  |
|  PHENOMENON: Water levels in the Ogallala Aquifer under          |
|  western Nebraska have dropped significantly while corn          |
|  and soybean irrigation has increased                            |
|  (Authentic Nebraska phenomenon)                                 |
|                                                                  |
|  CONTENT: Earth Systems — Groundwater; Human impacts             |
|  PRACTICE: Analyzing and Interpreting Data                       |
|  CROSSCUTTING: Cause and Effect                                  |
|                                                                  |
|  STIMULUS: Data table showing irrigation acreage, annual         |
|  precipitation, and aquifer depth over 50 years                  |
|                                                                  |
|  PROMPT: "Using data from the table, construct an explanation    |
|  for the decrease in aquifer water levels. Include specific      |
|  evidence from the data and explain the cause-and-effect         |
|  relationships between irrigation and groundwater depletion."    |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

| CCC | Grade 5 Emphasis | Grade 8 Emphasis | Grade 11 Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patterns | Crane migration timing; seasonal weather | Aquifer depletion trends; population cycles | Evolutionary patterns; climate data trends |
| Cause and Effect | Food chain disruption; erosion | Human impacts on aquifer and soil; chemical reactions | Evolution mechanisms; ecosystem feedback loops |
| Energy and Matter | Food webs; water cycle | Thermal energy transfer; chemical energy | Metabolic pathways; Earth system energy cycles |

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Constructed Response (CR) Rubric — 0–2 Points

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 2 | Response accurately addresses the science concept; demonstrates understanding of practices and crosscutting concepts; includes specific, relevant evidence from the stimulus; explanation is complete, accurate, and logically connected |
| 1 | Response addresses the science concept with partial accuracy; evidence present but may be incomplete; reasoning partially developed |
| 0 | Response does not address the science concept; blank; off-topic; restates stimulus without explanation; no science understanding demonstrated |

### CR Scoring Notes

- **Evidence from the stimulus is required.** Responses relying solely on prior knowledge earn a maximum of 1 point.
- **Scientific reasoning** integrating content with practices or crosscutting concepts is needed for full credit.
- **Scientific misconceptions** reduce the score by at least one point.

## 10. Contextualization — Nebraska Science

When generating NSCAS Science items, prioritize authentic Nebraska natural and agricultural settings:

### Nebraska Science Context Guide

| Setting / Phenomenon | Grade | Science Domain | Standard Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ogallala Aquifer depletion in western Nebraska | 8, 11 | Earth Science | Groundwater systems; human impact |
| Nebraska Sandhills ecology and grass-stabilized dunes | 5, 8, 11 | Life/Earth Science | Ecosystems; geology |
| Platte River sandhill crane migration | 5, 8 | Life Science | Animal behavior; ecosystems |
| Nebraska grassland restoration and prescribed burns | 5, 8 | Life Science | Ecosystems; biodiversity |
| Nebraska soil science and erosion prevention | 5, 8, 11 | Earth Science | Earth systems; human impact |
| Platte River hydrology and water cycle | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Water cycle; systems |
| Nebraska wind energy and turbine technology | 8, 11 | Physical Science | Energy transfer; engineering |
| Chimney Rock geology (volcanic ash deposits) | 5, 8 | Earth Science | Rock formation; geologic history |
| Nebraska corn-ethanol production and chemistry | 11 | Physical Science | Chemical reactions; energy |
| Whooping crane conservation on the Platte River | 5, 8 | Life Science | Biodiversity; ecosystems |
| Nebraska tornado formation and severe weather | 5, 8, 11 | Earth Science | Weather systems; energy |

### Suggested Anchoring Phenomena by Grade

**Grade 5 Phenomena:**
- "Every March, over 500,000 sandhill cranes stop along the Platte River in Nebraska during their migration. Why do they all stop at this specific stretch of the river?"
- "A Nebraska farmer noticed that the field where corn was planted last year has less topsoil than the field where native grasses still grow. What explains this difference?"

**Grade 8 Phenomena:**
- "Water levels in the Ogallala Aquifer under western Nebraska have dropped by more than 25 feet in some areas over the past 50 years while irrigation farming has expanded. What explains this pattern?"
- "Chimney Rock in western Nebraska is made of volcanic ash and clay that was deposited millions of years ago, even though there are no volcanoes in Nebraska today. What does this evidence tell us about Nebraska's geologic history?"

**Grade 11 Phenomena:**
- "Scientists found that restored native prairies in Nebraska support three times more plant species and twice as many insect species as adjacent corn fields. What ecological factors explain this difference in biodiversity?"
- "Nebraska produces over 2 billion gallons of corn-based ethanol annually. What chemical processes convert the starches in corn into ethanol fuel, and what are the energy implications?"

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] Grade verified as 5, 8, or 11 ONLY
- [ ] Nebraska science standard is accurate for grade level
- [ ] Item integrates content knowledge with a scientific practice
- [ ] Anchoring phenomenon is authentic and Nebraska-relevant where possible
- [ ] Stimulus has clear labels, units, and realistic data
- [ ] CR prompt directs students to use evidence from the stimulus
- [ ] All distractors represent real misconceptions
- [ ] MS items specify how many answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] Science content is accurate and reflects current scientific understanding
- [ ] Vocabulary is grade-appropriate

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing science items for Grades 3, 4, 6, or 7 | NSCAS Science is only at Grades 5, 8, 11 | Restrict to tested grades |
| Content-only items with no scientific practice | NSCAS values practice integration | Embed a scientific practice into item design |
| CR prompts without evidence directive | Students may respond without citing data | Include "Use evidence from the [data/graph/diagram]" |
| Generic Midwest phenomena | NSCAS favors Nebraska-specific contexts | Use Sandhills, Platte River, Ogallala, Nebraska weather contexts |
| MS items with only 2 options in 4 total | Too easy to guess | Use 5–6 options with exactly 2–3 correct |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Data-to-Conclusion (MC, DOK 2)
> "The [graph / table / diagram] below shows [Nebraska science scenario]. Based on the data, which [conclusion / explanation / pattern] is BEST supported?"

### Pattern 2: Phenomenon Explanation (CR, DOK 3)
> "Scientists observed [Nebraska-anchored phenomenon]. Using evidence from the [data / model / diagram], construct an explanation for [specific science focus]. Explain the [cause-and-effect / energy flow / pattern] relationship."

### Pattern 3: Model Evaluation (MC or MS, DOK 2–3)
> "A student made a model showing [Nebraska science concept]. Which statement BEST describes what the model shows about [content focus]?"

### Pattern 4: Investigation Design (CR, DOK 3)
> "A scientist in Nebraska wants to investigate whether [variable] affects [outcome] in [Nebraska ecosystem/setting]. Describe a procedure including what would be measured and how results would be interpreted."

### Pattern 5: Evidence-Based Argument (CR, DOK 3)
> "Two students disagree about [Nebraska science claim]. Student A says [misconception]. Student B says [correct claim]. Using evidence from the [data / diagram], explain which student is correct and why."

### Pattern 6: Human Impact and Solutions (MS or CR, DOK 2–3)
> "Human activities have [impacted Nebraska environment]. Select the TWO strategies that BEST [monitor / reduce / address] this impact."
