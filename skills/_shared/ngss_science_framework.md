## Overview

The Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) are organized around **three dimensions** that are integrated simultaneously in each performance expectation. Unlike previous standards that separated science concepts from practices, NGSS requires students to use practices to develop understanding of core ideas.

**Assessment design principle:** Every NGSS-aligned assessment item should integrate **all three dimensions**:
1. A Science and Engineering Practice (SEP) — *what scientists do*
2. A Disciplinary Core Idea (DCI) — *what scientists know*
3. A Crosscutting Concept (CCC) — *how ideas connect across science*

## Dimension 1: Science and Engineering Practices (SEPs)

| SEP # | Practice | Description | Item Trigger Words |
|-------|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **SEP 1** | Asking Questions and Defining Problems | Generating questions based on observations; defining engineering problems | "What question could a scientist ask about...?", "Which question could be investigated?" |
| **SEP 2** | Developing and Using Models | Creating and using representations of natural phenomena | "Which model BEST shows...?", "Use the diagram to explain..." |
| **SEP 3** | Planning and Carrying Out Investigations | Designing experiments; identifying variables; controlling for confounds | "What should the student CHANGE/KEEP THE SAME?", "Which investigation would BEST test...?" |
| **SEP 4** | Analyzing and Interpreting Data | Organizing, displaying, analyzing patterns in data | "Based on the data in the table/graph...", "What pattern does the data show?" |
| **SEP 5** | Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking | Using math to represent relationships; computational modeling | "Calculate...", "Based on the equation..." |
| **SEP 6** | Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions | Building explanations from evidence; designing solutions | "What BEST explains...?", "Construct an argument from the data..." |
| **SEP 7** | Engaging in Argument from Evidence | Using evidence to support or refute claims | "Which evidence BEST supports...?", "A student claims... Is this claim supported?" |
| **SEP 8** | Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information | Reading/writing scientific text; critiquing sources | "Which source provides evidence that...?" |

## Dimension 3: Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs)

| CCC # | Concept | Description | Item Signal |
|-------|---------|-------------|------------|
| **CCC 1** | Patterns | Observed patterns guide organization and classification | "What pattern is shown...?", "How does X change as Y increases?" |
| **CCC 2** | Cause and Effect | Cause and effect relationships | "What CAUSED...?", "What is the EFFECT of...?" |
| **CCC 3** | Scale, Proportion, and Quantity | Scale and proportion in natural phenomena | "What happens if the size/amount is doubled...?" |
| **CCC 4** | Systems and System Models | Defining systems and using models | "In this system, what role does X play...?" |
| **CCC 5** | Energy and Matter | Tracking energy and matter flows | "Where does the energy come from?", "How does matter cycle?" |
| **CCC 6** | Structure and Function | Shape and structure determine function | "How does the structure of X allow it to...?" |
| **CCC 7** | Stability and Change | Conditions for stability and dynamic change | "What conditions would change the system?", "Why does the system remain stable?" |

## Performance Expectations by Grade Band

### Grades 3–5

#### Physical Sciences
- **3-PS2-1:** Plan and conduct investigations on the effects of balanced and unbalanced forces
- **3-PS2-2:** Make observations and measurements to produce data to serve as the basis for evidence
- **4-PS3-1:** Use evidence to construct an explanation relating speed of an object to energy possessed
- **4-PS3-4:** Apply scientific ideas to design, test, and refine a device that converts energy
- **5-PS1-1:** Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen
- **5-PS1-3:** Make observations and measurements to identify materials based on their properties
- **5-PS3-1:** Use models to describe that energy in animals' food was once energy from the sun

#### Life Sciences
- **3-LS1-1:** Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles
- **3-LS2-1:** Construct an argument that some animals form groups that help members survive
- **3-LS4-3:** Construct an argument with evidence that in a particular habitat some organisms survive well
- **4-LS1-1:** Construct an argument that plants and animals have internal and external structures
- **5-LS2-1:** Develop a model to describe the movement of matter among organisms and physical environment

#### Earth and Space Sciences
- **3-ESS2-1:** Represent data in tables and graphical displays to describe typical weather conditions
- **4-ESS2-1:** Make observations and/or measurements to provide evidence of the effects of weathering
- **5-ESS1-1:** Support an argument that differences in apparent brightness of the sun compared to other stars is due to their distance

### Grades 6–8 (Middle School)

#### Physical Sciences
- **MS-PS1-1:** Develop models to describe atomic composition of simple molecules and extended structures
- **MS-PS1-2:** Analyze data on properties of substances before and after the substances interact to determine if a chemical reaction has occurred
- **MS-PS2-1:** Apply Newton's Third Law to design a solution to a problem involving motion
- **MS-PS3-1:** Construct and interpret graphical displays of data to describe the relationships of kinetic energy to mass and speed
- **MS-PS4-1:** Use mathematical representations to describe a simple model for waves

#### Life Sciences
- **MS-LS1-1:** Conduct an investigation to provide evidence that living things are made of cells
- **MS-LS1-6:** Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence for the role of photosynthesis in the cycling of matter and flow of energy
- **MS-LS2-1:** Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence for the effects of resource availability on organisms and populations
- **MS-LS3-2:** Develop and use a model to describe why asexual reproduction results in offspring with identical genetic information
- **MS-LS4-4:** Construct an explanation based on evidence that describes how genetic variations of traits in a population increase some individuals' probability of surviving and reproducing

#### Earth and Space Sciences
- **MS-ESS1-1:** Develop and use a model of the Earth-sun-moon system to describe the cyclic patterns of lunar phases and eclipses
- **MS-ESS2-4:** Develop a model to describe the cycling of water through Earth's systems
- **MS-ESS3-3:** Apply scientific principles to design a method for monitoring and minimizing a human impact on the environment

---

### Grades 9–12 (High School)

#### Physical Sciences
- **HS-PS1-1:** Use the periodic table as a model to predict properties of elements
- **HS-PS2-1:** Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration
- **HS-PS3-1:** Create a computational model to calculate the change in kinetic energy of an object

#### Life Sciences
- **HS-LS1-1:** Construct an explanation based on evidence for how the structure of DNA determines the structure of proteins
- **HS-LS2-6:** Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning that the complex interactions in ecosystems maintain relatively consistent numbers and types of organisms
- **HS-LS4-2:** Construct an explanation based on evidence that the process of evolution primarily results from natural selection

#### Earth and Space Sciences
- **HS-ESS1-1:** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life span of the sun and the role of nuclear fusion
- **HS-ESS2-2:** Analyze geoscience data to make the claim that one change to Earth's surface can create feedbacks that cause changes to other Earth systems
- **HS-ESS3-6:** Use a computational representation to illustrate the relationships among Earth systems and how those relationships are being modified due to human activity

## NGSS Assessment Design Principles

### Three-Dimensional Item Design

Every NGSS-aligned item should:
1. **Anchor in a phenomenon** — a concrete, observable event or situation that needs explanation
2. **Require a practice** — the student must DO something (analyze data, develop a model, construct an argument)
3. **Apply core ideas** — the student must USE disciplinary knowledge
4. **Connect crosscutting concepts** — the item frame should reflect a CCC (cause/effect, patterns, structure/function, etc.)

### Item Stem Patterns

| SEP Targeted | Common Item Stem Pattern |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| SEP 2 (Modeling) | "Which diagram BEST models...", "The student made a model... What does [X] represent?" |
| SEP 4 (Data Analysis) | "Based on the data in the graph/table...", "What trend is shown by the data?" |
| SEP 6 (Explanation) | "What is the BEST explanation for...?", "Construct an explanation using evidence from the passage." |
| SEP 7 (Argument from evidence) | "Which evidence BEST supports the claim that...?", "Select TWO pieces of evidence that support..." |

### Avoiding Common NGSS Item Errors

- **Do NOT** ask recall-only questions (e.g., "What is photosynthesis?" → no practice, no phenomenon)
- **Do** provide a stimulus (data, diagram, scenario) that requires the student to USE science to explain
- **Do** use phenomena from students' real lives and environments
- **Do** ensure the DCI alone is not sufficient — the student must also apply the SEP
