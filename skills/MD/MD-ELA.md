# Maryland — MCAP ELA (Maryland Comprehensive Assessment Program)
## AssessmentForge Skill Guide | Grades 3–8, 10

---

## 1. Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | Maryland Comprehensive Assessment Program (MCAP) — English Language Arts/Literacy |
| **Administering Agency** | Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE) |
| **Vendor** | Cognia / Pearson |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 |
| **Testing Window** | March – May |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based; multi-session test with three task types |
| **Standards** | Maryland College and Career Ready Standards for English Language Arts (MCCRS-ELA) |
| **Score Scale** | Scale score varies by grade; 4 performance levels |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Report; School, District, State aggregates |

> **MCAP and PARCC:** The MCAP ELA assessment is derived from the **Partnership for Assessment of Readiness for College and Careers (PARCC)** design. Item types, task structures, and rubrics follow the PARCC framework. Key features: MCAP uses **Literary Analysis Task (LAT)**, **Research Simulation Task (RST)**, and **Narrative Writing Task (NWT)** formats. The primary extended-response format is the **Prose Constructed Response (PCR)** scored 0–4 points on a holistic rubric.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Distinguished** | Student demonstrates distinguished command of the knowledge and skills; advanced analytical reasoning with sophisticated use of textual evidence |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Student demonstrates strong command of the knowledge and skills; thorough analysis with effective use of textual evidence |
| 2 | **Developing** | Student demonstrates partial command of the knowledge and skills; limited analysis with inconsistent evidence use |
| 1 | **Beginning** | Student demonstrates minimal command of the knowledge and skills; significant gaps in reading and writing |

## 2. Standards Framework

> **Reference:** See `_shared/ccss_ela_framework.md` for full CCR Anchor Standards and grade-by-grade progressions. Maryland uses the **Maryland College and Career Ready Standards for ELA (MCCRS-ELA)**, which are closely aligned to Common Core ELA standards. Standards are coded with the "MCCRS.ELA-Literacy" prefix in the Maryland system.

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 10 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MCCRS.RL.1** — Key details & inference | Explicit details; simple inference from text | Cite textual evidence; analyze what text says explicitly vs. inferentially | Cite strong and thorough evidence; draw complex inferences |
| **MCCRS.RL.2** — Theme / central idea | Determine message or lesson; summarize | Analyze theme development; how it emerges through characters and plot | Analyze themes across complex texts; evaluate development |
| **MCCRS.RL.3** — Character, setting, events | Describe characters' traits and responses | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character and advance plot | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story development |
| **MCCRS.RL.4** — Word meaning | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative and connotative meanings; word choice and tone | Analyze how an author's choices of words create meaning and tone |
| **MCCRS.RL.6** — Point of view | Identify narrator's point of view | Analyze how differences in POV create effects like suspense or humor | Analyze multiple points of view across a text |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 10 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MCCRS.RI.1** — Key details & inference | Quote accurately; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **MCCRS.RI.3** — Connections & relationships | Explain relationships between events, ideas, or concepts | Analyze how a text makes connections between ideas | Analyze a complex set of ideas or sequence of events |
| **MCCRS.RI.6** — Author's purpose & point of view | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's point of view; analyze how purpose shapes content | Determine author's purpose in a text with multiple purposes |
| **MCCRS.RI.8** — Argument & evidence | Explain how reasons support points | Trace and evaluate argument; assess whether reasoning is sound | Evaluate reasoning and evidence in seminal U.S. texts |
| **MCCRS.RI.9** — Compare/integrate information | Compare multiple texts on same topic | Analyze how two texts treat similar topics differently | Analyze foundational U.S. documents of historical significance |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **MCCRS.W.1** — Argument/Opinion | Write arguments with clear claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **MCCRS.W.2** — Informative/Explanatory | Develop topics with facts, details, definitions, examples | 3 |
| **MCCRS.W.3** — Narrative | Establish situation; develop narrator/characters; use narrative techniques | 3–4 |
| **MCCRS.W.7** — Research | Conduct short research projects; gather relevant information | 3 |

## 3. Test Structure

MCAP ELA uses three distinct PARCC-derived task types delivered across multiple sessions.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|            MCAP ELA STRUCTURE (Grades 3–8, 10)                    |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 1 — Literary Analysis Task (LAT)                           |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  1–2 literary passages (paired or extended)                       |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts)                     |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 2 — Research Simulation Task (RST)                         |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  2–3 informational source documents (articles, data, excerpts)   |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts)                     |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNIT 3 — Narrative Writing Task (NWT)                           |
|  ~75–90 minutes                                                   |
|                                                                   |
|  1 literary or informational stimulus passage                     |
|  Machine-scored items (SR, MS, EBSR, TE)                         |
|  1 Prose Constructed Response (PCR, 0–4 pts) — narrative writing |
|                                                                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Machine-Scored Item Distribution (Per Unit)

| Item Type | Points | Machine Scored | DOK |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected Response (SR) | 1 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Multi-Select Response (MS) | 1 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR) | 2 | Yes | 2–3 |
| Technology Enhanced (TE) | 1–2 | Yes | 2 |

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR) Structure

| Component | Description | Points |
|---|---|---|
| LAT PCR | Analytical essay about literary text(s) | 0–4 pts |
| RST PCR | Analytical/argumentative essay synthesizing research sources | 0–4 pts |
| NWT PCR | Narrative writing extending or responding to stimulus | 0–4 pts |

### Grade-Level Test Specifications

| Grade | LAT Items + PCR | RST Items + PCR | NWT Items + PCR | Total Approx. Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~6–8 items + 1 PCR | ~38–46 |
| 6–8 | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~12–14 items + 1 PCR | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~44–52 |
| 10 | ~10–12 items + 1 PCR | ~12–14 items + 1 PCR | ~8–10 items + 1 PCR | ~44–52 |

## 4. Claims & Blueprint

### Reporting Category Weightings

| Category | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Reading Literary Text | ~30% | Students can read closely and analytically to comprehend literary texts of appropriate complexity |
| Reading Informational Text | ~30% | Students can read closely and analytically to comprehend informational texts of appropriate complexity |
| Writing — Written Expression | ~20% | Students can produce effective writing for a range of purposes and audiences; development, organization, evidence use |
| Writing — Knowledge of Language and Conventions | ~20% | Students demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar, usage, and mechanics |

> **Note:** Unlike SBAC's claim structure, MCAP reports reading by text type (literary vs. informational) and writing by expression vs. conventions. Research skills are embedded within the RST rather than reported as a separate claim.

### DOK Distribution

| DOK Level | Description | Approximate % |
|---|---|---|
| DOK 1 | Recall; identify; locate | ~5–10% |
| DOK 2 | Infer; explain; apply; analyze surface features | ~50–55% |
| DOK 3 | Analyze craft; evaluate argument; synthesize across texts | ~35–40% |

## 5. Item Types & Formats

### Selected Response (SR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  SELECTED RESPONSE — 1 point                             |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Passage-dependent; always tied to a stimulus            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**SR Example (Grade 6 Informational, DOK 3, MCCRS.RI.6.6):**

*[After reading an article about the ecological restoration of the Chesapeake Bay watershed]*

What is the author's MAIN purpose in writing this article?

A. To persuade readers to donate money to environmental organizations in Maryland
B. To entertain readers with stories about fishing on the Chesapeake Bay
C. To inform readers about how coordinated conservation efforts have helped restore the health of the Chesapeake Bay ecosystem
D. To warn readers about the dangers of boating in the bay during storm season

*Correct Answer: C*
*Alignment: MCCRS.ELA-Literacy.RI.6.6 | Reading Informational | DOK 2*

---

### Multi-Select Response (MS)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTI-SELECT RESPONSE — 1 point                         |
|                                                          |
|  5–8 options; select exactly 2 or 3 correct answers      |
|  Full credit only; partial credit not awarded            |
|  Machine scored                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MS Example (Grade 5 Literary, DOK 2, MCCRS.RL.5.3):**

*[After reading a short story about two friends exploring the Annapolis harbor during a sailing race]*

Select the TWO details from the story that BEST show how the relationship between the friends changes over the course of the story.

A. "The sailboats stretched across the harbor like a field of white wings."
B. "Jaya turned away from Khalil when he suggested they leave the dock before the storm."
C. "By the time the last boat crossed the finish line, Jaya had taken Khalil's hand and led him toward the old lighthouse they had always avoided."
D. "Their mother had warned them not to go past the Naval Academy seawall."
E. "The wind off the Severn River felt cold against their faces as the clouds rolled in."

*Correct Answers: B, C*
*Alignment: MCCRS.ELA-Literacy.RL.5.3 | Reading Literary | DOK 2*

---

### Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EVIDENCE-BASED SELECTED RESPONSE — 2 points            |
|                                                          |
|  Part A: Inference or analysis (1 pt)                   |
|  Part B: Text evidence supporting Part A (1 pt)          |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**EBSR Example (Grade 8 Informational, DOK 3, MCCRS.RI.8.8):**

*[After reading an argumentative essay about whether Maryland should invest in expanding the Baltimore light rail network to connect underserved communities]*

**Part A:** Which statement BEST describes the author's central argument in this essay?

A. Expanding light rail would only benefit residents of Baltimore City and should not receive statewide funding
B. Maryland should expand the Baltimore light rail because it would reduce highway congestion, lower carbon emissions, and connect underserved neighborhoods to economic opportunities
C. The cost of rail expansion makes it an impractical investment for Maryland at this time
D. Light rail systems have failed in other East Coast cities, and Maryland should wait to see improvements in technology

**Part B:** Which sentence from the essay BEST supports your answer to Part A?

A. "The construction phase alone would create an estimated 8,000 jobs across central Maryland."
B. "Critics have raised concerns about the initial capital investment required for new track infrastructure."
C. "By connecting West Baltimore, Columbia, and BWI Airport with expanded express service, Maryland could cut commute times in half for 150,000 daily riders — while reducing I-95 and I-695 congestion significantly."
D. "Washington, D.C., and several other metropolitan areas offer models of successful light rail, though their geography differs from Maryland's in important ways."

*Correct Answers: Part A: B | Part B: C*
*Alignment: MCCRS.ELA-Literacy.RI.8.8 | Reading Informational | DOK 3*

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+

```

**TE Example (Grade 5 — Selectable Text, DOK 2):**

*Click on the sentence in paragraph 3 that BEST shows the narrator's feelings about visiting the Harriet Tubman Underground Railroad Visitor Center for the first time.*

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  PROSE CONSTRUCTED RESPONSE — 0–4 points                 |
|                                                          |
|  Extended written response to passage(s)                 |
|  LAT: literary analysis essay                            |
|  RST: analytical/argumentative essay from sources        |
|  NWT: narrative writing from stimulus                    |
|  Human scored on a single 0–4 holistic rubric            |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**PCR Prompt Example — RST (Grade 7, Argumentative):**

*[Sources provided: Article 1 on the Chesapeake Bay's role in Maryland's economy and culture; Article 2 on pollution challenges threatening the bay's health; Data table on blue crab harvest numbers and water quality measurements over the past decade]*

You have read three sources about the Chesapeake Bay and its importance to Maryland communities. Write an essay that argues whether Maryland should strengthen regulations on agricultural runoff to protect the bay. Use evidence from at least two of the three sources to support your argument. Be sure to acknowledge a counterclaim.

**PCR Prompt Example — LAT (Grade 6, Literary Analysis):**

*[After reading two short stories about young people growing up in Baltimore — one set during the Civil Rights era, one set in the present day]*

Both stories explore the theme of finding courage in challenging circumstances. Write an essay analyzing how each author develops this theme. Use specific details from both texts to support your analysis.

**PCR Prompt Example — NWT (Grade 4, Narrative):**

*[After reading a passage about Harriet Tubman's journeys on the Underground Railroad through the Eastern Shore of Maryland]*

Write a story about a young person who must make a brave decision to help someone in need. Use details from the passage about Harriet Tubman to help you develop your narrative. Be sure to include a clear setting, characters, and a sequence of events.

## 8. Scoring Rubrics

### Prose Constructed Response (PCR) Rubric — Holistic 0–4 Points

**LAT and RST PCR (Analytical/Argumentative)**

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Demonstrates thorough understanding of the text(s). Skillfully develops a central idea with convincing and well-chosen evidence. Effectively organizes ideas with purposeful, clear coherence. Sophisticated use of language with precise vocabulary. Few or no errors in conventions. |
| 3 | Demonstrates adequate understanding of the text(s). Develops a central idea with sufficient and relevant evidence. Organizes ideas with adequate coherence and logical progression. Appropriate use of language with generally correct vocabulary. Some errors in conventions that do not interfere with meaning. |
| 2 | Demonstrates partial understanding of the text(s). Develops a central idea with limited or loosely connected evidence. Organization is inconsistent; some ideas lack coherence. Simplistic or imprecise language. Errors in conventions may occasionally interfere with meaning. |
| 1 | Demonstrates minimal understanding of the text(s). Central idea is vague or missing. Little or no evidence from the text. Lacks organization or coherence. Limited vocabulary. Frequent errors in conventions that interfere with meaning. |
| 0 | Blank, copied text, entirely off-task, or written in a language other than English. |

**NWT PCR (Narrative)**

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 4 | Effectively establishes and develops a real or imagined experience. Clear narrative arc with purposeful sequence of events. Well-developed characters and setting using dialogue, description, and pacing. Precise and vivid language. Few or no errors in conventions. |
| 3 | Adequately establishes and develops a real or imagined experience. Logical sequence of events with adequate character and setting development. Uses some narrative techniques effectively. Generally appropriate language. Some errors in conventions. |
| 2 | Partially establishes and develops an experience. Inconsistent sequence of events. Limited character or setting development. Basic or repetitive language. Errors in conventions may interfere with meaning. |
| 1 | Minimally establishes an experience. Little to no narrative structure. Undeveloped characters and setting. Limited vocabulary. Frequent errors in conventions. |
| 0 | Blank, copied text, entirely off-task, or written in a language other than English. |

## 11. Quality Checklist

### Pre-Generation Checklist

- [ ] MCCRS standard code verified (MCCRS.ELA-Literacy prefix)
- [ ] Task type identified: LAT, RST, or NWT
- [ ] EBSR Part B is a quote that directly supports Part A (not just same topic)
- [ ] MS items specify exact number of answers to select (TWO/THREE)
- [ ] PCR prompt is open-ended enough for student voice
- [ ] PCR scored on holistic 0–4 rubric (not 3-trait rubric)
- [ ] Passage Lexile matches grade-band range
- [ ] RST sources include at least one non-print source (graph, data table, image)
- [ ] Maryland context incorporated where appropriate (Chesapeake Bay, Baltimore, history, ecology)
- [ ] Distractors represent authentic student misconceptions (not trivially wrong)
- [ ] DOK level matches the actual cognitive demand of the question

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| EBSR Part B that supports a different answer | Part B must directly support Part A's correct answer | Cross-check: does Part B quote only make sense given Part A answer? |
| Using SBAC Full Write (0–12) instead of PCR (0–4) | MCAP uses PARCC-derived holistic 0–4 PCR rubric | Score all PCR items 0–4 on holistic rubric |
| Mixing up task types | LAT = literary analysis; RST = research/argument; NWT = narrative | Match prompt type to correct task structure |
| PCR prompts with only one correct interpretation | Essay needs room for student perspective | Keep prompts debatable or open-ended |
| RST sources all the same format (three articles) | MCAP RST includes varied source types | Include data tables, graphs, or images as at least one RST source |
| Missing Grade 10 | MCAP ELA tests Grade 10, not Grade 11 | Use Grade 10 for high school assessment |
| Using CCSS codes without MCCRS prefix | Maryland uses MCCRS prefix | Use MCCRS.ELA-Literacy for all standard codes |

## 12. Prompt Patterns

### Pattern 1: Central Idea / Theme (SR or EBSR, DOK 2–3)
> "What [central idea / theme] does the [passage / author] develop? Which detail from the text BEST supports this [idea / theme]?"

### Pattern 2: Author's Craft (SR, DOK 3)
> "How does the author's use of [figurative language / structure / word choice / point of view] in [this section / paragraph #] affect the [meaning / tone / reader's understanding of ____]?"

### Pattern 3: Evidence Selection (EBSR Part B)
> "Which [sentence / detail] from the [passage] BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 4: Multi-Source Argument — RST (PCR, DOK 4)
> "Using evidence from at least two of the three sources, write an [argumentative / analytical] essay about [MD-relevant topic]. Be sure to [acknowledge a counterclaim / support your analysis with specific evidence]."

### Pattern 5: Literary Analysis — LAT (PCR, DOK 4)
> "Both texts explore [shared theme or idea]. Write an essay analyzing how each author develops [theme / central idea]. Use specific details from both texts."

### Pattern 6: Narrative Writing — NWT (PCR, DOK 3–4)
> "Using what you have learned from reading [stimulus passage], write a [story / narrative] about [scenario related to stimulus]. Develop your narrative with a clear setting, characters, and sequence of events."

### Pattern 7: Multi-Select Evidence (MS, DOK 2)
> "Select the TWO [details / sentences / examples] from the text that BEST [support / show / explain] [specific focus]."

### Pattern 8: Compare Texts (SR or EBSR, DOK 3)
> "Both sources discuss [common topic]. How does [Source 1] treat this topic differently from [Source 2]?"
