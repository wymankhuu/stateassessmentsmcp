## 1. OVERVIEW

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assessment Name** | MAAP — Mississippi Academic Assessment Program, English Language Arts |
| **Administering Agency** | Mississippi Department of Education (MDE) |
| **Vendor** | Questar (ETS successor) |
| **Grades Tested** | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 |
| **Testing Window** | April–May (annual summative) |
| **Delivery** | Computer-based testing (CBT); paper accommodations available |
| **Standards** | Mississippi College- and Career-Readiness Standards (MS CCRS) for ELA — CCSS-aligned |
| **Performance Levels** | 4 levels: Level 1 (Minimal), Level 2 (Basic), Level 3 (Pass/Proficient), Level 4 (Advanced) |
| **Reporting** | Individual Student Reports; School, District, State aggregates |

> **CRITICAL NOTE:** MAAP ELA aligns to the **Mississippi College- and Career-Readiness Standards (MS CCRS)** for English Language Arts, which are aligned to Common Core State Standards (CCSS). Standard codes follow the CCSS format (e.g., RL.5.2, RI.8.6). Grade 10 uses the 9–10 band standards (e.g., RL.9-10.2). MAAP replaced the previous PARCC-based assessment beginning in 2015–2016.

### Performance Levels

| Level | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Minimal** | Student demonstrates minimal understanding of grade-level ELA standards; significant gaps in reading and writing |
| 2 | **Basic** | Student demonstrates partial understanding of grade-level ELA standards; inconsistent application of skills |
| 3 | **Pass (Proficient)** | Student demonstrates sufficient understanding of grade-level ELA standards; meets college/career readiness expectations |
| 4 | **Advanced** | Student demonstrates thorough, in-depth understanding of grade-level ELA standards; exceeds expectations |

> **Proficiency Target:** Level 3 (Pass) is the state proficiency target. Students scoring at Level 3 or Level 4 are considered on track for college and career readiness.

## 2. STANDARDS FRAMEWORK

> **Reference:** See `_shared/ccss_ela_framework.md` for full CCR Anchor Standards and grade-by-grade progressions. Mississippi College- and Career-Readiness Standards for ELA align directly to CCSS ELA.

### Reading Literature (RL) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 10 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RL.1** | Ask and answer questions; quote from text | Cite textual evidence to support analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence; draw inferences |
| **RL.2** | Determine theme or central message; summarize | Determine theme; analyze development over course of text | Analyze theme development; provide objective summary |
| **RL.3** | Describe characters, settings, events in detail | Analyze how dialogue and incidents reveal character | Analyze impact of author's choices regarding story elements |
| **RL.4** | Determine meaning of words; recognize figurative language | Analyze figurative, connotative meanings; word choice and tone | Analyze cumulative impact of specific word choices on meaning |
| **RL.5** | Explain how chapters, scenes, stanzas fit together | Analyze how structure contributes to meaning and style | Analyze how author's choices concerning structure create effects |
| **RL.6** | Identify narrator's point of view | Analyze how differences in POV create effects | Analyze a case in which understanding POV requires distinguishing stated from implied |

### Reading Informational Text (RI) — Key Standards by Grade Band

| Standard | Gr 3–5 Focus | Gr 6–8 Focus | Gr 10 Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RI.1** | Quote accurately; draw inferences | Cite textual evidence supporting analysis | Cite strong and thorough evidence to support analysis |
| **RI.2** | Determine main idea; summarize | Determine central idea; analyze development | Determine two or more central ideas; analyze development |
| **RI.3** | Explain relationships between events, ideas, concepts | Analyze how key individuals, events, or ideas are introduced | Analyze a complex set of ideas and explain how they interact |
| **RI.6** | Identify author's purpose | Determine author's POV; analyze how purpose shapes content | Determine author's purpose; analyze how style and content contribute |
| **RI.8** | Explain how reasons support points | Trace and evaluate argument; assess reasoning | Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal texts |

### Writing Standards

| Standard | Focus | DOK |
|---|---|---|
| **W.1** | Write arguments with clear claims, evidence, and reasoning | 3–4 |
| **W.2** | Write informative/explanatory texts with relevant evidence | 3 |
| **W.3** | Write narratives with well-chosen details and event sequences | 3–4 |
| **W.4** | Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience | 3 |

## 3. TEST STRUCTURE

MAAP ELA is administered in **2 sessions** across one or two days.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|             MAAP ELA STRUCTURE (Grades 3–8, 10)                   |
+---------------------------------+--------------------------------+
|   SESSION 1                     |   SESSION 2                    |
|   ~60–90 minutes                |   ~60–90 minutes               |
|                                 |                                |
|  READING PASSAGES               |  READING PASSAGES              |
|  - Literary + Informational     |  - Literary + Informational    |
|  - Multiple Choice (MC)         |  - Multiple Choice (MC)        |
|  - EBSR (Evidence-Based         |  - EBSR (Evidence-Based        |
|    Selected Response)           |    Selected Response)          |
|                                 |                                |
|  WRITING PROMPT                 |  (MC and EBSR only)            |
|  - Text-based essay (0–4 pt)   |                                |
+---------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

### Session Item Counts by Grade Band

| Session | Grades 3–5 | Grades 6–8 | Grade 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Session 1 | 14–18 MC/EBSR + 1 Writing Prompt | 16–20 MC/EBSR + 1 Writing Prompt | 18–22 MC/EBSR + 1 Writing Prompt |
| Session 2 | 14–18 MC/EBSR | 16–20 MC/EBSR | 18–22 MC/EBSR |

> **Writing Prompt:** MAAP ELA includes a text-based writing prompt in Session 1 that requires students to read one or more passages and compose an analytical, argumentative, or informative essay. The writing prompt is scored on a 0–4 rubric assessing reading comprehension and written expression.

## 5. ITEM TYPES

### Multiple Choice (MC)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  MULTIPLE CHOICE — 1 point                               |
|                                                          |
|  4 options (A, B, C, D)                                  |
|  Single correct answer                                   |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Both sessions                                           |
|  DOK 1–2                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**MC Example (Grade 5, DOK 2, RL.5.2 — Theme):**

*Passage excerpt: A boy living along the Mississippi River learns from his grandmother that the river has shaped their family's history for generations, from steamboat pilots to catfish farmers.*

Which theme is BEST supported by the passage?

- A. Rivers are dangerous and should be avoided
- B. Family connections to the land shape who we are
- C. Farming is the most important occupation in Mississippi
- D. Grandparents always know more than their grandchildren

*Correct Answer: B*
*Alignment: RL.5.2 | DOK 2*

---

### Evidence-Based Selected Response (EBSR)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  EVIDENCE-BASED SELECTED RESPONSE — 2 points            |
|                                                          |
|  Part A: Inference or analysis (1 pt)                   |
|  Part B: Text evidence supporting Part A (1 pt)          |
|  Machine scored                                          |
|  Both sessions                                           |
|  DOK 2–3                                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**EBSR Example (Grade 7, DOK 3, RI.7.6 — Author's Purpose):**

*[After reading an article about the history of the Natchez Trace Parkway and its significance to Mississippi]*

**Part A:** What is the author's MAIN purpose in the section describing the original Natchez Trace trail?

- A. To entertain the reader with adventure stories about early travelers
- B. To show how the historic trail connected different cultures and shaped Mississippi's development
- C. To argue that the Natchez Trace should be closed to modern traffic
- D. To explain why roads in Mississippi are poorly maintained today

**Part B:** Which sentence from the article BEST supports your answer to Part A?

- A. "Today, the Natchez Trace Parkway draws thousands of visitors each year who enjoy its scenic views."
- B. "The trail served as a vital link between the Natchez people, European settlers, and traders who together built the cultural fabric of the region."
- C. "The original trail stretched approximately 440 miles from Natchez to Nashville."
- D. "Many sections of the old trail have been paved over by modern roads."

*Correct Answers: Part A: B | Part B: B*
*Alignment: RI.7.6 | DOK 3*

### Writing Prompt

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  WRITING PROMPT — 0–4 points                             |
|                                                          |
|  Text-based analytical, argumentative, or informative    |
|  essay                                                   |
|  Multi-paragraph response                                |
|  Must reference passage(s) from Session 1                |
|  Human scored using holistic rubric                      |
|  DOK 3–4                                                 |
|  Session 1 only; 1 per test                              |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Writing Prompt Example (Grade 8, DOK 3, W.8.1 + RI.8.8):**

*"After reading the two articles about the impact of the Mississippi River on Delta communities, write an essay that argues whether flood control measures have done more to help or harm Mississippi Delta communities. Use evidence from both articles to support your argument."*

**Writing Prompt Example (Grade 10, DOK 3, W.9-10.2 + RI.9-10.3):**

*"Based on the passages about William Faulkner's portrayal of the American South, write an essay explaining how Faulkner uses the setting of Yoknapatawpha County to explore themes of change and tradition. Use evidence from the texts to support your explanation."*

## 8. SCORING RUBRICS

### Writing Prompt Rubric — 0–4 Points

The MAAP Writing Prompt rubric evaluates **Reading Comprehension** and **Written Expression** holistically.

| Score | Reading Comprehension | Written Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Demonstrates thorough understanding of the text(s); analysis is insightful and well-developed; all claims are fully supported with specific evidence | Writing is clear, coherent, and well-organized; effective transitions; precise vocabulary; strong command of conventions with few errors |
| 3 | Demonstrates adequate understanding; analysis is clear; claims supported with relevant evidence | Writing is generally clear and organized; adequate transitions; appropriate vocabulary; adequate conventions with some errors |
| 2 | Demonstrates partial understanding; analysis is limited; evidence present but may be vague | Writing has some organization; limited transitions; general vocabulary; errors may interfere with meaning |
| 1 | Demonstrates minimal understanding; little analysis; evidence absent or inaccurate | Writing lacks organization; minimal transitions; imprecise vocabulary; frequent errors interfere with meaning |
| 0 | Does not address the prompt; blank; off-topic; copied from passage; incomprehensible | No evidence of writing skill |

### EBSR Scoring

| Part | Points | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Part A | 1 | Correct analytical response selected |
| Part B | 1 | Correct text evidence selected that supports Part A |
| **Total** | **2** | Both parts scored independently |

| Mississippi Gulf Coast | 5–10 | Environmental informational; cause-and-effect analysis |
| Mississippi Delta farmland | 3–8 | Agricultural history; economic informational |

| Piney Woods region | 4–8 | Nature writing; ecological informational |

## 11. QUALITY CHECKLIST AND COMMON PITFALLS

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Standard code uses CCSS format: RL/RI/W.[Grade].[Standard]
- [ ] Grade 10 uses 9–10 band notation (e.g., RL.9-10.2)
- [ ] EBSR Part B directly supports Part A correct answer
- [ ] Writing Prompt includes clear analytical focus (not "summarize")
- [ ] Writing Prompt ends with evidence directive
- [ ] Passage Lexile within grade-band range (see Section 7)
- [ ] MC options are plausible; no "trick" or obviously wrong choices
- [ ] Mississippi-specific context used where appropriate
- [ ] Performance levels use Mississippi terminology (Minimal through Advanced)
- [ ] Writing prompt scored on 0–4 rubric for Reading Comprehension and Written Expression
- [ ] DOK level verified against item cognitive demand
- [ ] EBSR Part B options are actual text excerpts from the passage

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing prompt not text-based | MAAP writing is passage-dependent | Always tie writing prompt to reading passages |
| EBSR Part B supports different Part A answer | Part B must directly support Part A's correct answer | Cross-check: does Part B quote only make sense given Part A? |
| Generic national contexts | Mississippi test values MS-specific authenticity | Anchor passages in real MS places and communities |
| Ignoring civil rights sensitivity | Mississippi civil rights history must be portrayed with depth and respect | Present civil rights content with historical accuracy and dignity |
| Confusing Level 3 (Pass) with Level 2 (Basic) | Level 3 is the proficiency target | Ensure item difficulty targets Level 3 performance |
| Using non-CCSS standard codes | MAAP uses MS CCRS aligned to CCSS | Reference CCSS-format codes exclusively |
| Missing Grade 10 in test design | MAAP ELA tests Grade 10 (not just 3–8) | Include Grade 10 items using 9–10 band standards |

## 12. PROMPT PATTERNS

The following patterns reflect the most commonly tested item structures on MAAP ELA.

### Pattern 1: Theme Development (EBSR, DOK 2–3, RL.X.2)
> "Part A: What theme does the author develop in this passage? Part B: Which sentence from the passage BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 2: Argument from Sources (Writing Prompt, DOK 3–4, W.X.1 + RI.X.8)
> "After reading the articles about [Mississippi topic], write an essay that argues [position]. Use evidence from both passages to support your argument."

### Pattern 3: Author's Purpose and Evidence (EBSR, DOK 2–3, RI.X.6)
> "Part A: What is the author's MAIN purpose in [section/paragraph]? Part B: Which sentence from the text BEST supports your answer to Part A?"

### Pattern 4: Figurative Language and Meaning (MC, DOK 2, RL.X.4)
> "Read this sentence from the passage: '[sentence with figurative language].' What does this [metaphor/simile/idiom] MOST LIKELY mean in the context of the passage?"

### Pattern 5: Central Idea with Evidence (EBSR, DOK 2–3, RI.X.2)
> "Part A: What is the central idea of [passage]? Part B: Which detail from the text BEST supports the central idea identified in Part A?"

### Pattern 6: Informative/Explanatory Essay (Writing Prompt, DOK 3, W.X.2 + RI.X.3)
> "Based on the passages about [Mississippi topic], write an essay explaining [how/why analytical focus]. Use evidence from the text to support your explanation."
